1. MLP DEFINITION, INITIALISATION, BIASES, ESTIMATION TARGET
=====================================================================

Definition 2.1 (verbatim): "The multilayer perceptron (MLP) with L ≥ 0 hidden layers, width n ≥ 1, activation functions ϕ_1, . . . , ϕ_L : R → R and weights θ = (W^(1), . . . , W^(L+1)) ∈ R^((L+1)×n×n) is the function M_θ : R^n → R^n defined by
  M_θ(x) = W^(L+1) ϕ_L( W^(L) . . . ϕ_2( W^(2) ϕ_1( W^(1) x ) ) . . . ),
where the activation functions are applied coordinatewise."

"We consider MLPs with equal input dimension, output dimension, and hidden dimensions, and without a final activation function. We do not incorporate bias terms, but we show how to adapt our algorithms to work for these in Appendix F." (Appendix F.1: "Our cumulant propagation algorithms simply add the bias vector to the estimate of the mean at the corresponding layer. Equivalently, this can be thought of as using a different activation function for each neuron that has been shifted by the corresponding bias component.")

Layer notation (S.1.1): "Let X = X^(0) ∼ N(0, I_n) denote the input to the MLP. Then recursively define ∀1 ≤ ℓ ≤ L+1, Z^(ℓ) := W^(ℓ) X^(ℓ−1); ∀1 ≤ ℓ ≤ L, X^(ℓ) = ϕ_ℓ(Z^(ℓ)). Then Z^(ℓ) and X^(ℓ) are the preactivation and activation at layer ℓ, respectively."

Activation: "our default choice of activation function is the rectified linear unit, ReLU(z) := max(z, 0)".

Central estimation problem (Sec 2.2, verbatim): "Given weights θ ∈ R^((L+1)×n×n) drawn independently from N(0, 2/n) (following He et al. [2015]), estimate E_{X∼N(0,I_n)}[M_θ(X)], where M_θ is the MLP with weights θ. More precisely, an estimation procedure should take as input θ and ε > 0, and produce an estimate with mean squared error O(ε²) over the choice of θ for each output neuron."

The algorithm's returned quantity is "W^(L+1) η̃_1[X^(L)]", i.e. the final linear layer applied to the estimated first-order cumulant (mean vector) of the last post-activation layer. The per-layer post-ReLU mean estimates η̃_1[X^(ℓ)] (= a_i in Algorithms 1/2 below) are exactly what the algorithm tracks at every layer.

Initialisation detail (Appendix F.2): Definition F.1 (He-initialised, no biases): "W^(1)_ij i.i.d. ∼ N(0, 1/n); W^(ℓ+1)_ij i.i.d. ∼ N(0, 1/(n E_{Z∼N(0,1)}[ϕ_ℓ(Z)²])), ℓ = 1, . . . , L". Note the paper's main ReLU setting (Sec 2.2, Thm 5.1) draws ALL weights from N(0, 2/n). The paper remarks: "He initialization and critical initialization at q* = 1 coincide for ReLU, both giving weight variance 2/n and zero bias. Thus our main ReLU experiments can be thought of as being performed in either initialization." Footnote 5: "We assume the use of naive rather than Strassen-type matrix multiplication in all algorithms."

Quenched vs annealed: "our algorithms take as input the weights of a particular network and estimate the expected output over random inputs, i.e., a quenched average ... After comparing our estimates with ground truth, we average over the choice of weights".

2. HERMITE EXPANSION CONVENTIONS AND RELU HERMITE COEFFICIENTS
=====================================================================

Sec 3.2 (verbatim): "Given Y ∼ N(μ, σ²) and ϕ : R → R with E[ϕ(Y)²] < ∞, the Hermite expansion of ϕ with respect to Y is the series expansion
  ϕ(z) = Σ_{k=0}^∞ (1/k!) ϕ̂_k^{(μ,σ²)} σ^k He_k((z − μ)/σ)
where He_k is the kth probabilist's Hermite polynomial, the monic polynomial of degree k that is orthogonal to all polynomials of lower degree under the standard Gaussian measure. The Hermite coefficients in this expansion are given by
  ϕ̂_k^{(μ,σ²)} := (1/σ^k) E[ ϕ(Y) He_k((Y − μ)/σ) ].   (1)
Truncating the expansion gives the best low-degree approximation to ϕ in mean squared error under Y." Footnote 6: "Other authors use the term Hermite coefficient to refer to the same expression divided by k!."

Defining property used in proofs (S.7.2): "He_s(x) = (−1)^s e^{x²/2} (d^s/dx^s) e^{−x²/2}."

Integration-by-parts identity (Appendix A.1): "ϕ̂_k^{(μ,σ²)} := (1/σ^k) E[ϕ(Y) He_k((Y−μ)/σ)] = E[ϕ^(k)(Y)] = (∂^k/∂μ^k) E[ϕ(Y)] for Y ∼ N(μ, σ²)."

Polynomials (Lemma S.7.2): for ϕ(t) = Σ_k (1/k!) a_k t^k, "ϕ̂_s^{(μ,σ²)} = Σ_{k=0}^∞ (1/k!) a_{s+k} E[Y^k]", with E[Y^k] = Σ_{π⊢[k], all |S|≤2} Π_{S∈π} κ_{|S|}[Y]  (25).

Orthogonality identity used for diagonal entries (S.1.4): "(ϕ²)^_0^Z = E[ϕ²] = Σ_{k=0}^∞ (1/k!) (ϕ̂_k^Z)² σ^{2k}  and so  κ_2[ϕ(Z)]_{i,i} = (ϕ²)^_0^Z − (ϕ̂_0^Z)²."

Hermite coefficients of powers of ReLU — Proposition S.3.8 (verbatim modulo layout): "Let ϕ = ReLU. Fix μ ∈ R and σ² ∈ R_{>0} and let Z ∼ N(μ, σ²). Write α = μ/σ, let He_i denote the ith standard probabilist's Hermite polynomial, and let φ and Φ denote the standard Gaussian probability density function and cumulative distribution function, respectively. Then, for any k ∈ Z_{≥0} and p ∈ Z_{>0},
  (ϕ^p)^_k^{(μ,σ²)} =
     p! Φ(α)                                            if k = p
     p! (−1)^{k−p−1} σ^{−(k−p)} He_{k−p−1}(α) φ(α)        if k > p
     σ^{p−k} ( P_{1,p,k}(α) φ(α) + P_{2,p,k}(α) Φ(α) )    if k < p
where
  P_{1,p,k}(α) := (p − k + 1)_k Σ_{j=0}^{p−k} C(p−k, j) α^{p−k−j} [ Σ_{m=0}^{⌊(j−1)/2⌋} C(j, 2m) (2m − 1)!! He_{j−2m−1}(−α) ]
  P_{2,p,k}(α) := (p − k + 1)_k Σ_{j=0}^{p−k} 1[j even] C(p−k, j) α^{p−k−j} (j − 1)!!
and (a)_t := a(a + 1)...(a + t − 1) is the Pochhammer symbol. Proof. This is a scaling and shifting of the (μ, σ²) = (0, 1) case, which is calculated in Kagawa [2015]."
(C(a,b) is my rendering of the binomial coefficient.) The Gaussian expectations needed in Algorithms 1–2 (E[ϕ(Y)], E[ϕ(Y)²], E[ϕ(Y)(Y−μ)/σ]/σ) are the p=1,2 / k=0,1 cases. Appendix B: "Each expectation can be decomposed into a finite sum of integrals of polynomials against the standard Gaussian density over finite intervals. By repeated integration by parts, these integrals can be expressed in terms of the standard Gaussian density function and cumulative distribution function evaluated at the interval endpoints."

Non-ReLU activations (F.3): Gauss–Hermite quadrature "E_{Z∼N(0,1)}[ψ(Z)] ≈ Σ_{i=1}^m ((m − 1)! / (m He_{m−1}(x_i)²)) ψ(x_i)", x_i the roots of He_m, "we take the number of roots to be m = 100."

Caveat (S.6.2): "the Hermite coefficients of ReLU have a singularity at σ² = 0, so much more care is required".

3. DIAGRAM SUMMATION FORMULA FOR CUMULANTS
=====================================================================

Cumulants (Sec 3.1): defined recursively by "E[X_{i_1} · · · X_{i_k}] = Σ_{partitions π of {1,...,k}} Π_{S∈π} κ[X_{i_s} : s ∈ S]". Multilinearity under linear maps: "κ_r[Z^(ℓ)]_{i_1,...,i_r} = Σ_{j_1,...,j_r=1}^n κ_r[X^(ℓ−1)]_{j_1,...,j_r} Π_{s=1}^r W^(ℓ)_{i_s,j_s}" (symmetric contraction, Definition S.2.3: "(T ⊙ W)_{i_1,...,i_r} := Σ_{j_1,...,j_r} T_{j_1,...,j_r} Π_s W_{i_s,j_s}. This operation can be computed in O(n^{r+1}) operations by multiplying in the matrix one index at a time.")

Definition A.1 (verbatim): "Let r ∈ Z_{>0} and k ∈ Z^r_{≥0}. A diagram on k is a set partition π ⊢ {(v, w) : v ∈ {1, . . . , r}, w ∈ {1, . . . , k_v}}. We say that π is connected to mean that π ∪ {{(v, w) : w ∈ {1, . . . , k_v}} : v ∈ {1, . . . , r}} forms the edge set of a connected hypergraph. (In the degenerate case in which k_v = 0 for some v, we consider π to be connected if and only if r = 1.) The set of all connected diagrams on k is denoted cDia(k). We say that π is [≤ M]-mixed to mean that every part S ∈ π of size at most M satisfies {(v_1, w_1), (v_2, w_2)} ⊆ S for some v_1 ≠ v_2. The set of all connected [≤ M]-mixed diagrams on k is denoted cDia^{[≤M]}(k)." Visualisation: "Hermite coefficients correspond to encircled vertex sets and cumulants correspond to hyperedges." "Our diagrams are essentially cluster-expansion diagrams from statistical physics [Malyshev, 1980], except that we generalize pairwise edges to hyperedges".

Theorem A.2 (Hermite-based diagram summation formula for cumulants, verbatim): "Let Z ∈ R^n be a random variable with finite moments and let ϕ : R → R be a polynomial. Then
  κ[ϕ(Z_{j_1}), . . . , ϕ(Z_{j_r})] = Σ_{k∈Z^r_{≥0}} ( Π_{v=1}^r (1/k_v!) ϕ̂_{k_v}^{(E[Z_{j_v}], Var[Z_{j_v}])} ) Σ_{π∈cDia^{[≤2]}(k)} Π_{S∈π} κ[Z_{j_v} : (v, w) ∈ S]."
"Note that in this formula, we do not use a single Hermite expansion of ϕ, but rather take separate Hermite expansions of ϕ with respect to Gaussian distributions with means and variances matching each of Z_1, . . . , Z_n." "The special case of this formula in which ϕ is the step function 1_{z≥0} and Z is multivariate Gaussian appears in Kendall [1941]". Footnote 11: "also applies to non-polynomials under certain analytic conditions."

Theorem S.2.1 (Polynomial diagram summation formula): same but with "ϕ^{(k_v)}(0)" in place of ϕ̂_{k_v} and the sum over all of cDia(k) (not [≤2]-mixed). Equation (3): for ϕ(t) = Σ_{k=0}^D (1/k!) a_k t^k, "κ_r[ϕ(Z)]_{i_1,...,i_r} = Σ_{k∈{0,...,D}^r} (1/k!) (Π_v a_{k_v}) Σ_{π⊢[k_1]⊔...⊔[k_r] connected} Π_{S∈π} κ[Z_{i_v} : (v,l) ∈ S]". Lemma S.7.1 (cumulants of powers): "κ[Z^{k_1}_{j_1}, ..., Z^{k_r}_{j_r}] = Σ_{π∈cDia(k)} Π_{S∈π} κ[Z_{j_v} : (v, l) ∈ S]".

Derivation of the Hermite form (eq. (4)→(5)): split each diagram into a "base diagram" that is [≤2]-mixed plus, per vertex-group v, parts of size ≤2 inside [k_v]; "the sum over τ is equal to E[(Y_{i_v})^d], where Y_{i_v} is the 1d Gaussian with the same mean and variance as Z_{i_v}. This means that the sum over d evaluates to (1/k_v!) E[ϕ^{(k_v)}(Y_{i_v})]. This expectation is exactly the k_v-th Hermite coefficient". "if i_1, ..., i_r are distinct, we have collected together all of the Θ(1)-growth terms, and this is the expansion that we truncate in our Hermite-based algorithms."

K = 2 special case — Corollary A.3 (Wright et al. [2024], verbatim): "Let Y ∈ R^n be a multivariate Gaussian random variable and let ϕ : R → R be a polynomial. Then
  Cov[ϕ(Y_{j_1}), ϕ(Y_{j_2})] = Σ_{k=1}^∞ (1/k!) ϕ̂_k^{(E[Y_{j_1}],Var[Y_{j_1}])} ϕ̂_k^{(E[Y_{j_2}],Var[Y_{j_2}])} Cov[Y_{j_1}, Y_{j_2}]^k."
"This formula exactly matches the covariance propagation formula of Wright et al. [2024, Theorem 1] using the integration-by-parts identity" above. Proof sketch: only pairings survive for Gaussian Y, k_1 = k_2, the k_1! permutations cancel one 1/k_1!, connectedness kills k=0. In Algorithm 2 the off-diagonal covariance uses only "the leading-order term of the formula given in Corollary A.3" (k=1): Σ_ij ← Σ_ij c_i c_j.

Repeated-index (diagonal) case, r=2, i_1=i_2=i, Z = N(0, I_n): "κ_2[ϕ(Z)]_{i,i} = Σ_{k≥1} (1/k!) (ϕ̂_k^Z)² Var[Z_i]^k. So once again, when some indices are equal, any truncation of (5) will fail." Resolution = power cumulants (Sec 4 below): "κ_2[ϕ(Z)]_{i,i} = κ_1[ϕ²(Z)]_i − κ_1[ϕ(Z)]_i²;  κ_3[ϕ(Z)]_{i,i,j} = κ_2[ϕ²(Z), ϕ(Z)]_{i,j} − 2 κ_2[ϕ(Z)]_{i,j} κ_1[ϕ(Z)]_i."

K = 3 example (footnote 17, Z mean-zero multivariate Gaussian, i ≠ j):
"κ_3[ϕ(Z)]_{i,i,j} = Σ_{k≥1} (1/k!) (ϕ̂_k^Z)_j Cov[Z]^k_{i,j} [ Σ_{a≥0} (1/a!) Var[Z]^a_i Σ_{0≤b≤k, a+b(k−b)≠0} C(k,b) (ϕ̂_{a+b}^Z)_i (ϕ̂_{k+a−b}^Z)_i ]
 ... the inner parentheses evaluate to ((ϕ²)^_k^Z)_i − 2 (ϕ̂_0^Z)_i (ϕ̂_k^Z)_i and so
 κ_3[ϕ(Z)]_{i,i,j} = Σ_{k≥1} (1/k!) ((ϕ²)^_k^Z)_i (ϕ̂_k^Z)_j Cov[Z]^k_{i,j} − 2(ϕ̂_0^Z)_i Σ_{k≥1} (ϕ̂_k^Z)_i (ϕ̂_k^Z)_j Cov[Z]^k_{i,j}  [sic: the second sum is missing a 1/k! in the extraction]
 = κ_2[ϕ(Z)², ϕ(Z)]_{i,j} − 2 κ_1[ϕ(Z)] κ_2[ϕ(Z)]_{i,j}".

Combinatorial restatement — Theorem S.3.5 / eq. (10) (this is the form the algorithm implements): "κ_r[ϕ(Z)]_{j_1,...,j_r} = Σ_{k∈Z^r_{≥0}} ( Π_{v=1}^r (1/k_v!) ϕ̂_{k_v}^{(E[Z_{j_v}],Var(Z_{j_v}))} ) Σ_{ν∈cVec^{[≤2]}(k)} c^vec_ν Π_{u∈ν} (D̃_u κ_{|u|}[Z])_{j_1,...,j_r}." Vector partitions (Def S.3.3): ν = (u_1,...,u_b), u_i ∈ Z^r_{≥0}, Σ_i u_{i,j} = k_j, lexicographically descending; "Each vector partition is an equivalence class of diagrams modulo the forgetting of the distinction between vertices in the same group". "(D̃_(1,1,1) κ̃^P[X^(ℓ)])_{i_1,i_2,i_3} = 1[i_j all distinct] ( Σ_{σ∈A_3} Σ_{k_1,...,k_4∈[2]} (1/k!) (f_{k_1})_{i_σ(1)} (f_{k_2+k_3})_{i_σ(2)} (f_{k_4})_{i_σ(3)} (Q_(k_1,k_2))_{i_σ(1),i_σ(2)} (Q_(k_3,k_4))_{i_σ(2),i_σ(3)}

4. THE ALGORITHMS
=====================================================================

4a. Basic algorithm, high-level (Sec 4.1, verbatim): "Our algorithm first picks some maximum order K of cumulants to track. ... Given K, we start with the input distribution N(0, I_n), which has second-order cumulant matrix I_n and all other cumulants equal to 0. We then proceed through each operation in the network, updating the estimated cumulants at each step.
• At each matrix multiplication step, we update the estimated cumulants using the multilinearity property of cumulants. This involves taking an einsum of the kth-order estimated cumulant tensor with k copies of the weight matrix, for k = 1, . . . , K.
• At each activation function step, we update the estimated cumulants using a truncation of the diagram summation formula for cumulants. ...
Once we reach the output of the network, we return the final estimated first-order cumulant vector.
As stated so far, the error of this algorithm is too large, because it fails to take into account the large correlations that occur when the same neuron appears more than once in the same cumulant. To remedy this, we make two further adjustments.
1. Power cumulants. Instead of taking Hermite expansions of the activation function only, we take Hermite expansions of powers of the activation function, and use these to estimate the power cumulants of the activation output. The kth-order power cumulants of X are the cumulants of the form κ[X^{α_1}_{i_1}, . . . , X^{α_m}_{i_m}] with α_1, . . . , α_m ≥ 1, α_1 + · · · + α_m = k and i_1, . . . , i_m distinct, from which we can obtain the kth-order cumulants of X by applying the partition formula and its inverse version. For example, when K = 2, we compute Gaussian variances exactly, while continuing to use an approximation for off-diagonal Gaussian covariances.
2. Extra full trace for odd K. When the maximum cumulant order K is odd, we also track a single number corresponding to the full trace of the (K + 1)th-order cumulant tensor, Σ_{i_1,...,i_{(K+1)/2}=1}^n κ[X_{i_1}, X_{i_1}, X_{i_2}, X_{i_2}, . . . , X_{i_{(K+1)/2}}, X_{i_{(K+1)/2}}]. For example, when K = 1, we also track the covariance matrix trace Σ_{i=1}^n Var[X_i]."

4b. Pseudocode K=1 and K=2 (Appendix B, verbatim). Note: "Mean propagation tracks both the mean and the trace of the covariance matrix of activations. Tracking the entire diagonal of the covariance matrix would have lower mean squared error, but we provide the version that is consistent with our more general formulation."

Algorithm 1 Mean propagation (K = 1)
Require: weights W^(1), . . . , W^(L+1) ∈ R^{n×n}, activation functions ϕ_1, . . . , ϕ_L
1: μ ← 0 ∈ R^n
2: σ²_mean ← 1 ∈ R
3: for ℓ = 1, . . . , L + 1 do
4:   μ ← W^(ℓ) μ
5:   σ²_i ← σ²_mean Σ_{j=1}^n (W^(ℓ)_ij)²  for i = 1, . . . , n
6:   if ℓ ≤ L then
7:     a_i ← E_{Y∼N(μ_i,σ²_i)}[ϕ_ℓ(Y)]  for i = 1, . . . , n
8:     b_i ← E_{Y∼N(μ_i,σ²_i)}[ϕ_ℓ(Y)²]  for i = 1, . . . , n
9:     μ_i ← a_i  for i = 1, . . . , n
10:    σ²_mean ← (1/n) Σ_{i=1}^n (b_i − a_i²)
11:  end if
12: end for
13: return μ

Algorithm 2 Covariance propagation (K = 2)
Require: weights W^(1), . . . , W^(L+1) ∈ R^{n×n}, activation functions ϕ_1, . . . , ϕ_L
1: μ ← 0 ∈ R^n
2: Σ ← I_n ∈ R^{n×n}
3: for ℓ = 1, . . . , L + 1 do
4:   μ ← W^(ℓ) μ
5:   Σ ← W^(ℓ) Σ (W^(ℓ))^T
6:   if ℓ ≤ L then
7:     a_i ← E_{Y∼N(μ_i,Σ_ii)}[ϕ_ℓ(Y)]  for i = 1, . . . , n
8:     b_i ← E_{Y∼N(μ_i,Σ_ii)}[ϕ_ℓ(Y)²]  for i = 1, . . . , n
9:     c_i ← (1/√Σ_ii) E_{Y∼N(μ_i,Σ_ii)}[ ϕ_ℓ(Y) (Y − μ_i)/√Σ_ii ]  for i = 1, . . . , n
10:    μ_i ← a_i  for i = 1, . . . , n
11:    Σ_ii ← b_i − a_i²  for i = 1, . . . , n
12:    Σ_ij ← Σ_ij c_i c_j  for i, j = 1, . . . , n if i ≠ j
13:  end if
14: end for
15: return μ

B.2: "In mean propagation, we avoid computing the full covariance matrix explicitly, and instead approximate Σ by σ²_mean I_n." "For the activation function step, we use a Gaussian approximation ... we approximate the mean and covariance matrix of ϕ(Z) by the mean and covariance matrix of ϕ(Y), where Y ∈ R^n is a multivariate Gaussian with the same mean and covariance matrix as Z." "Using power cumulants allows us to take advantage of this by handling these variances separately from the off-diagonal covariances."

4c. General basic algorithm (S.4.1). Preliminaries:
- Trace/cup (Def S.2.2): "(tr T)_{i_1,...,i_{r−2}} = Σ_{i=1}^n T_{i,i,i_1,...,i_{r−2}}"; cup "g(T)_{i_1,...,i_{r+2}} = Σ_{u<v∈[r+2]} δ_{i_u,i_v} T_{i_s : s∉{u,v}}"; M-cup "g_M(T)_{i_1,...,i_{r+2}} = Σ_{u<v} M_{i_u,i_v} T_{i_s : s∉{u,v}}".
- Harmonic decomposition (Thm S.2.4): T ∈ S^r(R^n) has unique traceless h_{r−2s}(T) ∈ S^{r−2s}, 0 ≤ s ≤ ⌊r/2⌋, with T = Σ_s g^s(h_{r−2s}(T)); "h_{r−2s}(T) = Σ_{0≤t≤⌊(r−2s)/2⌋} c^h_{s,t} g^t(tr^{s+t}(T))  (6)" with (as extracted) "c^h_{s,t} = (−1)^t (r + n/2 − 2s − 1) / ( 2^{s+t} s! t! (r + n/2 − 2s − t − 1)_{s+t+1} )" (Pochhammer); from the proof: "c^h_{s,0} = (r + n/2 − 2s − 1)! / (2^s s! (r + n/2 − s − 1)!)" and "c^h_{s,t} = (−1)^t (r − 2s + n/2 − 2 − t)! / (2^t t! (r − 2s + n/2 − 2)!) c^h_{s,0}"; commutation relation "tr(g^k(T)) − g^k(tr(T)) = (2kr + kn + 2k(k − 1)) g^{k−1}(T)". (Sanity check, mine: r=2, s=1 gives h_0(T) = tr(T)/n, matching line 10 of Algorithm 1.) Notation h_{≤r−2s}, h_{≥r−2s} for partial sums. Rationale: "the harmonic decomposition ... coincides with the usual decomposition of polynomials into spherical harmonics"; "decomposition of S^r(R^n) into irreducible representations of the action of O(n) via the symmetric contraction ⊙ ... Because the distribution of Gaussian-initialized W is O(n)-symmetric, this is intuitively the right level of granularity to track cumulant tensors of activations".
- Diagonal slices (Def S.3.1/S.3.2): D_π T = T restricted to index tuples of type π; D_λ T = Σ_{π of type λ} D_π T; slice "(D̃_λ T)_{i_1,...,i_b} := (D_λ T)_{i_1,...,i_1 (λ_1 times),...,i_b,...,i_b (λ_b times)}" (9); D̃_λ T is λ-symmetric (invariant under the Young subgroup); pseudoinverse D̃^{−1}_λ places a slice back on the diagonal, "D̃_λ D̃^{−1}_λ = id, D̃^{−1}_λ D̃_λ = D_λ".
- Power cumulants (Def S.3.6): "κ^P_α[X]_{i_1,...,i_r} = κ[X^{α_1}_{i_1}, ..., X^{α_r}_{i_r}] if i_1, ..., i_r are pairwise distinct; 0 o/w." Proposition S.3.7 (power cumulant partition formula): "for each λ ⊢ r with b blocks, D̃_λ κ_r[X] = Σ_{ν∈Vec(λ)} c^vec_ν c^P_ν Π_{u∈ν} D̃_u κ^P_{|u|}[X], where the product is taken elementwise and c^P_ν := Σ_{ω⊢ν} 1[ω totally disconnected] (−1)^{|ω|−1} (|ω| − 1)!" (sum over set partitions ω of the blocks of ν; totally disconnected = tuples in each block of ω have disjoint support). "each entry of the cumulants tensors is calculated in O(1) time from the power cumulants."
- Diagonal↔harmonic (Props S.3.10, S.3.11): "tr^s D_λ T = Σ_{γ∈Γ^{t.d.}([b],s)} ( c^g_{λ,γ} 1_{λ≥d_γ} D̃^{−1}_{(λ−d_γ)} Sum_{{j: λ_j = d_γ(j)}} D̃_λ T )  (11)", "c^g_{λ,γ} := 2^s s! r! Π_{i∈[b]} (λ_i − d_γ(i) + 1)_{d_γ(i)} / ( (r − 2s + 1)_{2s} Π_{j∈[r]} b_j! Π_{i∈[b]} λ_i! 2^{γ({i})} Π_{{i,j}⊆[b]} γ({i,j})! )", computable "in O(n^{r−2s}) time"; and "D̃_λ g^s_M(T) = Σ_{γ∈Γ([b],s)} c̃^g_{λ,γ} T ⊙_γ M  (12)", "(T ⊙_γ M)_{i_1,...,i_b} = (D̃_{λ−d_γ} T)_{i_1,...,i_b} Π_{{u,v}∈γ} M_{i_u,i_v} if i all distinct, 0 otherwise", "c̃^g_{λ,γ} := Π_{i∈[b]} (λ_i − d_γ(i) + 1)_{d_γ(i)} / ( Π_{i∈[b]} 2^{γ({i})} Π_{{i,j}⊆[b]} γ({i,j})! )", computable "in O(n^b) time". Here γ is a multigraph on the b blocks, d_γ(j) = Σ_{j'≠j} γ({j,j'}) + 2γ({j,j}).

S.4 overview: "Propagating the harmonic projections through a linear layer W is conceptually easy (but is asymptotically the most expensive step): we simply apply the symmetric contraction ⊙W and replace cups with WW^T-cups. For nonlinearities, we use the formulae in Section S.3.5 to move from the harmonic to the diagonal decomposition, apply the power cumulant combinatorics in the diagonal setting, then project back to harmonics."

S.4.1 Basic algorithm (verbatim modulo layout). Input: MLP (L, n, ϕ_ℓ, θ) and "The 'maximum degree' K ≥ 1". "Our algorithm then propagates tensors η̃_r[X^(ℓ)] ∈ S^{r−2s_K(r)}(R^n) through MLP layers. These are estimates for h_{≥r−2s_K(r)}(κ_r[X^(ℓ)]), where
  s_K(r) := 0 if r ≤ K;  r/2 if r = K + 1 ≡ 0 mod 2;  ∞ otherwise.
By convention, if 2s_K(r) > r, then η̃_r[X^(ℓ)] = 0. Thus, only cumulants with degree r no more than R := K (K ≡ 0 mod 2) or K + 1 (K ≡ 1 mod 2) are tracked at all.
1. Initialize tensors η̃_r[X^(0)] = h_{≥r−2s_K(r)}(κ_r[X^(0)]) = 1 if r = 2 and K = 1; I_n if r = 2 and K > 1; 0 if r ≠ 2.
2. For 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ L, repeat the following:
 (a) Compute the tensors T^(ℓ)_r := η̃_r[X^(ℓ−1)] ⊙ W^(ℓ) ∀r ∈ [R];  M^(ℓ) := D_(2)(W^(ℓ)W^(ℓ)T) if K = 1; W^(ℓ)W^(ℓ)T if K > 1. Note that each T^(ℓ)_r is a symmetric contraction of a (r − 2s_K(r))-tensor, and thus takes time O(n^{r−2s_K(r)+1}) ≤ O(n^{K+1}). The matrix M^(ℓ) takes time O(n²) if K = 1, otherwise O(n³). In any case, this is no more than the O(n^{K+1}) budget.
 (b) Write the estimated pre-activation mean and variance vectors μ := T^(ℓ)_1 ∈ R^n, σ² := D̃_(2) g^{s_K(2)}_{M^(ℓ)} T^(ℓ)_2 ∈ R^n. For each r ∈ [K] and λ ⊢ r, compute the λ-slice of the activation power cumulant using a truncation of (10) applied to powers of the activation function:
   D̃_λ(κ̃^P[X^(ℓ)])_{i_1,...,i_b} := Σ_{k∈Z^b_{≥0}, |k|≤2K−1} (1/k!) Π_{a=1}^b ( (ϕ_ℓ^{λ_a})^_{k_a}^{(μ_{k_a},σ²_{k_a})} )_{i_a}  Σ_{ν∈cVec^{[≤2]}(k), k(ν)≤K} Π_{u∈ν} ( D̃_u g^{s_K(|u|)}_{M^(ℓ)} T^(ℓ)_{|u|} )_{i_1,...,i_b},   (13)
   where each D̃_u g^{s_K(|u|)}_{M^(ℓ)} T^(ℓ)_{|S|} is computed using (12), each Hermite coefficient is computed using Proposition S.3.8 if ϕ_ℓ = ReLU and Gauss-Hermite otherwise (see Section F), and
   1 + Σ_{u∈ν} ( Σ_{j=1}^b u_j − 1 − 1[u_j all even] ) =: k(ν) ≤ K   (14)
   is the condition for including vector partition ν. If K is odd, do the same for all λ ⊢ K + 1 having no singleton blocks.
 (c) For the same set of λ as in the previous step, convert power cumulants to cumulants slicewise: D̃_λ κ̃_r[X^(ℓ)] := Σ_{ν∈Vec(λ)} c^vec_ν c^P_ν Π_{u∈ν} D̃_u κ^P_r[X^(ℓ)]  (This is (S.3.7) applied to the estimated power cumulants.)
 (d) For each r ∈ [R], project the estimated cumulants onto the (≤ r − 2s_K)-harmonics: η_r[X^(ℓ)] := Σ_{s'=s}^{⌊r/2⌋} g^{s'−s} ( Σ_{t=0}^{⌊(r−2s)/2⌋} c^h_{s',t} g^t tr^{s'+t} ( Σ_{λ⊢[r]} D̃_λ κ̃_r[X^(ℓ)] ) ) where the trace is distributed over the sum of diagonals then computed with (11).
3. Return W^(L+1) η̃_1[X^(L)]."
Footnote 19: "In practice, instead of summing diagrams directly, we sum orbits of diagrams under permutations of indices then λ-symmetrize the result."
Equivalent truncation condition in diagram language (S.5.2): "k(π) := 1 + |k| − |π| − #{S ∈ π : ∀v, |S ∩ [k_v]| ≡ 0 mod 2}", include iff k(π) ≤ K, with |k| ≤ 2K − 1.
What is tracked at each order, summarised: all cumulant tensors of order r ≤ K in full (as diagonal slices D̃_λ for all λ ⊢ r, then re-projected to harmonics); if K is odd additionally the scalar h_0 (full trace) of the order-(K+1) cumulant (via its no-singleton-block diagonal slices); for the pre-activation layer, the K+1 scalar re-enters as a g^{(K+1)/2}_{WW^T} cup.

(The polynomial algorithm of S.2.6 used for the proofs is structurally identical but uses the polynomial formula (3) and no power cumulants; "this algorithm does not agree with the basic algorithm, even in the case that the activation functions are polynomials".)

4d. Augmented algorithm (S.4.2, verbatim): "The basic algorithm in Section S.4.1 works by tracking every harmonic part that would incur at least Ω(n^{−K+1}) MSE if dropped; the largest of these parts takes Θ(n^{K+1}) time to propagate. We may consider an algorithm that additionally tracks every harmonic part that can be propagated in O(n^K) time and contributes at least Ω(n^{−K}) MSE. This augmented algorithm would improve the constant factor of the leading-order term in MSE, while keeping the leading-order term in FLOPs the same. It follows from our bounds on cumulant sizes (Proposition S.2.12) that the augmented algorithm corresponds to replacing s_K in the basic algorithm with
  s^aug_K(r) := 0 if r ≤ K;  1 if r = K + 1;  r/2 if r = K + 2 ≡ 0 mod 2;  ∞ otherwise.
All other parts of cumulant propagation remain the same." I.e. for order K+1 it tracks everything except the traceless component (h_{≤K−1}), and for odd K also the full trace of order K+2. Sec 4.2: "(when K is odd, say) ... track not merely the full trace of the (K + 1)th-order cumulant tensor, but the entire tensor except for its traceless component". Footnote 20: "As long as K ≥ 3. For lower K, the linear step is not the only computation that is leading order in FLOPs, so augmenting cumulant propagation in fact increases the leading-order FLOPs term." (Confirmed by Table 1: K=2 augmented is 11n³L vs 7n³L.)

4e. Factorized algorithm (S.4.3). "The time complexity of our cumulant propagation algorithm is dominated by the O(n^{K+1}) symmetric contraction of the full degree-K cumulant tensor during the linear step. By representing this tensor in a factorized form, we are able to reduce the linear step's runtime, and thus that of the entire algorithm, to O(n^K)." "If we track the dependence on L explicitly, the time complexity of basic cumulant propagation through the entire MLP scales linearly with L, while that of the factored version scales quadratically. The reason is that the factored version propagates a running list of factors ... adding a constant number of new factors at each layer." Sec 4.3: "For K ≥ 3, the tensor of Kth-order cumulants admits a factorized representation of size n^{K−1} that can be preserved through each step of the algorithm. ... improve the runtime of our algorithm by a factor of n, up to constant factors, without changing its final output." "Unlike the augmented version of our algorithm, the factorized version offers a strict performance improvement for sufficiently large n".
K = 3 factorization (15): "T_{i_1,i_2,i_3} = (1/6) Σ_{σ∈S_3} Σ_{j=1}^J A_{i_σ(1),j} B_{i_σ(2),j} C_{i_σ(3),j} where J is the number of factors and A, B, C ∈ R^{n×J}. Throughout, J = O(n). We notate (15) sans indices as T = F(A, B, C)." Sums concatenate: "F(A,B,C) + F(A',B',C') = F([A A'], [B B'], [C C'])". Linear step: "F(A, B, C) ⊙ W = F(WA, WB, WC). When J = O(n), the factors of the symmetric contraction can be computed in O(n³) time—this is what gives us the asymptotic improvement over the naïve O(n⁴) linear step." Asymptotics (Sec 5, verbatim): "The runtime of the basic version of our algorithm is at most c'_K L n^{K+1}, and the runtime of the factorized version is at most c''_K L² n^K whenever K ≥ 3. Here, c_K, c'_K and c''_K are constants that depend only on K." Footnote 7: "The new constant factors scale quadratically rather than linearly width depth, as can be seen for K ≤ 4 in Appendix J." S.4: "Our basic cumulant propagation estimator takes O(n^{K+1}) time and incurs O(n^{−K}) MSE for all integral K ≥ 0, thus matching sampling for non-integral K as well (by taking the floor). The factorized version of this algorithm takes only O(n^K) time to compute the same estimate". Theorem 5.1 time: O(n²/ε²); Theorem 5.2 time: O(n/ε²) given ε² = O(1/n²). Choice of K: "for each n, we take K to be the smallest integer such that 1/n^K ≤ ε²". Footnote 16: "One might have thought that O(n^{K+2}) would be sufficient to achieve a time of O(n²/ε²), but this is not the case because K is constrained to be an integer, while ε² could be Θ(n^{−k}) for some non-integer k."

Per-step costs: symmetric contraction of an r-tensor O(n^{r+1}); M^(ℓ) O(n²) (K=1) or O(n³); (11) O(n^{r−2s}); (12) O(n^b); power-cumulant→cumulant O(1) per entry.

FLOP accounting (Appendix J): "we use an extension of PyTorch's FlopCounterMode"; "Entrywise operation tracking: Unlike sampling baselines, our cumulant propagation algorithm expends a large portion of its FLOPs in entrywise operations during the nonlinear step. Thus ... we add tracking for entrywise arithmetic operations (+-*/)". Footnote 14: "This is a practical weakness of using the Hermite expansion for the nonlinearity which we believe can be corrected in future work." "Mock symmetric tensor kernels: Cumulant tensors are symmetric, but our proof-of-concept implementation ... uses full torch.Tensor objects. To simulate the number of FLOPs that would be consumed by an implementation with efficient symmetric tensor CUDA kernels, we multiply FLOP counts for elementwise operations on symmetric tensors by the adjustment factor α_{n,d} := dim S^d(R^n)/dim T^d(R^n) = C(n+d−1, d) n^{−d}." For λ = (1^{c_1} 2^{c_2} ... d^{c_d}) ⊢ d with c = Σ c_i blocks: "α_{π,n} := dim S^λ(R^n)/dim T^d(R^n) = Π_{i=1}^d C(n+c_i−1, c_i) n^{−c_i}." Contraction: "Over full tensors, W-contraction takes 2n^{d+1} FLOPs: there are n^d output entries, and each takes 2n FLOPs to compute (counting add and multiply separately). Thus − ⊙ W takes 2dn^{d+1} FLOPs total. Over symmetric tensors, observe that the intermediate after contracting in the ith W is (1^i 2^{d−i})-symmetric ... The total number of FLOPs over symmetric tensors can thus be multiplied by the adjustment factor β_{d,n} := (1/d) Σ_{i=1}^d α_{(1^i 2^{d−i}),n} = (1/(d n^d)) Σ_{i=1}^d C(n+i−1, i) C(n+d−i−1, d−i). As n → ∞, α_{π,n} → α_{π,∞} := Π_{i=1}^d 1/c_i!;  β_{d,n} → β_{d,∞} := (1/d) Σ_{i=1}^d 1/(i!(d−i)!). For reference, β_{1,∞} = 1, β_{2,∞} = 3/4, β_{3,∞} = 7/18, β_{4,∞} = 5/32. Note that there may be algorithms for performing symmetric tensor contractions even more efficiently than this [Solomonik and Demmel, 2015], but we disregard these for the sake of fairness and simplicity." "Polynomial fit: Because the FLOP count for cumulant propagation through a depth L MLP is a polynomial in n and L with leading term n^{K+1}L for the unfactored version (and n^K L² for factored), we can simply fit a polynomial of the specified degree to the FLOP counts for a few small values of n, L to find the exact counts for any values. We check that these fit polynomials are exact on held out values of n, L."

Theorem 5.1 (verbatim): "Fix an integer L ≥ 0 and polynomial activation functions ϕ_1, . . . , ϕ_L. Given weights θ ∈ R^((L+1)×n×n) drawn independently from N(0, 2/n) for an integer n ≥ 1, write M_θ : R^n → R^n for the MLP with L hidden layers, width n, activation functions ϕ_1, . . . , ϕ_L and weights θ. Then there is an estimation procedure G(θ, ε) such that for every noticeable function 0 < ε(n) ≤ 1 and every output index i, as n → ∞,
  E_θ[ ( G(θ, ε(n))_i − E_{X∼N(0,I_n)}[M_θ(X)]_i )² ] = O(ε(n)²)
and G(θ, ε) runs in time O(n²/ε²)."
Theorem 5.2 (verbatim): "In Theorem 5.1, if we also require that ε(n)² = O(1/n²), then G(θ, ε) can be made to run in time O(n/ε²)."
Footnote 8 (noticeable): "This means that for some k ≥ 0, ε(n) ≥ 1/n^k for all sufficiently large n [Goldreich, 2005, Chapter 1.2]." Footnote 9: "A mean squared error of ε(n)² = O(1/n²) is achieved with K ≥ 2, which costs as much as sampling with Ω(n) samples." Footnote 10: "We make the mild assumptions that the activation functions are measurable and polynomially bounded." "We believe that with additional effort, both results can be extended from polynomials to general activation functions." Intro: "roughly speaking, if ε(n)² ∼ 1/n^K, then we track cumulants of order at most K." Conjectured depth/width scaling (Sec 5, verbatim): "These results treat the number of hidden layers L as a constant, and choose the maximum cumulant order K based on the error tolerance ε. Expressed in terms of these parameters, we conjecture that our algorithms have mean squared error at most c_K (L/n)^K, although the dependence on L is difficult to analyze precisely." Appendix D: "We conjecture this depth scaling based on a heuristic analysis of the error, but we can also test it empirically. ... As conjectured, the mean squared error roughly follows a curve proportional to L^K when L is large. This depth scaling is worse than Monte Carlo sampling, whose error does not increase with depth. We believe that a sample-free algorithm can be developed whose error also does not increase with depth either, but we leave this problem to future work." Sec 6.3: "The central finding of our theoretical analysis is that the mean squared error of our algorithm is O(1/n^K) ... the mean squared error closely follows a curve proportional to 1/n^K when n is large. Note, however, that increasing K can worsen performance at small values of n."

Cumulant-size bound, eq. (2) / Corollary S.2.14 (verbatim): "E[κ_r[Z^(ℓ)]²_{i_1,...,i_r}] = O(n^{2−r}) if r is even, and each value in i_1, . . . , i_r appears with even multiplicity; O(n^{1−r}) otherwise." (Hence the odd-K trace: diagonal-type entries of the (K+1)-th cumulant are a factor n larger than generic ones.) Proposition S.2.12: "any connected scalar contraction of cumulants grows linearly in n as n → ∞" (fully-contracted connected diagram of pre-/post-activation cumulants "has n-growth"). Growth definition S.2.6: T = O(n^ρ) iff sup over n, indices of ‖(T_n)_{i_1..i_r}‖_p n^{−ρ} < ∞ for all 1 ≤ p < ∞.
Heuristic from (2): "to get mean squared error (MSE) O(n^{−K}) we can do the following: If K is even, estimate all cumulants of order at most K; If K is odd, estimate all cumulants of order at most K and the elements κ_{K+1}[Z^(ℓ)]_{i_1,i_1,...,i_{(K+1)/2},i_{(K+1)/2}}. (As a more efficient bookkeeping device, we track these terms via the scalar harmonic component of κ_{K+1})."
Main error theorems: Theorem S.2.15 (polynomial algorithm) and Theorem S.5.2 (basic Hermite algorithm): "For all r ≥ 1 and ℓ ∈ [L + 1], the tensor sequence Δ_r[Z^(ℓ)] has n^{−(K/2)}-growth", where Δ_r := κ_r − κ̃_r; Thm S.5.2 assumes (1) each ϕ_ℓ "tame" w.r.t. Z^(ℓ) and (2) Prop S.2.12's conclusion. "the error of the factorized algorithm is precisely the same, the only difference is in the run-time". Proof mechanism: expectation over W via Isserlis (Prop S.2.9: E_W[D ⊙ W] = (2/n)^{|E|/2} Σ_{pairings} D_{π,1} D_{π,2}, vanishing if |E| odd), Cauchy–Schwarz for diagrams (Lemma S.2.11), Hölder; dropped truncation terms with k(π) > K have growth n^{(p'/2)(1−k(π))} = O(n^{−Kp'/2}).
Tameness (Def S.5.1): (1) the residual R^P_{α,k_0}[ϕ(Z)] of truncating the power-cumulant diagram formula at |k| ≤ k_0 "has n^{−(k_0+1)/4}-growth"; (2) Hermite coefficient vectors (ϕ^α)^_k^{(E[Z],Var[Z])} "have O(1)-growth"; (3) if E[Z]−μ and Var[Z]−σ² have n^{−k_0}-growth then Δ(ϕ^α)^_k has n^{−k_0}-growth. Proposition S.5.3: polynomials are tame. For ReLU/general: not proven (S.6).
Variance concentration (eq. 22): "E[(κ_2[Z^(ℓ)]_{i,i} − (1/n) Tr κ_2[Z^(ℓ)])²] = 8n^{−4}(n − 1)² ‖κ_2[X^(ℓ)]‖²_F, which has n^{−1}-growth."
Ablation (Sec 6.4): without power cumulants and the odd-K trace, "the mean squared error is O(1) instead of O(1/n^K), and so the ablated version of our algorithm no longer matches the performance of sampling in the large-width limit."
Exactness: "Our procedures are exact when there is only 1 hidden layer."

9. DISCUSSION: DEPTH, TRUNCATION, SYMMETRIC-TENSOR ACCOUNTING, DOMINANT CUMULANTS, NUMERICS, OPEN PROBLEMS
=====================================================================

Why depth hurts: only the heuristic conjecture c_K(L/n)^K and the empirical L^K slope (Fig 8); "the dependence on L is difficult to analyze precisely"; the proofs treat L as constant and the error induction (Δ_r[Z^(ℓ)] has n^{−K/2} growth for every ℓ) carries L-dependent constants. Factorized runtime constants grow as L² because "J^(ℓ) = 6nℓ" factors accumulate. Open problems (Sec 7.3, verbatim): "Even for random MLPs, we do not know whether it is possible to match the performance of random sampling at large depth (i.e., asymptotically as the number of hidden layers L tends to infinity), or for narrow networks (i.e., asymptotically as the error tolerance ε → 0 while the width n is held fixed). We could also ask the same questions for MLPs with weights drawn from non-independent or non-Gaussian distributions."

Truncation: truncation of (13) at |k| ≤ 2K − 1 and k(ν) ≤ K (14); the heuristic justification is that dropped diagrams have squared size O(n^{1−k(ν)}) ≤ O(n^{−K}). "any finite truncation will surely fail" for the naive Taylor-based diagram sum because means/variances are Θ(1) — fixed by Hermite coefficients (absorbing size-1,2 parts within a vertex group) and power cumulants (absorbing Θ(1) terms with repeated indices). S.7: "For non-polynomials, the formulas still hold in a formal sense, but can fail to converge. Convergence of these formal expansions is fragile: we do not expect these expansions to converge for a fixed n, but instead truncate to a finite summation such that the error converges to zero as n → ∞." S.6.2: "We do not, in fact, expect this infinite summation formula to converge at any fixed width n, but instead that a fixed truncation should be an asymptotically good approximation as n → ∞. This is the property of an asymptotic expansion, and in fact we can motivate the diagram summation formula through the theory of Edgeworth expansions". Conditions (2),(3) of tameness "roughly correspond to a bounded, Lipschitz property of the Hermite coefficients of ϕ around (E[Z], Var[Z])"; "Provided the activation functions are normalized correctly to keep Tr κ_2[Z^(ℓ)] constant, this suggests that as n → ∞ the variances will concentrate, and then provided the Hermite coefficients are well-behaved in a rectangle (−C, C) × (1 − ϵ, 1 + ϵ), the properties (2) and (3) should follow."

Which cumulants dominate: eq. (2): generic entries of κ_r[Z] have squared size n^{1−r}; entries of even r with all indices paired have n^{2−r}. Hence for odd K the paired entries of κ_{K+1} (squared size n^{1−K}) are as large as the generic κ_K entries and must be tracked, via the scalar full trace; the augmented variant tracks all harmonic parts of κ_{K+1} except the traceless one (and for odd K the trace of κ_{K+2}). Ablation: dropping power cumulants/trace yields O(1) MSE. For the K=2 algorithm, the exact per-neuron variance (b_i − a_i²) vs the one-term off-diagonal covariance is precisely the power-cumulant split. Low-probability: third-and-higher cumulants of the final pre-activations dominate the error of Gaussian approaches.

Symmetric-tensor FLOP accounting: see Section 5 above (α, β factors; β_{d,∞} = 1, 3/4, 7/18, 5/32 for d = 1..4; Solomonik–Demmel ignored). Footnote 19 (orbit summation then λ-symmetrize). Mock kernels only; actual code uses full torch tensors, hence redundant computation.

Numerical issues: the paper contains NO discussion of float32 vs float64 or conditioning. The only numerics-adjacent remarks: ReLU Hermite coefficients "have a singularity at σ² = 0"; Hermite expansions make the nonlinear step entrywise-FLOP heavy (footnote 14); pure-Python entrywise ops dominate wall-clock; Gauss–Hermite with m = 100 for GELU/tanh.

10. BASELINES / RELATED METHODS AND HOW THEY RELATE
=====================================================================

- Monte Carlo sampling (the main baseline, Sec 2.3).
- Wright, Nakahira, Moura [2024] "An analytic solution to covariance propagation in neural networks" (AISTATS): "Theorem 1 of Wright et al. [2024] is a special case of our diagram summation formula" = Corollary A.3 (K=2, Gaussian inputs, full Hermite series for covariances); the paper's K=2 Algorithm 2 keeps only the k=1 term off-diagonal and computes variances exactly.
- Gast and Roth [2018] "Lightweight probabilistic deep networks": "derive formulas for propagating means and variances only, but not off-diagonal covariances" (Wright et al. builds on it).
- Bibi, Alfadly, Ghanem [2018]: "an alternative covariance propagation formula specifically for ReLU networks with 1 hidden layer."
- Frey and Hinton [1999]: "analytic formulas for the mean and variance of ReLU and several other activation functions on a Gaussian input distribution."
- Fischer et al. [2022] "Decomposing neural networks as mappings of correlation functions": cumulant propagation via Hermite/Gram–Charlier expansions, class-conditional cumulants for mechanistic training on MNIST; "their implementation and error analysis focuses on covariance propagation, i.e., ... maximum cumulant order 2". The present paper "build[s] on their work extending it to arbitrarily high cumulant order, in setting of wide random networks".
- Christiano, Neyman, Xu [2022] "Formalizing the presumption of independence", Appendix D: origin of cumulant propagation (arithmetic circuits); their "mean propagation" tracks only the mean (= this paper's ablated K=1).
- Kendall [1941] / Genz & Bretz [2009, eq. 3.2]: step-function/Gaussian special case of the diagram formula.
- Hanin [2023, 2024], Dunbar & Aaronson [2025], Roberts–Yaida–Hanin [2022], Neal, Lee et al., Jacot et al.: wide-network/annealed (random-weight ensemble) literature; explicitly distinguished as studying fixed inputs over random weights rather than fixed weights over random inputs.
- Low-probability baselines (App G): naive MC; MC + Gaussian extrapolation (mean/variance of final pre-activations from samples, then Gaussian tail; "similar to the Gaussian Logit Difference method of Wu and Hilton [2025]"; "performs similarly to using cumulant propagation to estimate the mean and variance of each of these neurons"); MC + cumulant extrapolation (mean, variance and third cumulant from samples, then the Hermite-based propagation through the threshold); Zero.
- Wu and Hilton [2025], Neyman et al. [2025] "Competing with sampling": motivation and the conjecture that any estimation procedure can be matched by a mechanistic one.
- Solomonik and Demmel [2015]: faster symmetric contractions, deliberately not used.
- Malyshev [1980] cluster expansions; Hall [1992] Edgeworth expansions; Kagawa [2015] Hermite expansion of the Heaviside function (source of Prop S.3.8); Dai and Xu [2013] spherical harmonics.

Correspondence to the caller's task (observation, not from the paper): the per-layer post-ReLU means required by the challenge are the vectors a_i (lines 7/9–10 of Algorithms 1–2) = η̃_1[X^(ℓ)] in the general algorithm; the paper's reported output MSE is that of W^(L+1) η̃_1[X^(L)], a fixed linear image of the final hidden-layer mean error.
