## 4. The endpoint the chat had to reach: the paper's proof architecture

*Everything in this section is **Source** (arXiv:2609.38007v1, full text read through alphaXiv's extraction and Exa's rendering of the arXiv HTML; equation numbers follow the HTML rendering, with the PDF numbering in brackets where it differs). ### 4.1 Setting

Sites $\Gamma$ with metric $d$; finite-dimensional $\mathcal H_x$; finite volume $\Lambda$; $H_\Lambda=\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}\Phi(X)$, $\Phi(X)$ self-adjoint and supported on $X$; $\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}=e^{-\beta H_\Lambda}/Z_\Lambda$, $\beta>0$. A **full partition** $\Lambda=A\sqcup B\sqcup C$, $A,C\neq\varnothing$, $r=d(A,C)$; "This hypothesis is essential to the cut-Hamiltonian argument; a marginal with an arbitrary unobserved exterior is not silently identified with a local Gibbs state." CMI $I(A:C|B)=S(AB)+S(BC)-S(B)-S(ABC)$. Cut interaction and cut strength:
$$
V=\sum_{X\cap A\neq\varnothing,\ X\cap A^c\neq\varnothing}\Phi(X),\qquad g_{A|A^c}=\sum_{X\cap A\neq\varnothing,\ X\cap A^c\neq\varnothing}\|\Phi(X)\|_\infty\ \ (\ge\|V\|_\infty).
$$

### 4.2 The three theorems

**Theorem II.1 (finite range).** If $H_\Lambda=\sum_\gamma h_\gamma$ with $\|h_\gamma\|\le J$, $\operatorname{diam}\operatorname{supp}h_\gamma\le R_0$, and each term overlaps at most $\mathfrak d$ terms (counting itself), all uniform in $\Lambda$, then for every $\beta>0$ there are $C_\beta,c_\beta>0$ depending only on $(J,R_0,\mathfrak d,\beta)$ with
$$
I(A:C|B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\le C_\beta\exp\big(C_\beta\,g_{A|A^c}-c_\beta r\big),
$$
and on $\mathbb Z^D$, $g_{A|A^c}\le C|\partial_eA|$ ($\partial_eA$ = nearest-neighbour edges with one endpoint in $A$), so $I\le C_\beta e^{C_\beta|\partial_eA|-c_\beta r}$. Chen–Rouzé's prefactor was exponential in $|A|$.

**Theorem II.2 (stretched-exponential, unrestricted body size).** $\Gamma=\mathbb Z^D$, $D\ge2$, $\ell^1$ metric, bounded on-site dimensions, $\mu>0$, $0<\theta\le1$, $J_{\mu,\theta}:=\sup_{x,y}e^{\mu d(x,y)^\theta}\sum_{X\ni x,y}\|\Phi(X)\|<\infty$ (supremum includes $x=y$; no bound on $|X|$ or on the number of terms per site). Then
$$
I(A:C|B)\le C_\beta\exp\big[C_\beta(1+|\partial_eA|)^{\theta/D}-c_\beta(1+r)^{\theta/(D+1-\theta)}\big].
$$

**Theorem II.3 (power law, unrestricted body size).** $J_\zeta:=\sup_{x,y}(1+d(x,y))^\zeta\sum_{X\ni x,y}\|\Phi(X)\|<\infty$, $\zeta>D$; $b_\zeta=1+2\zeta/D$, $\nu_\zeta=b_\zeta(\zeta-2D)/(\zeta-D+b_\zeta)$. For $D<\zeta<2D$: $I\le C_\beta(1+|A|)^{b_\zeta+1}[\log(e+r)]^{-b_\zeta}$; at $\zeta=2D$: $C_\beta(1+|A|)^6[\log(e+r)]^{-5}[\log(e+\log(e+r))]^2$; for $\zeta>2D$: $C_\beta(1+|A|)^{b_\zeta+1}(1+r)^{-\nu_\zeta}$.

**Consequences stated in §VII.** (i) Coarse-graining in 2D: for partitions with $|\partial_eA_s|\le ps$ and $d(A_s,C_s)\ge qs$, $I\le C_\beta\exp[-(c_\beta q-C_\beta p)s]$, which vanishes exponentially once the buffer is wide enough relative to $A_s$; "The essential point in both cases is that the boundary length and buffer width scale linearly in two dimensions." (ii) A trade-off: under II.1 or II.2, for every $p>0$, $I\le C_{\beta,p}(1+|A|)^{q_p}(1+r)^{-p}$. Open: polynomial region dependence with exponential distance decay; algebraic decay throughout $D<\zeta\le2D$; bosons.

### 4.3 The finite-range proof, lemma by lemma

The proof has one comparison step, one exact variational identity, one estimate that localises it, and two physical inputs. Write $\delta_R(\rho,\sigma)=D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\rho_R\|\sigma_R)$, the loss of relative entropy when the complement $E$ of $R$ is traced out.

**Step 0. Lemma III.1 (cut comparison).** $H_\Lambda=H_0+V$, $H_0=H_A+H_{BC}$, $\sigma=e^{-\beta H_0}/Z_0=\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_{BC}$, $\rho=e^{-\beta(H_0+V)}/Z$. Then
$$
\delta_{AB}(\rho,\sigma)=I(A:C|B)_\rho+D(\rho_{BC}\|\gamma_{BC})-D(\rho_B\|\gamma_B)\ \ge\ I(A:C|B)_\rho ,\qquad |\log Z-\log Z_0|\le\beta g_{A|A^c}.
$$
*Uses:* the product form of $\sigma$ (which needs $B=\Lambda\setminus(A\cup C)$: the full-partition hypothesis), and data processing under $\mathrm{Tr}_C$ for the last difference. The CMI, a three-region quantity, has become a two-state monotonicity defect with the retained region $R=AB$.

**Step 1. Lemma III.2 (resolvent formula).** With $\Delta=L_\sigma R_{\rho^{-1}}$ on Hilbert–Schmidt space (the relative modular operator), $K=\log\Delta$, $\Delta_R=L_{\sigma_R}R_{\rho_R^{-1}}$:
$$
\delta_R=\int_0^\infty j(s)\,ds,\qquad j(s)=\langle\rho^{1/2},(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\rangle-\langle\rho_R^{1/2},(\Delta_R+s)^{-1}\rho_R^{1/2}\rangle .
$$
*Proof idea:* $D(\rho\|\sigma)=-\langle\rho^{1/2},\log\Delta\,\rho^{1/2}\rangle$ and $-\log\lambda=\int_0^\infty\big(\frac1{\lambda+s}-\frac1{1+s}\big)ds$ (Appendix B).

**Step 2. Lemma III.3 (variational form; imported).** $W_R(Y)=(Y\rho_R^{-1/2}\otimes\mathbf 1_E)\rho^{1/2}$ is an isometry with $W_R\rho_R^{1/2}=\rho^{1/2}$, $W_R^\dagger\Delta W_R=\Delta_R$, and
$$
j(s)=\inf_{y\in\operatorname{Ran}W_R}\big\|(\Delta+s)^{1/2}\big(y-(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\big)\big\|_2^2 ,
$$
attained at $y=W_R(\Delta_R+s)^{-1}\rho_R^{1/2}$. So every trial vector in $\operatorname{Ran}W_R$ gives an upper bound. The paper credits it as "the variational form of [Carlen–Vershynina, *Recovery map stability for the data processing inequality*, J. Phys. A 53 (2020), Lemma 2.1 and Eqs. (2.8)–(2.9)]".

**Step 3. The trial vector and the modular leakage (definition (29)).** With $\mathsf E_R(O)=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_EO}{\dim\mathcal H_E}\otimes\mathbf 1_E$:
$$
\eta_R(t)=\big\|\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}-\mathsf E_R(\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it})\big\|_\infty,\qquad \mathfrak q_R=\tfrac12\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\eta_R(t)}{|\sinh\pi t|}\,dt .
$$
The scalar identity $\frac1{s+e^u}=\frac1{1+s}+\frac{i}{2s}\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{s^{-it}(e^{itu}-1)}{\sinh\pi t}dt$ (from $\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin vt}{\sinh\pi t}dt=\frac12\tanh\frac v2$, DLMF 4.40.8) and $\Delta^{it}\rho^{1/2}=\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}\rho^{1/2}$ give $(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}=B_s\rho^{1/2}$ with $B_s$ a $1/\sinh$-weighted modular-time integral of $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$. The trial vector is $y_s=\mathsf E_R(B_s)\rho^{1/2}\in\operatorname{Ran}W_R$, and $\|B_s-\mathsf E_R(B_s)\|_\infty\le\mathfrak q_R/s$.

**Step 4. Lemma III.4 and Corollary III.5 (localisation of the loss).** The key estimate is (36):
$$
0\le j(s)\le\frac{1+s}{s^2}\,\mathfrak q_R^2 .
$$
For small $s$, $\int_0^a j\le\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+a\Delta^{-1})\rho^{1/2}\rangle$; for large $s$, Jensen on the spectral measure of $\Delta_R$ (whose mean is $\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma_R=1$) gives $\int_b^\infty j\le b^{-1}$. Hence, for every $\ell>0$, $\delta_R\le\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+e^{-\ell-K})\rho^{1/2}\rangle+e^{-\ell}+\mathfrak q_R^2(e^\ell+2\ell)$, and with $\log(1+w)\le w^\alpha/\alpha$ and $\langle\rho^{1/2},\Delta^{-\alpha}\rho^{1/2}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})$, optimising $a,b$:
$$
\delta_R(\rho,\sigma)\le\Big(\frac1\alpha+3\Big)\big[\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\big]^{1/(1+\alpha)}\,\mathfrak q_R^{\,2\alpha/(1+\alpha)}\qquad(0<\alpha\le1).
$$
In words: the loss is at most a Rényi-size factor of the perturbation times a power of the leakage of the Connes cocycle $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$ out of the retained algebra. If $\mathfrak q_R=0$ then $\delta_R=0$.

**Step 5. Lemma IV.1 (leakage, from locality; imported Lieb–Robinson).** For the cut pair, $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}=e^{-i\beta tH_0}e^{i\beta t(H_0+V)}$ is the interaction-picture propagator of $V(u)=e^{-iuH_0}Ve^{iuH_0}$. Crude bound: $\eta_{AB}(t)\le\min\{2,2\beta g|t|\}$, so $\mathfrak q_{AB}\le\beta g/2$. Localised bound: compare with $W_{AB}(u)$ generated by $\mathsf E_{AB}(V(u))$ (unitary, supported in $AB$, hence invisible to $\mathrm{id}-\mathsf E_{AB}$); Duhamel gives $\eta_{AB}(t)\le2\int_0^{\beta|t|}\|V(\pm u)-\mathsf E_{AB}V(\pm u)\|du$, and the finite-range Lieb–Robinson bound with local approximation (Nachtergaele–Sims–Young, Eq. (3.26) and Cor. 4.4) gives $\|e^{-iuH_0}h_\gamma e^{iuH_0}-\mathsf E_{AB}(\cdot)\|\le C_{LR}\|h_\gamma\|e^{v|u|-\mu_{LR}d(\operatorname{supp}h_\gamma,C)}$. Every cut term meets $A$ and has diameter $\le R_0$ (**finite range used here**), so $d(\operatorname{supp}h_\gamma,C)\ge(r-R_0)_+$. Cutting the modular-time integral at $T=\mu_{LR}(r-R_0)_+/(\pi+\beta v)$, where light-cone growth $e^{v\beta T}$ meets the kernel decay $e^{-\pi T}$:
$$
\mathfrak q_{AB}\le C_0(1+\beta g_{A|A^c})\,e^{-\lambda_\beta(r-R_0)_+},\qquad \lambda_\beta=\frac{\pi\mu_{LR}}{\pi+\beta v}.
$$

**Step 6. Lemma IV.2 (short imaginary time; imported counting).** For $2\alpha\beta\mathfrak dJ<1$,
$$
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\le\exp\Big[\alpha\beta g-\frac{g}{2\mathfrak dJ}\log(1-2\alpha\beta\mathfrak dJ)\Big],\quad\text{and }\le e^{3\alpha\beta g}\text{ for }\alpha\le\alpha_0=\min\{1,\tfrac1{4\beta\mathfrak dJ}\}.
$$
*Uses:* bounded degree, through the nested-commutator count $\|\mathrm{ad}^n_{H_0}(h_\gamma)\|\le n!(2\mathfrak dJ)^n\|h_\gamma\|$ "of [Chen–Rouzé, Lemma IX.3 and its proof]", which sums to $\|e^{-uH_0}Ve^{uH_0}\|\le g/(1-2\mathfrak dJ|u|)$; then Grönwall for $Q(u)=e^{-u(H_0+V)}e^{uH_0}$, and $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})=(Z_0/Z)^\alpha\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\,Q(\alpha\beta)]$. **This is the only place the prefactor $e^{O(g)}$ enters**, and $g$ counts only cut terms, which is why the boundary replaces the volume.

**Assembly.** Lemma III.1 + Cor. III.5 + Lemma IV.1 + (57), at $\alpha=\alpha_0$:
$$
I\le\Big(\frac1{\alpha_0}+3\Big)[C_0(1+\beta g)]^{\frac{2\alpha_0}{1+\alpha_0}}\exp\Big\{\frac{g}{1+\alpha_0}\Big[\alpha_0\beta-\frac{\log(1-2\alpha_0\beta\mathfrak dJ)}{2\mathfrak dJ}\Big]-\frac{2\alpha_0\lambda_\beta}{1+\alpha_0}(r-R_0)_+\Big\},
$$
decay length $\xi_\beta=\frac{1+\alpha_0}{2\alpha_0}\cdot\frac{\pi+\beta v}{\pi\mu_{LR}}$; $g\le\mathfrak dJ|A|$ always, $g\le C_{D,R_0}\mathfrak dJ|\partial_eA|$ on $\mathbb Z^D$.

## 5. Transferable moves visible in the final paper

*Interpretation (own), from the paper only. The chat may show that these arose differently, or that others were tried and dropped; this list is a placeholder for the chat-based section, not a substitute for it.*

1. **Replace a dynamical certificate by a static comparison with a decoupled reference.** Chen–Rouzé recover $A$ by running a sampler; here one deletes the couplings across the cut and measures what that costs. The cost of decoupling is controlled by the cut, not by the region.
2. **Turn a conditional quantity into a monotonicity defect.** CMI $\le\delta_{AB}(\rho,\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_{BC})$: once the reference is a product, a three-region quantity becomes a data-processing loss for one pair of states.
3. **Make the defect variational, then guess a trial vector.** An exact infimum (Carlen–Vershynina) means any admissible vector bounds the defect; the vector is the conditional expectation applied to an *exact* formula for the optimiser in modular time.
4. **Use a modular-time Fourier representation so that locality can be applied.** $(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}$ is rewritten as an integral of the Connes cocycle $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$, which is a real-time propagator; Lieb–Robinson then applies inside the integral.
5. **Split a parameter integral into regimes with different crude bounds and optimise the split.** Small $s$: a Rényi negative moment; middle: the leakage; large $s$: Jensen. Then $a,b$ (or $\ell$) are tuned.
6. **Balance a growth rate against a kernel's decay with a cutoff.** $T$ is chosen where $e^{v\beta T}e^{-\mu r}=e^{-\pi T}$; the same move fixes $T$ in the power-law case.
7. **Count only what crosses the cut.** Every estimate is expressed through $V$ and $g_{A|A^c}$, so the bound inherits the cut's size.

## 7. Bridges that can be stated now

### 7.1 Derivation: the variational bound holds for any subalgebra, so it is a quantitative sufficiency theorem

**Claim.** Lemmas III.2–III.4 and Corollary III.5 hold verbatim when the tensor factor $B(\mathcal H_R)\otimes\mathbf 1_E$ is replaced by any unital *-subalgebra $N$ of a finite-dimensional matrix algebra, with $\mathsf E_N$ its trace-preserving conditional expectation, $\rho_N=\mathsf E_N(\rho)$, $\sigma_N=\mathsf E_N(\sigma)$, $\delta_N=D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\rho_N\|\sigma_N)$ and $\mathfrak q_N=\frac12\int\|u_t-\mathsf E_N(u_t)\|_\infty|\sinh\pi t|^{-1}dt$, $u_t=\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$:
$$
\delta_N(\rho,\sigma)\le\Big(\frac1\alpha+3\Big)\big[\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\big]^{1/(1+\alpha)}\mathfrak q_N^{\,2\alpha/(1+\alpha)} .
$$
**Proof sketch.** Put $W_N(Y)=Y\rho_N^{-1/2}\rho^{1/2}$ for $Y\in N$. Isometry: $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\rho_N^{-1/2}Y^*Y\rho_N^{-1/2})=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathsf E_N(\rho)\rho_N^{-1/2}Y^*Y\rho_N^{-1/2})=\mathrm{Tr}(Y^*Y)$, because $\mathsf E_N$ is trace-preserving and $N$-bimodular. $W_N^\dagger(Z)=\mathsf E_N(Z\rho^{1/2})\rho_N^{-1/2}$, and $W_N^\dagger\Delta W_N(Y)=\mathsf E_N(\sigma Y\rho_N^{-1/2})\rho_N^{-1/2}=\sigma_NY\rho_N^{-1}$ by bimodularity. For $O\in N$, $O\rho^{1/2}=W_N(O\rho_N^{1/2})$, so $y_s=\mathsf E_N(B_s)\rho^{1/2}\in\operatorname{Ran}W_N$. The rest (operator-norm contractivity of $\mathsf E_N$, (35), the Jensen step with mean $\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma_N=1$) never uses a tensor structure.
**Check.** `check_2609_subalgebra_identities.py` (this folder), for $N=\bigoplus_iM_{k_i}\otimes\mathbf 1_{m_i}$ with block patterns $\{(2,2),(1,3)\}$, $\{(3,2)\}$, $\{(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)\}$ and random faithful $\rho,\sigma$: isometry error $\le5\cdot10^{-15}$; $\|W^\dagger\Delta W-\Delta_N\|\le6\cdot10^{-14}$; $\int j=\delta_N$ to 5 digits; $\max_s j(s)/[(1+s)\mathfrak q_N^2/s^2]\le0.36$ over $s\in[10^{-3},10^3]$. `check_2609_subalgebra_bound.py` checks the corollary itself on 12 random instances and 2 near-sufficient ones; no violation.

**Why it matters (Interpretation).** Petz's theorem, in the form Note 1 §6 quotes from Jenčová–Petz, says a subalgebra is sufficient for a family iff the Connes cocycles lie in it. Here $u_t=\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$ is the finite-dimensional Connes cocycle $(D\sigma:D\rho)_t$, and $\eta_N(t)$ is its distance from $N$. The corollary is therefore a quantitative, one-directional Petz theorem: *cocycle almost in $N$ ⇒ sufficiency almost holds*, with an explicit exponent $2\alpha/(1+\alpha)$ and a Rényi size factor. It is stated for an abstract pair $N\subset M$, so it passes the drag test (rule C2): it applies as it stands to the face subalgebra $D_0\subset D$ of Note 1, to a finite stage of a Bratteli diagram, or to a patch algebra of a tiling.

### 7.2 Testing the user's intuition: where the time-averaged Lindbladian went

- **Source** (Chen–Rouzé 2504.02208, Fig. 2 caption, from Exa's extract of the PDF): "In fact, the recovery map is a time-averaged detailed-balanced Lindbladian based on single-Pauli jumps on A." Conclusion: "Remarkably, this static property is proven using the dynamics … However, the bound on CMI grows exponentially with the size |A|, which comes from the possibility of an exponentially long mixing time." And: "If we further assume that the local gap of the Lindbladians with jumps on region A decays polynomially in the |A|, we may improve the CMI bound."
- **Source** (2609.38007, abstract and §VII): "Our proof is static, in contrast to the dynamical approach of Chen and Rouzé"; the results hold "without assuming clustering or rapid mixing".
- **Interpretation (own).** The static proof does not do away with the time average; it moves it. The average over Lindbladian *real* time becomes the average of $\eta_R(t)$ over *modular* time against $\frac1{2|\sinh\pi t|}$. KMS detailed balance, which the sampler is built to satisfy, becomes the relative modular operator $\Delta=L_\sigma R_{\rho^{-1}}$ itself, whose quadratic form $\langle X,\Delta X\rangle=\|\sigma^{1/2}X\rho^{-1/2}\|_2^2$ is the KMS-type pairing. The single-Pauli jumps on $A$ have no analogue: the static route needs only the conditional expectation onto the retained algebra. In the programme's terms, the canonical time of a KMS state is its modular flow (Note 1 §5.2: for $\mathbb P_\beta$ it is weighted layer depth), and the discrepancy between a state and $\mathbb P_\beta$ is the Connes cocycle $[D\varphi:D\mathbb P_\beta]_t$. So the user's sense that this phrase hides the analogue is supported in this form: **the programme's analogue of "time-averaged detailed-balanced Lindbladian on $A$" is the $1/\sinh(\pi t)$-weighted modular-time average of the Connes cocycle's leakage out of the face subalgebra**, and §7.1 shows it controls the sufficiency defect.
- **Known link, from memory and not opened this session:** the rotated Petz recovery maps of Junge–Renner–Sutter–Wilde–Winter also average over modular time, with density $\frac\pi2(\cosh\pi t+1)^{-1}$. Read that paper before relying on this.

### 7.3 Derivation: the boundary gain is isoperimetric, and expansion removes it

- Theorem II.1 is stated for a general site set $\Gamma$ with a metric; its constants depend on $J,R_0,\mathfrak d,\beta$ and on the finite-range Lieb–Robinson constants of (52). On a bounded-degree interaction graph with its graph metric the latter exist by path counting (own remark: paths of length $n$ number at most $\mathfrak d^n$, absorbed into $v$), so the theorem applies there, expanders included. The prefactor's exponent is the weighted **edge boundary** $g_{A|A^c}$, the numerator of a Cheeger ratio.
- If the interaction graph has edge expansion $h$ (so $|\partial_eA|\ge h|A|$ for $|A|\le n/2$), then boundary and volume are comparable, and the improvement over $e^{O(|A|)}$ is at most a constant in the exponent. The gain is largest on amenable (Følner) geometries such as $\mathbb Z^D$, where $|\partial_eA|/|A|\to0$ along large boxes.
- On a bounded-degree expander, $r\le\operatorname{diam}=O(\log n)$, so the bound is at best $C_\beta e^{C_\beta g}\,n^{-c}$: polynomial decay in system size, not decay in a free parameter.
- The §VII coarse-graining corollary needs $|\partial_eA_s|\lesssim s$ together with $d(A_s,C_s)\gtrsim s$. That holds in $D=2$ (the paper's own remark) and fails in $D\ge3$ (boundary $\sim s^{D-1}$) and on expanders.
- **Interpretation.** The expander-theory sense ("large cuts force mixing") and this paper's sense ("small cuts make decoupling cheap") use **the same cut functional in opposite directions**. The user's intuition fits the dynamical lineage (spectral gaps, rapid mixing: Chen–Rouzé's local-gap remark, Bakshi–Liu–Moitra–Tang's Dobrushin condition). For this paper it fits only through the shared currency, the cut; the mechanism here is isoperimetric and locality-based (Lieb–Robinson), not spectral. Both fit the programme's §4 pattern "local certificate → global bound": the certificate here is $(g_{A|A^c}$, Lieb–Robinson velocity $v)$, and there is no gap.
- **Known link:** amenability is the regime in which the programme's tiling reductions work (Shubin's trace per unit volume, unique ergodicity, local-to-global-unlocks §3), and, from memory, Connes's classification of injective factors ties amenability to injectivity (hyperfiniteness) on the operator-algebra side. The boundary-controlled prefactor is the Gibbs-state instance of the Følner principle, "boundary/volume → 0 makes the local representative".

### 7.4 Conjecture: a quantitative U3 (sufficiency certified on squares)

*Conjecture, commutative case first, for the abstract objects of Note 1.* Let $\rho=\mathbb P^F_\beta$ and $\sigma=\mathbb P^{\delta U}_\beta$ on complete histories with the same origin weights, and $N=D_0$ (face functions at the endpoint). Then $u_t=(\sigma/\rho)^{it}=e^{i\beta t(F-\delta U)(\mu)}\cdot e^{it\,c(s(\mu))}$, where the second factor depends only on the origin face (the ratio of the per-origin normalisations $Z_{\sigma_0}$ of Note 1 §5.2). Take $N$ generated by origin and endpoint faces (the "pair" statistic of Note 1 §6), or fix the origin; the trace-preserving $\mathsf E_N$ then averages uniformly over histories with the same origin and endpoint, and $\operatorname{osc}(\cdot\,|\,\text{endpoint})$ below is read on those fibres. So $\eta_N(t)\le\beta|t|\operatorname{osc}(F-\delta U\,|\,\text{endpoint})$ and $\mathfrak q_N\le\frac\beta4\operatorname{osc}(F-\delta U\,|\,\text{endpoint})$, using $\int|t|/|\sinh\pi t|\,dt=\frac12$. With §7.1, the sufficiency defect of the face is at most $(\frac1\alpha+3)e^{\alpha D_{1+\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)/(1+\alpha)}\,(\frac\beta4\operatorname{osc})^{2\alpha/(1+\alpha)}$. If the square complex of the layered quiver is an $h$-coboundary expander (U3), choose $U$ with $\operatorname{dist}(F,\delta U)\le\|\delta F\|/h$. The defect is then controlled by **alternating sums of $F$ around squares**, four arrows at a time. *What must be true:* that the coboundary-expansion distance controls the oscillation on fibres (it controls a norm, and the norm must be the sup norm along complete histories, so a path-length factor will appear); and that $D_{1+\alpha}$ stays bounded, which, by the analogue of Lemma IV.2, needs the "cut" (the non-coboundary part of $F$) to be small in total, not only locally. *Guard:* the history algebra's limit may have no trace, and then $\mathsf E_N$ must be replaced by the state-preserving generalised conditional expectation, for which §7.1 has not been checked.

### 7.5 Guards (rule P3.4)

- The comparison step (Lemma III.1) needs a reference state that is a **product** across the cut. Its programme analogue needs a decomposition of the cocycle $F=F_{\text{kept}}+F_{\text{cut}}$ under which the kept part makes $N$ sufficient. That is a hypothesis, not a theorem.
- Steps 5–6 use **real-time locality** (Lieb–Robinson) and **bounded interaction degree**. The programme has no carrier metric (rule P1.4); a light cone would have to be defined from the quiver's own path length, and the arrows are not reversible (local-to-global-unlocks §6, "Acyclicity").
- Everything here is finite-dimensional, with faithful states.
