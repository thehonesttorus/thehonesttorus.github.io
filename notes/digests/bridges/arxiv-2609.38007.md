## 1. The paper in one paragraph (Source)

Tai Hsuan Yang (UIUC), dated 28 Sep 2026, arXiv v1 29 Sep 2026 [quant-ph]. For Gibbs states $\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}=e^{-\beta H_\Lambda}/Z_\Lambda$ on a full partition $\Lambda=A\sqcup B\sqcup C$ with $r=d(A,C)$, it bounds the conditional mutual information. Finite range, bounded degree: $I(A{:}C|B)\le C_\beta\exp(C_\beta g_{A|A^c}-c_\beta r)$, where $g_{A|A^c}$ is the total norm of interaction terms crossing the cut $A|A^c$; on $\mathbb Z^D$ this is $C_\beta e^{C_\beta|\partial_eA|-c_\beta r}$. "Our main quantitative improvement is to replace the exponential volume dependence by exponential boundary dependence." Stretched-exponential aggregate decay (any body size, $D\ge2$): $C_\beta\exp[C_\beta(1+|\partial_eA|)^{\theta/D}-c_\beta(1+r)^{\theta/(D+1-\theta)}]$. Power law $\zeta>D$ (any body size): inverse-logarithmic decay for $D<\zeta\le2D$ and algebraic decay for $\zeta>2D$, with polynomial $|A|$-dependence and no $|C|$ or volume dependence. Method: "Our approach is static: we compare the Gibbs state with the reference state obtained by removing interactions across the cut $A\,|\,BC$. The reference state is a product across this cut, and the CMI is bounded by the decrease in relative entropy between the two states when $C$ is traced out. A variational bound based on a resolvent formula reduces the estimate of this loss to spatial localization, controlled by Lieb–Robinson bounds [17, 18], and spectral estimates for the relative modular operator."

## 2. Setting and notation (Source, [Y] §II and App. C)

- $\Gamma$ is a set of sites with a distance $d$, and each site carries a finite-dimensional $\mathcal H_x$. On $\mathbb Z^D$, $d(x,y)=|x-y|_1$. A finite volume $\Lambda\Subset\Gamma$ has $\mathcal H_\Lambda=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal H_x$. Write $XY=X\cup Y$ for disjoint regions, $X^c=\Lambda\setminus X$ (complements taken *in $\Lambda$*), and $d(X,Y)=\min_{x\in X,y\in Y}d(x,y)$.
- Unnormalised trace, natural logarithms, operator norm $\|\cdot\|_\infty$, trace norm $\|\cdot\|_1$, Hilbert–Schmidt inner product $\langle X,Y\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(X^\dagger Y)$.
- $D(\rho\|\sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)$. The letter $D$ also denotes the spatial dimension; the paper flags the clash.
- $n=|\Lambda|$. "The positive constants $C_\beta,c_\beta$ may depend on temperature and fixed interaction parameters, but not on the regions or total volume", and they "may be enlarged or decreased from one occurrence to the next".
- $L_X(Y)=XY$, $R_X(Y)=YX$ are left and right multiplication on Hilbert–Schmidt space.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Conditional mutual information, recovery, and three Markov properties

- **CMI** (Source, [Y] (1.1)): $I(A{:}C|B)_\rho=S(\rho_{AB})+S(\rho_{BC})-S(\rho_B)-S(\rho_{ABC})$, with $S(\omega)=-\mathrm{Tr}\,\omega\log\omega$.
- **Exact recovery** (Source, [Y] §I): "Vanishing CMI is equivalent to exact recovery of $A$ from $B$: there is a completely positive trace-preserving map $\mathcal R_{B\to AB}$ with $\rho_{ABC}=(\mathcal R_{B\to AB}\otimes\mathrm{id}_C)(\rho_{BC})$ [3]" (Hayden–Jozsa–Petz–Winter). "The Fawzi–Renner theorem makes this interpretation quantitative, bounding the recovery error in terms of the CMI [4]."
- **Markov notions** (Source, [CR] Fig. 1 and §I.A): the *pairwise* property has $|A|,|C|=O(1)$, the *local* property has $\min(|A|,|C|)=O(1)$, the *global* property allows $|A|,|C|=O(|\Lambda|)$. Classically, for positive distributions, the three "are equivalent … In the quantum case, these three are not known to be equal, even allowing for approximations." The conjecture attributed to [KKB20] in [CR]: $I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_\beta}\le\mathcal D(\mathrm{dist}(A,C))$, with $\mathcal D$ decaying superpolynomially.
- **Which formulation [Y] uses.** Only the CMI. No recovery map is constructed. [Y] §II says: "The quantitative statements below imply this property and, through the recovery theorem, the existence of recovery maps supported on the shielding neighborhood of A." The antecedent of "this property" seems to have been lost in the PDF extraction. *Mine:* recovery then follows from Fawzi–Renner applied with $B$ taken as the shielding neighbourhood. [CR], by contrast, constructs an explicit recovery map (§8).

### 3.2 Gibbs states and the three interaction classes (Source, [Y] (2.2), (2.5), (2.8), (2.10))

$H_\Lambda=\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}\Phi(X)$ with $\Phi(X)=\Phi(X)^\dagger$ supported on $X$, and $\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}=e^{-\beta H_\Lambda}/Z_\Lambda$, $\beta>0$. "All Gibbs states and their marginals are faithful in finite volume."

1. **Finite range, bounded degree** (Thm II.1). $H_\Lambda=\sum_\gamma h_\gamma$ with $\|h_\gamma\|_\infty\le J$ and $\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{supp}\,h_\gamma)\le R_0$; "each term overlaps at most $d$ terms, counting itself"; $J,R_0,d$ are uniform in $\Lambda$. $\Gamma$ is a general metric site set.
2. **Aggregate stretched-exponential decay, unrestricted body size** (Thm II.2). $\Gamma=\mathbb Z^D$, $D\ge2$, uniformly bounded on-site dimension, $\mu>0$, $0<\theta\le1$, and
$$J_{\mu,\theta}:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb Z^D}e^{\mu d(x,y)^\theta}\sum_{X\Subset\mathbb Z^D,\ x,y\in X}\|\Phi(X)\|_\infty<\infty .$$
"The supremum includes $x=y$." "No uniform bound on $|X|$ or on the number of terms meeting a site is assumed."
3. **Aggregate power law, unrestricted body size** (Thm II.3). $J_\zeta:=\sup_{x,y}(1+d(x,y))^\zeta\sum_{X\ni x,y}\|\Phi(X)\|_\infty<\infty$ with $\zeta>D$, the supremum again including $x=y$.

### 3.3 Full partition, cut interaction, cut strength, cut Gibbs pair (Source, [Y] (2.3)–(2.4), Lemma III.1)

- **Full partition**: $\Lambda=A\sqcup B\sqcup C$ with $A,C\neq\emptyset$, so that $B$ contains every site of $\Lambda$ outside $A\cup C$. "This hypothesis is essential to the cut-Hamiltonian argument; a marginal with an arbitrary unobserved exterior is not silently identified with a local Gibbs state."
- **Cut interaction and cut strength**:
$$V=\sum_{X\cap A\ne\emptyset,\ X\cap A^c\ne\emptyset}\Phi(X),\qquad g_{A|A^c}=\sum_{X\cap A\ne\emptyset,\ X\cap A^c\ne\emptyset}\|\Phi(X)\|_\infty,\qquad \|V\|_\infty\le g_{A|A^c}.$$
- **Cut Gibbs pair**: $H_\Lambda=H_0+V$, $H_0=H_A+H_{BC}$ with $H_R=\sum_{X\subseteq R}\Phi(X)$, $\gamma_R=e^{-\beta H_R}/\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-\beta H_R}$ and $\gamma_B=\mathrm{Tr}_C\gamma_{BC}$. Then
$$\sigma=e^{-\beta H_0}/Z_0=\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_{BC},\qquad \rho=e^{-\beta(H_0+V)}/Z .$$

### 3.4 Entropy loss and the modular objects (Source, [Y] (3.1), (3.7), (3.9)–(3.13))

For faithful $\rho,\sigma$ on $R\otimes E$:
- **entropy loss** $\delta_R(\rho,\sigma)=D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\rho_R\|\sigma_R)$, the relative entropy lost when $E$ is traced out;
- **relative modular operator** $\Delta=L_\sigma R_{\rho^{-1}}$, $\Delta(Y)=\sigma Y\rho^{-1}$, strictly positive on Hilbert–Schmidt space since $\langle X,\Delta X\rangle=\|\sigma^{1/2}X\rho^{-1/2}\|_2^2>0$; $K=\log\Delta=L_{\log\sigma}-R_{\log\rho}$; reduced $\Delta_R=L_{\sigma_R}R_{\rho_R^{-1}}$;
- **resolvent gap** $j(s)=\langle\rho^{1/2},(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\rangle-\langle\rho_R^{1/2},(\Delta_R+s)^{-1}\rho_R^{1/2}\rangle$;
- **isometry** $W_R(Y)=(Y\rho_R^{-1/2}\otimes 1_E)\rho^{1/2}$, with $W_R^\dagger(Z)=\mathrm{Tr}_E(Z\rho^{1/2})\rho_R^{-1/2}$;
- **tracial conditional expectation** $E_R(O)=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_EO}{\dim\mathcal H_E}\otimes 1_E$;
- **modular leakage** $\eta_R(t)=\|\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}-E_R(\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it})\|_\infty$;
- **integrated modular leakage**
$$q_R=\tfrac12\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\eta_R(t)}{|\sinh(\pi t)|}\,dt ,$$
finite because $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$ equals $1$ at $t=0$ and is differentiable there, and $\eta_R\le2$.

**Mine (identification, standard).** For density matrices, $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$ is the Connes cocycle $(D\sigma:D\rho)_t$, and $\Delta$ is Araki's relative modular operator realised on Hilbert–Schmidt space. So $\eta_R(t)$ is the operator-norm distance of the Connes cocycle from the subalgebra $B(\mathcal H_R)\otimes1$. [Y] uses neither name; it cites Carlen–Vershynina for the variational identity. §12, bridge B7, takes this up.

### 3.5 Support-weighted norms and the modular action map (Source, [Y] (5.8), (6.10), (6.19))

- **Decomposition norm**: $\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}=\inf_{O=\sum_XO_X}\sum_Xe^{s|X|^{\theta/D}}\|O_X\|_\infty$ for $0\le s\le a_{\rm supp}$. It is submultiplicative, by concavity $|X\cup Y|^{\theta/D}\le|X|^{\theta/D}+|Y|^{\theta/D}$, and it dominates $\|\cdot\|_\infty$.
- **Coefficient tail**: $T_O(N)=\sum_{|X|>N}\|O_X\|_\infty$ for a fixed local decomposition of $O$.
- **Modular action map**: $\mathcal D(O)=\beta[O,H_0]+\beta OV+(\log Z-\log Z_0)O$. By (5.16), $K(O\rho^{1/2})=\mathcal D(O)\rho^{1/2}$ and $K^j\rho^{1/2}=\mathcal D^j(1)\rho^{1/2}$. So powers of the relative modular logarithm acting on $\rho^{1/2}$ are *operators built by commutators with $H_0$ and multiplication by $V$*. This is the hinge between the modular spectral theory and locality.

### 3.6 Gibbs-interpolation objects (Source, [Y] (5.37), (5.39))

- **Logarithmic Markov defect** $K_M(\omega)=\log\omega-\log\omega_{AB}-\log\omega_{BC}+\log\omega_B$, an operator on the physical space (distinct from the superoperator $K$). It satisfies $\frac{d}{ds}I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_s}=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_s'K_M(\rho_s)]$.
- **BKM inner product** $\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathrm{BKM},\rho}=\int_0^1\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^uX\rho^{1-u}Y)\,du\le$ (on the diagonal) $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho X^2)$.

## 4. Main theorems (Source, transcribed from [Y] §II)

**Theorem II.1 (finite-range interactions).** Under the finite-range hypotheses of §3.2, for every fixed $\beta>0$ there exist $C_\beta,c_\beta>0$, depending only on $J,R_0,d,\beta$, such that
$$I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\le C_\beta\exp\big(C_\beta g_{A|A^c}-c_\beta r\big).\tag{2.6}$$
On $\mathbb Z^D$ with the nearest-neighbour graph metric, let $\partial_eA$ be the set of unoriented nearest-neighbour edges with exactly one endpoint in $A$. Then $g_{A|A^c}\le C|\partial_eA|$, with $C$ depending only on the local interaction parameters, and therefore
$$I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\le C_\beta e^{C_\beta|\partial_eA|-c_\beta r}.\tag{2.7}$$
Explicit decay length before absorptions (4.23): $\xi_\beta=\frac{1+\alpha_0}{2\alpha_0}\cdot\frac{\pi+\beta v}{\pi\mu_{LR}}$ with $\alpha_0=\min\{1,1/(4\beta dJ)\}$. *Mine:* at large $\beta$ this is $\xi_\beta\approx2\beta dJ\cdot\beta v/(\pi\mu_{LR})=O(\beta^2)$.

**Theorem II.2 (stretched-exponential interactions of unrestricted body size).** Under (2.8) with $D\ge2$, for every fixed $\beta>0$ there are $C_\beta,c_\beta>0$, depending only on $\beta,D,\mu,\theta,J_{\mu,\theta}$ (and, for $C_\beta$, on the on-site dimension bound), such that
$$I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\le C_\beta\exp\Big[C_\beta(1+|\partial_eA|)^{\theta/D}-c_\beta(1+r)^{\theta/(D+1-\theta)}\Big].\tag{2.9}$$
"The boundary $\partial_eA$ is taken in the ambient lattice $\mathbb Z^D$, including edges leaving $\Lambda$. The estimate is uniform over all finite $\Lambda$ and all full partitions." Exponential interactions are the case $\theta=1$.

**Theorem II.3 (power-law interactions of unrestricted body size).** Under (2.10) with $\zeta>D$, put $b_\zeta=1+\frac{2\zeta}D$ and $\nu_\zeta=\frac{b_\zeta(\zeta-2D)}{\zeta-D+b_\zeta}$. Then:
- for $D<\zeta<2D$: $\;I\le C_\beta(1+|A|)^{b_\zeta+1}[\log(e+r)]^{-b_\zeta}$ (2.12);
- for $\zeta=2D$: $\;I\le C_\beta(1+|A|)^6[\log(e+r)]^{-5}[\log(e+\log(e+r))]^2$ (2.13);
- for $\zeta>2D$: $\;I\le C_\beta(1+|A|)^{b_\zeta+1}(1+r)^{-\nu_\zeta}$ (2.14).

"The constants depend only on $\beta, D, J_\zeta, \zeta$ and the fixed on-site dimension bound, not on $\Lambda, A, B, C$ or $r$."

**Consequences stated in [Y] §VII (Source).**
- *Proportional rescaling in 2D.* If $|\partial_eA_s|\le ps$ and $d(A_s,C_s)\ge qs$, then $I\le C_\beta\exp[-(c_\beta q-C_\beta p)s]$ (7.2). The CMI therefore vanishes exponentially once the buffer is wide enough relative to the boundary. For $\theta=1$ both exponents scale as $s^{1/2}$. "This suppression under geometric rescaling has a renormalization-group interpretation and may find applications in approximate entanglement bootstrap for mixed quantum states in two dimensions [21, 22]."
- *Polynomial-size trade-off.* For every $p>0$, under II.1 or II.2, $I\le C_{\beta,p}(1+|A|)^{q_p}(1+r)^{-p}$ (7.3). In the finite-range case take $\alpha=\alpha_0/(1+\beta g)$ in Cor. III.5; this gives a bound $\propto(1+\beta g)\exp[-c_\beta r/(1+\beta g)]$.

**Table I of [Y] (Source; the bounds it attributes to prior work, all for full partitions).** Brown–Poulin, commuting: $0$. Kato–Brandão, 1D: $C_\beta e^{-c_\beta\sqrt r}$. Kuwahara, $D>1$: $C_\beta e^{C_\beta(|A|+|C|)}e^{-c_\beta r/\log(2+r)}$; 1D: $C_\beta e^{-c_\beta r}$. Kato–Kuwahara: $\mathrm{poly}(n)e^{-c_\beta r^{1/(D+3)}}$ under rapid mixing of the Gibbs samplers, and $\mathrm{poly}(n)e^{-c_\beta r^{1/D}}$ under uniform clustering. ## 5. Proof architecture, lemma by lemma (Source, with my glosses marked)

### 5.0 Roadmap (Mine, assembled from [Y] §I and §III–VI)

$$I(A{:}C|B)_\rho\ \overset{\text{III.1}}{\le}\ \delta_{AB}(\rho,\sigma)\ \overset{\text{III.2–III.4}}{\le}\ \underbrace{\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+e^{-\ell-K})\rho^{1/2}\rangle}_{\text{spectral tail of }K:\ \text{boundary}}+e^{-\ell}+\underbrace{q_{AB}^2(e^\ell+2\ell)}_{\text{leakage: distance}} .$$

The cut strength (boundary) controls the left term. Lieb–Robinson localisation of the interaction-picture cocycle, integrated against $1/|\sinh\pi t|$, controls $q_{AB}$ (distance). In the finite-range case the spectral tail is handled by a negative modular moment instead (Cor. III.5). In the quasi-local cases, long interactions near $A$ are first truncated, at a CMI cost controlled by a Gibbs-interpolation lemma.

### 5.1 Lemma III.1 (cut-Hamiltonian comparison)

**Statement.** $\delta_{AB}(\rho,\sigma)=I(A{:}C|B)_\rho+D(\rho_{BC}\|\gamma_{BC})-D(\rho_B\|\gamma_B)$, hence $I(A{:}C|B)_\rho\le\delta_{AB}(\rho,\sigma)$. Moreover $|\log Z-\log Z_0|\le\beta\|V\|_\infty\le\beta g_{A|A^c}$.

**Proof.** Expand both relative entropies using $\sigma=\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_{BC}$ and collect the four entropies. The last difference is $\ge0$ by data processing under $\mathrm{Tr}_C$. The free-energy bound follows from $\frac{d}{ds}\log Z(s)=-\beta\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_sV)$. (*Mine:* I re-derived the identity by hand; it holds.)

**Where hypotheses enter.** The full partition makes $H_0=H_A+H_{BC}$ exact, so $\sigma$ is a product across $A|BC$. Nothing about locality is used yet.

### 5.2 Lemma III.2 (resolvent representation)

$\delta_R(\rho,\sigma)=\int_0^\infty j(s)\,ds$, absolutely convergent. **Proof** (App. B). $-\langle\rho^{1/2},\log\Delta\,\rho^{1/2}\rangle=D(\rho\|\sigma)$, the same holds on the reduced space, and $\int_0^\infty\big(\frac1{\lambda+s}-\frac1{1+s}\big)ds=-\log\lambda$ by spectral calculus.

### 5.3 Lemma III.3 (variational representation)

$W_R$ is an isometry with $W_R\rho_R^{1/2}=\rho^{1/2}$ and $W_R^\dagger\Delta W_R=\Delta_R$, and
$$j(s)=\inf_{y\in\mathrm{Ran}W_R}\big\|(\Delta+s)^{1/2}\big(y-(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\big)\big\|_2^2 ,$$
attained uniquely at $y=W_R(\Delta_R+s)^{-1}\rho_R^{1/2}$. So $j\ge0$, and *every trial vector in $\mathrm{Ran}W_R$ gives an upper bound*. This is "the variational form of [19, Lemma 2.1 and Eqs. (2.8)–(2.9)]" (Carlen–Vershynina), proved in App. B by expanding the square: (B7) gives trial value $=\|(\Delta_R+s)^{1/2}(Y-(\Delta_R+s)^{-1}\rho_R^{1/2})\|^2+j(s)$.

### 5.4 Lemma III.4 (variational localisation of the entropy loss): the key lemma

**Statement.** For every $\ell>0$,
$$\delta_R(\rho,\sigma)\le\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+e^{-\ell-K})\rho^{1/2}\rangle+e^{-\ell}+q_R^2(e^\ell+2\ell).\tag{3.14}$$
More generally, for $0<a<1<b$ and $0<\alpha\le1$,
$$\delta_R(\rho,\sigma)\le\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\,a^\alpha}{\alpha}+b^{-1}+q_R^2\big(a^{-1}+\log\tfrac ba\big).\tag{3.15}$$

**Proof architecture.**
1. *A Fourier identity for the resolvent* (3.16), derived in App. B.1 from $\int_0^\infty\frac{\sinh(at)}{\sinh(\pi t)}dt=\frac12\tan(a/2)$ (NIST DLMF 4.40.8), continued to $a=iv$:
$$\frac1{s+e^u}=\frac1{1+s}+\frac{i}{2s}\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{s^{-it}(e^{itu}-1)}{\sinh(\pi t)}dt .$$
2. Since $\Delta^{it}\rho^{1/2}=\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}\rho^{1/2}$, the resolvent applied to $\rho^{1/2}$ is a *left multiplication*: $(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}=B_s\rho^{1/2}$, with
$$B_s=\frac1{1+s}+\frac i{2s}\int\frac{s^{-it}(\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}-1)}{\sinh\pi t}dt .$$
3. *Trial vector* $y_s=E_R(B_s)\rho^{1/2}\in\mathrm{Ran}W_R$, because $E_R(B_s)$ is supported on $R$. Also $\|B_s-E_R(B_s)\|_\infty\le q_R/s$ (3.18): the scalar part is fixed by $E_R$, and the integral part is bounded by the leakage.
4. For any operator $O$, $\|(\Delta+s)^{1/2}(O\rho^{1/2})\|_2^2=\|\sigma^{1/2}O\|_2^2+s\|O\rho^{1/2}\|_2^2\le(1+s)\|O\|_\infty^2$ (3.19). Hence $0\le j(s)\le(1+s)q_R^2/s^2$ (3.20).
5. *Small resolvent*: positivity of the reduced resolvent gives $\int_0^aj\le\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+a\Delta^{-1})\rho^{1/2}\rangle$ (3.21). *Large resolvent*: $\langle\rho^{1/2},(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\rangle\le1/s$, and Jensen gives the reduced term $\ge1/(1+s)$ (using $\langle\rho_R^{1/2},\Delta_R\rho_R^{1/2}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}\sigma_R=1$), so $\int_b^\infty j\le b^{-1}$ (3.24).
6. Integrate (3.20) over $[a,b]$ and set $a=e^{-\ell}$, $b=e^\ell$ to get (3.14). For (3.15), use $\log(1+w)\le w^\alpha/\alpha$ and $\langle\rho^{1/2},\Delta^{-\alpha}\rho^{1/2}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})$.

**What the lemma says (Mine).** Relative entropy is lost under $\mathrm{Tr}_E$ only through two channels: (i) the Connes cocycle $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}$ leaving the algebra of $R$ at modular times $|t|\lesssim1$ (the $1/|\sinh\pi t|$ weight suppresses large modular times exponentially); (ii) spectral weight of $K=\log\Delta$ far out in the negative direction (large $\rho/\sigma$ ratios). If $q_R=0$ then $\delta_R=0$ ([Y]: "If $q_R=0$, (3.20) gives $\delta_R=0$"). This is the qualitative Petz sufficiency theorem, now with a quantitative remainder (bridge B7).

### 5.5 Corollary III.5 (optimised negative-moment bound)

For $0<\alpha\le1$:
$$\delta_R(\rho,\sigma)\le\Big(\frac1\alpha+3\Big)\big[\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\big]^{1/(1+\alpha)}q_R^{2\alpha/(1+\alpha)} .$$
For $0<q_R<1$, choose $a=(q_R^2/\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha}))^{1/(1+\alpha)}$ and $b=a^{-1}$ in (3.15). For $q_R\ge1$, use $\delta_R\le D(\rho\|\sigma)\le\frac1\alpha\log\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})$. Jensen gives $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\ge e^{\alpha D(\rho\|\sigma)}\ge1$.

### 5.6 Finite range (Section IV): proof of Theorem II.1

**Lemma IV.1 (finite-range integrated modular leakage).** There are $C_0,v,\mu_{LR}>0$, independent of $\Lambda,A,B,C$, with
$$q_{AB}\le C_0(1+\beta g_{A|A^c})\exp\big[-\lambda_\beta(r-R_0)_+\big],\qquad \lambda_\beta=\frac{\pi\mu_{LR}}{\pi+\beta v}.\tag{4.1}$$
- *Step 1 (crude bound).* Up to a phase, $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}=e^{-i\beta tH_0}e^{i\beta t(H_0+V)}$, an interaction-picture evolution generated by $V(u)=e^{-iuH_0}Ve^{iuH_0}$. So $\eta_{AB}(t)\le\min\{2,2\beta g|t|\}$, and with $\int|t|/|\sinh\pi t|=\tfrac12$, $q_{AB}\le\beta g/2$ (4.3).
- *Step 2 (spatial decay).* Let $W_{AB}(u)$ solve $W'=iE_{AB}(V(u))W$, $W(0)=1$; it is unitary and supported on $AB$. Duhamel's formula (Lemma A.1) gives $\eta_{AB}(t)\le2\int_0^{\beta|t|}\|V(\pm u)-E_{AB}(V(\pm u))\|_\infty du$ (4.7). The finite-range Lieb–Robinson bound with local approximation ([17] = Nachtergaele–Sims–Young, Eq. (3.26), Cor. 4.4) gives $\|e^{-iuH_0}h_\gamma e^{iuH_0}-E_{AB}(\cdot)\|\le C_{LR}\|h_\gamma\|e^{v|u|-\mu_{LR}d(\mathrm{supp}\,h_\gamma,C)}$ (4.8). Every cut term meets $A$ and has diameter $\le R_0$, so $d\ge(r-R_0)_+$, and $\eta_{AB}(t)\le\min\{2,C\beta g|t|e^{v\beta|t|-\mu_{LR}(r-R_0)_+}\}$ (4.9).
- *Step 3 (modular-time cutoff).* Split at $T=\mu_{LR}(r-R_0)_+/(\pi+\beta v)$, using $\int_{|t|>T}dt/|\sinh\pi t|\le Ce^{-\pi T}$. The choice makes $v\beta T-\mu_{LR}(r-R_0)_+=-\pi T$, so both pieces are $\le C(1+\beta g)e^{-\pi T}$.

**The key estimate (Mine, rephrasing Step 3).** Lieb–Robinson growth $e^{v\beta|t|}$ in modular time is beaten by the KMS-strip kernel $1/|\sinh\pi t|\sim e^{-\pi|t|}$. Balancing them sets the effective modular time $T\propto r$ and yields the rate $\pi\mu_{LR}/(\pi+\beta v)$. Classically, with $[H_0,V]=0$, the cocycle is $e^{i\beta tV}$ (up to phase), which is supported on $\mathrm{supp}V$; then $\eta_{AB}\equiv0$ as soon as $V$ does not touch $C$, and $\delta=0$. This is the exact Gibbs⇒Markov direction of Hammersley–Clifford, recovered as "the cocycle does not leak".

**Lemma IV.2 (short-imaginary-time moment).** For $2\alpha\beta dJ<1$:
$$\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\le\exp\Big[\alpha\beta g-\frac{g}{2dJ}\log(1-2\alpha\beta dJ)\Big],$$
and for $\alpha\le\alpha_0=\min\{1,1/(4\beta dJ)\}$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})\le e^{3\alpha\beta g}$.
- The proof uses "the nested-commutator counting argument of [1, Lemma IX.3 and its proof]" ([CR]): $\|\mathrm{ad}_{H_0}^n(h_\gamma)\|\le n!(2dJ)^n\|h_\gamma\|$, since after $m$ commutators the support is a union of $\le m+1$ supports and at most $d(m+1)$ terms can contribute next.
- Summing gives $\|e^{-uH_0}Ve^{uH_0}\|\le g/(1-2dJ|u|)$ (4.17).
- $Q(u)=e^{-u(H_0+V)}e^{uH_0}$ satisfies $Q'=-Q\,e^{-uH_0}Ve^{uH_0}$, and Grönwall (Lemma A.2) gives $\|Q(u)\|\le(1-2dJu)^{-g/(2dJ)}$.
- Finally $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{1+\alpha}\sigma^{-\alpha})=(Z_0/Z)^\alpha\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\,Q(\alpha\beta)]$ together with (3.5).

*Where bounded degree enters:* chiefly here, to make the imaginary-time expansion of the **cut** perturbation converge. (It also enters the Lieb–Robinson constants of Lemma IV.1, which "depend only on the fixed interaction parameters", and the cut bounds $g\le dJ|A|$ and $g\le C_{D,R_0}dJ|\partial_eA|$; see §5.9.) Because only $V$ is conjugated, the price is $e^{O(g)}$, a boundary quantity, not a volume quantity.

**Assembly** (4.22). Combining III.1, III.5, IV.1 and IV.2:
$$I\le\Big(\frac1\alpha+3\Big)\big[C_0(1+\beta g)\big]^{\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}\exp\Big\{\frac g{1+\alpha}\Big[\alpha\beta-\frac{\log(1-2\alpha\beta dJ)}{2dJ}\Big]-\frac{2\alpha\lambda_\beta}{1+\alpha}(r-R_0)_+\Big\}.$$
Take $\alpha=\alpha_0$. For any $A$, $g\le dJ|A|$. On $\mathbb Z^D$, every cut term contains an $A$-site within $R_0$ of a boundary edge, so $g\le C_{D,R_0}dJ|\partial_eA|$.

### 5.7 Stretched-exponential interactions (Section V): proof of Theorem II.2

"Two additional difficulties arise: individual interactions can span the shielding region, and their supports can contain arbitrarily many sites."

- **Lemma V.1 (support cardinalities from spatial decay).** There is $a_{\rm supp}\le\min\{1,\mu/(4\cdot6^\theta)\}$ with $J_{2a_{\rm supp}}:=\sup_x\sum_{X\ni x}e^{2a_{\rm supp}|X|^{\theta/D}}\|\Phi(X)\|\le CJ_{\mu,\theta}$ and $\sum_{X\text{ crossing}}e^{a_{\rm supp}|X|^{\theta/D}}\|\Phi(X)\|\le C|\partial_eA|$. The ingredients are $|X|^{\theta/D}\le3^\theta R_X^\theta$ (lattice counting), Cauchy–Schwarz over supports, and the displacement count $|\{x\in A:x+z\notin A\}|\le|\partial_eA|\,|z|_1$ (5.6). *This is where the boundary, not the volume, appears in the quasi-local case.*
- **Lemma V.2 (commutator loss of support weight).** $\|[H_0,O]\|_{s',\mathrm{dec}}\le2J_{a_{\rm supp}}\big[\frac{D}{e\theta(s-s')}\big]^{D/\theta}\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}$. A commutator costs a factor $|X|$, which is absorbed by spending support weight via $\sup_uue^{-(s-s')u^{\theta/D}}$.
- **(5.16)–(5.17).** $K(O\rho^{1/2})=(\beta[O,H_0]+\beta OV+(\log Z-\log Z_0)O)\rho^{1/2}$, so each application of $K$ costs $2\beta J_{a_{\rm supp}}[\ldots]^{D/\theta}+2\beta\|V\|_{a_{\rm supp},\mathrm{dec}}$.
- **Lemma V.3 (uniform modular moments and spectral tails).** $\|K^n\rho^{1/2}\|_2\le[C_\beta(n^{D/\theta}+|\partial_eA|)]^n$ (5.18), from $n$ equal steps of support weight $a_{\rm supp}/n$. Then $\langle\rho^{1/2},1_{\{|K|\ge\ell\}}\rho^{1/2}\rangle\le C_\beta\exp[C_\beta(1+|\partial_eA|)^{\theta/D}-c_\beta\ell^{\theta/D}]$ (5.19), from Chebyshev with $n\approx(\ell/4C_\beta)^{\theta/D}$.
- **Lemma V.4 (small-resolvent tail).** The same bound holds for $\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+e^{-\ell-K})\rho^{1/2}\rangle$, via $\log(1+e^{-\ell-u})\le e^{-\ell/2}+(\log2+|u|)1_{\{u<-\ell/2\}}$ and the tail-moment identity (5.25).
- **Lemma V.5 (CMI along a Gibbs interpolation).** For arbitrary Hermitian $H,W$ on $ABC$ with $d_A=\dim\mathcal H_A$: $|I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_1}-I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_0}|\le6\beta(1+\log d_A)\|W\|_\infty$, "no dependence on the Hilbert-space dimensions of $B$ or $C$".
  - *Proof:* the pinching inequality gives $\omega_{AY}\le d_A^2\tau$ with $\tau=1_A/d_A\otimes\omega_Y$; log-convexity in $\alpha$ and Hölder give the exponential moments (5.32) of the scalar variable $u_{ij}=\log p_i-\log q_j$; then $u^2\le\alpha^{-2}(e^{\alpha u}+e^{-\alpha u}-2)$ gives $\mathrm{Tr}(\omega R^2_{A|Y})\le(e-1)(1+2\log d_A)^2$ (5.36); then $\mathrm{Tr}[\omega K_M^2]\le4(e-1)(1+2\log d_A)^2$; then $\frac d{ds}I=-\beta\langle W-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_sW),K_M(\rho_s)\rangle_{\rm BKM}$ and Cauchy–Schwarz.
  - "A trace-distance continuity bound for conditional entropy introduces a binary-entropy term and therefore a small-error logarithm [20]. For states connected by a bounded Hamiltonian perturbation at fixed temperature, the following direct estimate avoids that loss. It holds for an arbitrary perturbation, not only a local one."
- **Lemma V.6 (leakage after local truncation).** Assume $J_0=\sup_x\sum_{X\ni x}\|\Phi(X)\|<\infty$, $J_1=\sup_x\sum_{X\ni x}|X|\|\Phi(X)\|<\infty$, $r\ge24$, $1\le L\le r/24$. Set $\Omega=\{x:d(x,A)\le\lfloor r/3\rfloor\}$ and delete every term touching $\Omega$ with diameter $>L$. If the weighted bound $\sup_x\sum_ye^{wd(x,y)}\sum_{X\ni x,y,X\subseteq\Omega,\mathrm{diam}X\le L}\|\Phi(X)\|\le J_*$ holds, then $q^{(L)}_{AB}\le C_\beta|A|^2(1+r)^De^{-c_\beta wr}$, with constants uniform in $w,L$.
  - *Proof:* split $H_0^{(L)}=H_{\rm in}+H_{\rm out}+W_\Omega$; the supports of $V^{(L)}$ and $W_\Omega$ are $\ge r/6$ apart; a Lieb–Robinson iteration for $H_{\rm in}$ only (5.53); an approximating unitary $\tilde U$ generated inside $\Omega$; Duhamel; then the same modular-time split. "Only commutators at the inner–outer interface are estimated. No locality assumption is imposed on $H_{\rm out}$."
- **Assembly (proof of II.2).**
  - Step 1: truncation costs $C_\beta(1+|A|)^2(1+r)^De^{-cL^\theta}$ (Lemma V.5 with (5.65)).
  - Step 2: (3.14) applied to the truncated pair.
  - Step 3: $w_L=(\mu/2)L^{\theta-1}$ gives $q^{(L)}\le C_\beta|A|^2(1+r)^D\exp[-c_\beta r/L^{1-\theta}]$; choose $\ell=c_0r/L^{1-\theta}$; the small-resolvent term is $C_\beta\exp[C_\beta(1+|\partial_eA|)^{\theta/D}-c_\beta(r/L^{1-\theta})^{\theta/D}]$.
  - Step 4: balance $L^\theta$ against $(r/L^{1-\theta})^{\theta/D}$ with $L=\lfloor r^{1/(D+1-\theta)}\rfloor$, which gives the exponent $r^{\theta/(D+1-\theta)}$. Polynomial factors in $|A|$ are absorbed using lattice isoperimetry $|A|\le C_D(1+|\partial_eA|)^{D/(D-1)}$ (this is where $D\ge2$ is used) and $\log d_A\le C|A|$. Small $r<r_0=\lceil24^{(D+1-\theta)/(D-\theta)}\rceil$ is handled by enlarging $C_\beta$.
- *Why not Cor. III.5 here* (Source): it "would also require volume-uniform control of the negative modular moment … The stretched-exponential spectral tail (5.19), with $\theta/D<1$, does not by itself provide such an exponential-moment bound."

### 5.8 Power-law interactions (Section VI): proof of Theorem II.3

- **Lemma VI.1 (interaction tails).** $\sup_x\sum_{X\ni x,\mathrm{diam}X>L}\|\Phi(X)\|\le CJ_\zeta(1+L)^{D-\zeta}$; $\sup_x\sum_{X\ni x,|X|>N}\|\Phi(X)\|\le CJ_\zeta(1+N)^{-\zeta/D}$ ("The factor $N^{-1}$ uses all the distant sites in a large support, rather than selecting just one"); $J_0\le J_\zeta$, $J_1<\infty$; $g\le J_0|A|$; and the crossing tail is $\le CJ_\zeta|A|(1+N)^{-\zeta/D}$. All bounds are uniform under deletions.
- **Lemma VI.2 (one-power loss under a commutator).** If $T_O(N)\le C_O(1+N)^{-s}$ with $s>1$, then $T_{[O,H_0]}(N)\le C(M_O+C_O)[(1+N)^{-(s-1)}+(1+N)^{-\zeta/D}]$, while $T_{OV}(N)\le C|A|[C_O(1+N)^{-s}+M_O(1+N)^{-\zeta/D}]$.
- **Lemma VI.3 (iterated modular action).** For $1\le j\le\lceil\zeta/D\rceil$: $\sum_{|X|>N}\|[\mathcal D^j(1)]_X\|\le C_{\beta,j}|A|^j(1+N)^{-(\zeta/D-j+1)}$ and $\sum_X\|[\mathcal D^j(1)]_X\|\le C_{\beta,j}|A|^j$. "Only the commutator lowers the tail exponent by one."
- **Lemma VI.4 (endpoint modular spectral tail).** With $m=\lceil\zeta/D\rceil$ and $s=\zeta/D-m+1\in(0,1]$, a fractional increment estimate $\|(e^{itK}-1)K^m\rho^{1/2}\|_2\le C_\beta|A|^{m+s}|t|^s$ (with a $\log$ at $s=1$) and time averaging over $[0,2/R]$ give
$$\langle\rho^{1/2},1_{\{|K|\ge R\}}\rho^{1/2}\rangle\le C_\beta|A|^{b_\zeta+1}R^{-2(1+\zeta/D)}\times\begin{cases}1&\zeta/D\notin\mathbb N\\ [\log(e+R)]^2&\zeta/D\in\mathbb N\end{cases}$$
and hence $\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+e^{-\ell-K})\rho^{1/2}\rangle\le C_\beta|A|^{b_\zeta+1}\ell^{-b_\zeta}(\ldots)$.
- **Lemma VI.5 (power-law leakage).** $\eta_{AB}(t)\le\min\{2,C\beta J_\zeta|t|e^{v\beta|t|}\sum_{a\in A,c\in C}(1+d(a,c))^{-\zeta}\}$ with $v=C_{D,\zeta}J_\zeta$, using the power-law LR bound [17, Thm 3.1] and the convolution property (6.43) of $F(r)=(1+r)^{-\zeta}$. This gives $q_{AB}\le C_\beta|A|(1+r)^{-\kappa_\beta}$ with $\kappa_\beta=\pi(\zeta-D)/(\pi+\beta v)$, the same modular-time balance as in §5.6, now with $T\propto\log(1+r)$.
- **Assembly.**
  - Case $D<\zeta\le2D$: $\ell=\kappa_\beta\log(1+r)$, which gives inverse-log decay.
  - Case $\zeta>2D$: truncate with $L\asymp r^\alpha$, $\alpha=(D+b_\zeta)/(\zeta-D+b_\zeta)$, and weight $w_L=\log(1+L)/L$ (here $\zeta>2D\Rightarrow\zeta>D+1$ makes the weighted sum finite). Choose $\ell=c_0r\log(1+L)/L$, and balance $b_\zeta(1-\alpha)=\alpha(\zeta-D)-D=\nu_\zeta$.
  - "The sufficient threshold $2D$ arises from the truncation error and the requirement that the retained interaction range be smaller than the separation."

## 6. Locality, mixing and spectral estimates: what is used and what is not

- **Lieb–Robinson** (Source): finite range, from [17] Eq. (3.26)/Cor. 4.4 ("Lieb–Robinson bound and local approximation"); power law, from [17] Thm 3.1 "Followed by Haar averaging on C" to realise $E_{AB}$ (Lemma VI.5; the finite-range text does not name the Haar average); a custom LR iteration in Lemma V.6 that needs only a row-sum bound and puts no bound on $|Y|$. LR enters only through the leakage $\eta_{AB}$ of the interaction-picture cocycle.
- **Imaginary-time / quasi-locality** (Source): the convergent nested-commutator expansion for $|u|<1/(2dJ)$ (from [CR] Lemma IX.3), applied *only to the cut perturbation $V$*; in the quasi-local case it is replaced by support-weighted norms and spectral tails of $K$.
- **No mixing, gap or clustering** (Source): "Our results relate approximate conditional independence in Gibbs states to the strength and spatial decay of their interactions, without assuming clustering or rapid mixing." There is no Lindbladian, no spectral gap, no clustering assumption. The spectral objects are the relative modular operator and its logarithm, not a generator's gap.
- **Kernels in the two proofs** (Mine, checked against both texts): [Y] integrates modular time against $1/|\sinh\pi t|$ and balances its $e^{-\pi|t|}$ tail against Lieb–Robinson growth $e^{v\beta|t|}$, which gives the rate $\pi\mu_{LR}/(\pi+\beta v)$ (Lemma IV.1). [CR]'s Dirichlet form integrates Heisenberg time against $g(t)=1/(\beta\cosh(2\pi t/\beta))$ (Lemma X.2), but [CR] uses $g$ only near $t=0$ (Lemma X.4 integrates it over $|t|\le\epsilon$); $g$ is not what beats Lieb–Robinson growth there. In [CR]'s quasi-locality proof (App. A) the time tails are cut by the Gaussian filter $f(t)$ (dissipative part, Lemma A.2, error $e^{-\sigma^2\ell^2/2d^2}+|A|e^{-\ell}$) and by the tails of the coherent-term weights $b_1$ (which contains $1/\cosh(2\pi t)$ in units where Heisenberg time is $\beta t$) and $b_2$ (Cor. A.2, $\|b_11(|t|\ge T)\|_1\lesssim e^{-cT}$). Balanced against the factorial path-counting bound of Lemma A.1 at $T=\ell/(4e^2d\beta)-1$, this gives the rate $c'/(d\beta)$, not a $\pi\mu/(\pi+\beta v)$ form. So a cosh-type KMS kernel appears in both papers, but only [Y] balances a KMS-strip kernel against Lieb–Robinson growth in *modular* time.

## 7. What is genuinely new

**Claims of the paper (Source).** (1) Boundary instead of volume in the exponential prefactor, for finite range. (2) A *static* proof, "in contrast to the dynamical approach of Chen and Rouzé". (3) Stretched-exponential decay for stretched-exponential and exponential interactions of unrestricted body size, with a boundary prefactor. (4) Power-law results with polynomial $|A|$ dependence and "No $|C|$ or total-volume dependence". (5) A Gibbs-specific continuity estimate (Lemma V.5) with no small-error logarithm and no $B$/$C$ dimension dependence.

**My assessment (Mine).**
- The structural novelty is Lemma III.4. It reduces relative-entropy loss under partial trace to two items: (a) the leakage of the relative modular cocycle from the retained algebra, with an explicit $1/|\sinh\pi t|$ kernel, and (b) a spectral tail of the relative modular logarithm.
- Combined with Lemma III.1 (cut comparison), this turns the CMI into a *locality question about one interaction-picture unitary generated by the cut interaction*.
- $|C|$ disappears because CMI is bounded by $\delta_{AB}$ directly, instead of going through a recovery-map trace distance and Alicki–Fannes/Winter continuity. [CR] Remark III.1.4 names exactly that conversion as the source of its $|C|$ and $\log\dim$ loss: "this loss is due to log (dim) factors common in conversion between entropies and trace distances."
- The boundary improvement comes from two places: only $V$ is imaginary-time conjugated (Lemma IV.2), and only the cut strength enters the moments of $K$ (Lemma V.3).
- Things the paper does *not* do: construct a recovery map; give a global Markov property; work beyond full partitions; reach $D=1$ in Thm II.2 (it needs $D\ge2$ for the isoperimetric absorption).

## 8. The Chen–Rouzé time-averaged detailed-balanced Lindbladian with single-Pauli jumps on $A$ (Source: [CR])

### 8.1 Exact construction

Setting ([CR] §II): $n$ qubits, $H=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}H_\gamma$ with $\|H_\gamma\|\le1$. Interaction graph: $\gamma_1\sim\gamma_2$ iff the supports overlap; $d$ is its maximal degree. $\mathrm{dist}(A,B)$ is the minimal length of an overlap path of terms. $H_\ell$ is the local patch of terms with $\mathrm{dist}(\gamma,A)<\ell-1$.

**The detailed-balanced Lindbladian** ([CR] (2.1), "[CKG23]"). For jumps $\{A^a\}$ closed under adjoints with $\|A^a\|\le1$:
$$\mathcal L[\cdot]=\underbrace{-i[B,\cdot]}_{\text{coherent}}+\sum_a\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma(\omega)\Big(\underbrace{\hat A^a(\omega)(\cdot)\hat A^a(\omega)^\dagger}_{\text{transition}}-\underbrace{\tfrac12\{\hat A^a(\omega)^\dagger\hat A^a(\omega),\cdot\}}_{\text{decay}}\Big)d\omega .$$

**Operator Fourier transform** (2.2)–(2.3), with $H=\sum_iE_iP_{E_i}$:
$$\hat A(\omega)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{iHt}Ae^{-iHt}e^{-i\omega t}f(t)\,dt=\sum_{\nu\in B(H)}A_\nu\hat f(\omega-\nu),\qquad A_\nu=\sum_{E_2-E_1=\nu}P_{E_2}AP_{E_1},$$
$$\hat f(\omega)=\frac1{\sqrt{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega^2}{4\sigma^2}\Big),\qquad f(t)=e^{-\sigma^2t^2}\sqrt{\sigma\sqrt{2/\pi}} .$$
Here $\sigma$ is an energy width.

**Transition weight.** The Metropolis weight (2.4) is $\gamma(\omega)=\exp\big(-\beta\max(\omega+\beta\sigma^2/2,0)\big)$. The Gaussian weight (2.5) is $\gamma_G(\omega)=\exp(-(\omega+\omega_\gamma)^2/2\sigma_\gamma^2)$ with $\beta(\sigma_\gamma^2+\sigma^2)=2\omega_\gamma$. Final bounds take $\sigma=1/\beta$ (Theorem III.1).

**Coherent term** ([CR] App. A (A1), "Recall the coherent term [CKG23] for the Metropolis weight"):
$$B^a=\int b_1(t)\,e^{-i\beta Ht}\Big(\lim_{\eta\to0^+}\int b_2^\eta(t')A^{a\dagger}_H(\beta t')A^a_H(-\beta t')dt'+c\,A^{a\dagger}A^a\Big)e^{i\beta Ht}dt ,$$
with $A_H(t)=e^{iHt}Ae^{-iHt}$, $b_1(t)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}e^{1/8}\big(\frac1{\cosh(2\pi t)}*\sin(-t)e^{-2t^2}\big)$ ($\|b_1\|_1<1$), and $b_2^\eta(t)=1(|t|\ge\eta)\frac{1}{2\sqrt2\pi}\frac{\exp(-2t^2-it)}{t(2t+i)}$. The constant $c$ appears as $\frac1{8\sqrt2\pi}$ in (A1) and as $\frac1{16\sqrt{2\pi}}$ in the proof of Cor. A.2 of the extraction; the dagger on the first $A$ is visible in Cor. A.1–A.2 but dropped in the (A1) extraction. "For now, we do not need to worry about the explicit form of B; what will matter is the Dirichlet form."

**The jumps and the time average** ([CR] (1.1), (3.1)):
$$\mathcal R_{A,t}[\cdot]:=\frac1t\int_0^t\exp(s\mathcal L_A)[\cdot]\,ds,\qquad \mathcal L_A:=\sum_{a\in P^1_A}\mathcal L_a,\qquad P^1_A:=\{X_i,Y_i,Z_i\}_{i\in A},$$
"Each $\mathcal L_a$ is the Lindbladian associated with each single-qubit Pauli jump $A^a$ (with metropolis weight)." The damaged state is $\rho_{\beta,-A}:=\mathrm{Tr}_A[\rho_\beta]\otimes\tau_A$, with $\tau_A$ maximally mixed on $A$.

### 8.2 Which detailed balance: KMS

"Given a full-rank state $\rho$, a Lindbladian $\mathcal L$ is said to satisfy the Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS)-$\rho$-detailed balance if it is symmetric with respect to the KMS inner product associated with $\rho$, $\langle X,Y\rangle_\rho:=\mathrm{Tr}[X^\dagger\rho^{1/2}Y\rho^{1/2}]$." Theorem II.1 ([CKG23]): $\mathcal L$ "satisfies KMS-$\rho_\beta$-detailed balance and hence fixes the Gibbs state exactly: $\mathcal L[\rho_\beta]=0$". By [CR] §I, $\mathcal L=\sum_a\mathcal L_a$ with $\mathcal L_a[\rho_\beta]=0$ *for each jump*. Remark II.0.1: "Other ρ-weighted norms are possible (e.g., GNS [KB16]), but in this paper, we will only consider the KMS inner product since the Lindbladian we consider is KMS-detailed balanced." [RSCA] §4: Gibbs states of non-commuting Hamiltonians are fixed points of quasi-local KMS Lindbladians whose jumps "are quasi-local, with tails controlled by Lieb-Robinson light-cones".

*Mine, from memory and not retrieved here:* GNS-detailed-balanced generators for non-commuting $H$ require jumps that are exact Bohr-frequency eigenoperators (Davies type). These are generally non-local, which is why the quasi-local construction is KMS. The frequency filtering $\hat A(\omega)$ with finite width $\sigma$ is the price of locality, and the coherent term $B$ restores exact detailed balance.

### 8.3 Why time averaging

[CR] Lemma VII.1: $\|\mathcal L^\dagger[\mathcal R_t^\dagger[O]]\|\le2/t$ for $\|O\|\le1$, because $\frac1t\mathcal L^\dagger\int_0^te^{\mathcal L^\dagger s}ds=\frac1t(e^{\mathcal L^\dagger t}-I)$. Corollary VII.1: for KMS-detailed-balanced $\mathcal L$, the Dirichlet form $\mathcal E(X,Y):=-\langle X,\mathcal L^\dagger(Y)\rangle_\rho$ satisfies $\mathcal E(\mathcal R_t^\dagger[O])\le2/t$.

"Note that such a property is generally false for the Lindblad evolution $e^{\mathcal L^\dagger_At}$ itself without time-averaging, because $\mathcal L^\dagger_A$ may have arbitrarily small eigenvalues. Thus, time-averaging provides a different mechanism to obtain a small Dirichlet form that is independent of the spectral gap." "The key property we exploit from the time-averaging map is that it is simultaneously (quasi)-localized and stationary. Interestingly, the interplay between time-averaged dynamics, stationarity, and Dirichlet form has also been recently exploited in full glory in the recent analysis of classical slow-mixing Markov chains [LMR+24]."

It is also implementable (Remark II.2.1): sample $s$ uniformly from $[0,t]$ and run $e^{s\mathcal L}$.

### 8.4 Why single-Pauli jumps

- Generation: products of single-site Paulis give every Pauli string $S\in P_A$. Lemma VIII.1 (Leibniz): $[\prod_{i=1}^wA_i,O]=\sum_j\prod_{i<j}A_i\,[A_j,O]\prod_{i>j}A_i$.
- Corollary VIII.1: $\|[S,O]\|_{\rho_\beta}\lesssim2^wr'\sum_j\|[A_j,O]\|_{\rho_\beta}^{16\beta_0^2/\beta^2}$ for $\beta>1/d$, and $\lesssim2^w\sum_j\|[A_j,O]\|_{\rho_\beta}$ for $\beta\le1/d$.
- Implementation cost: "it is desirable to have local Lindbladian jumps, as high-weight Paulis always incur an exponential time-overhead in the simulation." Remark VIII.0.1: "Without this reduction step to local jumps, the proof of Theorem III.1 still goes through with similar parameters. However, the exponential time-overhead is fundamental with high-weight Pauli and cannot be improved by any additional mixing time assumption."
- Imaginary-time control for single sites (Lemma IX.3): for $|\beta|<1/2d$, $\|e^{\beta H}Ae^{-\beta H}\|\le1/(1-2d|\beta|)$; for weight $w$ the bound is $(1-2d|\beta|)^{-w}$ (Cor. IX.1). At $\beta_0=1/4d$ this gives $\le2^{|A|}$, the constant used in the main proof.

### 8.5 Why on $A$

- The recovery must act near $A$. With jumps supported on $A$, $\mathcal L_A$ is quasi-local: for $\ell\ge4e^2\beta d$, $\|\mathcal L^\dagger_{A,\ell}-\mathcal L^\dagger_A\|_{\infty-\infty}\lesssim|A|(e^{-c'\ell/(d\beta)}+2^{-\ell})$ (Lemma VII.3, by Lieb–Robinson path counting, App. A).
- Truncation errors accumulate linearly in $t$ (Lemma VII.2: $\|\mathcal R^\dagger_{A,t,\ell}-\mathcal R^\dagger_{A,t}\|\lesssim t\|\mathcal L^\dagger_{A,\ell}-\mathcal L^\dagger_A\|$), "Thus, the quasi-locality holds for exponential times."
- The jumps on $A$ generate the full algebra on $A$, so any $X$ that is exactly stationary must commute with all of $P_A$, i.e. be trivial on $A$ (§IV.B and (4.1)).
- Every $\mathcal L_a$ fixes $\rho_\beta$, so $\mathcal R_{A,t}[\rho_\beta]=\rho_\beta$.
- "The proof intuition behind Theorem III.1 is that the long-time averaging map $\mathcal R_{A,t}$, which keeps updating region A using all supported single qubit Pauli jumps, must mix A very thoroughly, 'conditioned' on everything else."

### 8.6 Statements

**Theorem III.1 (quasi-local recovery maps via time-averaged Gibbs sampling).** "Consider the Gibbs state of a Hamiltonian H with interaction degree at most d and a region A ⊂ Λ. Then, the time-averaged Lindblad dynamics $\mathcal R_{A,t}$ with single-qubit Pauli jumps $\{A^a\}_{a\in P^1_A}$, Metropolis weight γ(ω) (2.4), and σ = 1/β, t > 0 gives an approximate recovery map at all temperatures β"
$$\|\mathcal R_{A,t}[\rho_{\beta,-A}]-\rho_\beta\|_1\le|A|^22^{2|A|}\begin{cases}r(\beta,d)\,t^{-\frac{128\beta_0^4}{\beta^3(\beta+5\beta_0)}}&\beta>4\beta_0\\ r'(\beta,d)\,t^{-\frac{2\beta_0}{\beta+5\beta_0}}&\beta\le4\beta_0\end{cases},\qquad\beta_0:=\frac1{4d};$$
"Therefore, there are numbers r, μ > 0 and 0 < λ < 1 depending only on β, d such that $\|\mathcal R_{A,t}[\rho_{\beta,-A}]-\rho_\beta\|_1\le re^{\mu|A|}t^{-\lambda}$."

Remark III.1.2: "The exponential dependence on |A| is hard to remove unconditionally using the current Lindbladian approach. Right now, it appears due to the slow, inverse polynomial decay of the Dirichlet form. If the present argument can be combined with a faster mixing time or spectral gap analysis, one might be able to improve the exponential dependence on |A|, hence establishing the global Markov property."

**Corollary III.1 (quasi-locality).** $\|\mathcal R_{A,t,\ell}-\mathcal R_{A,t}\|_{1-1}\lesssim|A|t(e^{-c'\ell/(d\beta)}+2^{-\ell})$. With $t^*(\ell)=e^{(\mu|A|+m\ell)/\lambda+1}$, $\|\rho_\beta-\mathcal R_{A,t^*,\ell}[\rho_{\beta,-A}]\|_1\lesssim r\exp(\mu'|A|-m\lambda\ell/2)$, where $m=\min(\ln2,c'/(d\beta))$ and $\mu'=\mu+2$.

**Corollary III.2 (Gibbs states are locally Markov).** If $\mathrm{dist}(A,C)\ge4e^2\beta d$, then $I(A{:}C|B)_{\rho_\beta}\lesssim r'|A||C|\exp(\mu'\min(|A|,|C|)-\lambda'\mathrm{dist}(A,C))$. Proof: take $\ell=\mathrm{dist}(A,C)-1$, so $H_\ell$ does not touch $C$. Then [Wil11, Thm 11.10.5] gives $I\le\Delta\log\dim C+h_2(\Delta)\lesssim\log(\dim C)\sqrt\Delta$.

**Corollary III.3 (uniform clustering ⇒ quasi-local preparation).**
- Uniform clustering (Def. III.1): $\mathrm{Cov}_{\rho^X_\beta}(A,C)\le\mathrm{Poly}(|A||C|)\|A\|\|C\|e^{-\ell/\xi}$ for every truncated Gibbs state $\rho^X$.
- Through local indistinguishability ([BK19] Thm 5), it gives a channel of dissipative gate complexity $e^{O(\log^D(n/\epsilon))}$ that prepares $\rho_\beta$ to error $\epsilon$.
- The proof patches $\mathcal F_{A^{h,j}}=\mathcal R_{A^{h,j},t,\ell}\circ(\tau\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{h,j}})$ over a tiling, "punching point-like holes … until the whole lattice is covered"; the errors add as $h_0N_A[\mathrm{Poly}\,e^{-\ell'/\xi'}+\Delta]$.

**Appendix B (local gap).**
- Definition B.1: $\mathcal L$ is "λ-locally-gapped if $-\lambda\langle X,\mathcal L^\dagger(X)\rangle_\rho\le\langle X,\mathcal L^{\dagger2}(X)\rangle_\rho$ for each operator X". It is *uniformly* locally gapped if this holds for every restricted Gibbs state $\rho_X$.
- This holds for commuting Hamiltonians with jumps $P^1_A$. "For general noncommutative Hamiltonians (with local jumps), we do not know of any a priori bound on the local gap, even assuming high temperature."
- Lemma B.2: under a local gap, the Dirichlet form decays as $e^{-2\lambda(t-1)}\|X\|_\rho^2$.
- Cor. B.1: a gap $c|A|^{-c'}$ gives preparation in $O(n\,\mathrm{polylog}(n/\epsilon))$.
- **Cor. B.2:** the same assumption gives $I(A{:}C|B)\le\mathrm{Poly}(|A|,|C|)\exp(-\mathrm{dist}(A,C)/\xi)$, a *global* Markov property.

### 8.7 Proof architecture of Theorem III.1 (Source, [CR] §XI.A with §VII–X)

1. **Pauli twirl.** $\rho_\beta-\rho_{\beta,-A}=2^{-2|A|-1}\sum_{S\in P_A}[S,[S,\rho_\beta]]$ (*Mine:* checked by hand from $\sum_{\text{all }S}S\rho S=4^{|A|}\rho_{-A}$). So, for $\|X\|\le1$ and by stationarity,
$$|\mathrm{Tr}[X\mathcal R(\rho-\rho_{-A})]|\le2^{-2|A|-1}\sum_S\|I\|_\rho\,\|[S,[S,\mathcal R^\dagger X]]\|_\rho ,$$
by Cauchy–Schwarz in the KMS inner product. Recoverability means $\mathcal R^\dagger_{A,t}[X]\approx(\mathcal R^\dagger_{A,t}[X])_{-A}$.
2. **Peeling the outer product without diverging Gibbs conjugation** (Lemma IX.5): $\|AO\|_{\rho_\beta}\lesssim(\ldots)\|O\|_{\rho_\beta}^{4\beta_0/\beta}(\|\rho_{\beta_0}A\rho_{\beta_0}^{-1}\|+\|\rho_{\beta_0}^{-1}A\rho_{\beta_0}\|)$ for $\beta>4\beta_0$.
   - It uses the decomposition $A=\frac1{\sqrt{2\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}}\int\hat A(\omega)d\omega$ (Lemma IX.1), the exact shift $e^{\beta H}\hat A(\omega)e^{-\beta H}=e^{\beta\omega+\sigma^2\beta^2}\hat A(\omega+2\sigma^2\beta)$ (Lemma IX.2), and a frequency cutoff $\Omega$ balanced against $\|O\|_\rho$.
   - The obstruction this regularises (Source): "a local operator A can change the energy by a lot with an exponentially small amplitude; once β gets large enough, it sufficiently amplifies the exponentially small amplitudes, causing a divergence"; "this is a genuinely noncommutative phenomenon absent in commuting or classical Hamiltonians".
3. **Global to local** (Cor. VIII.1): commutators with strings cost $2^w$ and a Hölder exponent.
4. **Dirichlet form as an integral of squared commutators.**
   - Lemma X.1 ([RFA24, Lemma C.2]): $\mathcal E(X,Y)=\sum_a\sum_{\nu_1,\nu_2}\bar\alpha_{\nu_1,\nu_2}\mathrm{Tr}[\sqrt\rho[A^a_{\nu_1},X]^\dagger\sqrt\rho[A^a_{\nu_2},Y]]$.
   - Lemma X.2: $\mathcal E(X,Y)=\sum_a\iint g(t)h^G(\omega)\,\mathrm{Tr}[\sqrt\rho[\hat A^a(\omega,t),X]^\dagger\sqrt\rho[\hat A^a(\omega,t),Y]]\,dt\,d\omega$ with $g(t)=\frac1{\beta\cosh(2\pi t/\beta)}$.
   - Lemma X.3 (Metropolis): replace $h^G$ by $h(\omega)=e^{-\sigma^2\beta^2/8}e^{-|\omega|\beta/2}$.
   - "Remarkably, this yields an elegant, manifestly PSD quadratic form of commutators."
5. **Small Dirichlet form ⇒ small commutator** (Lemma X.4): $\|[A,O]\|_{\rho_\beta}\lesssim d^2|A|(\ldots)^{\frac{\beta+4\beta_0}{\beta+5\beta_0}}(\ldots)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+5\beta_0}}\mathcal E(O)^{\frac{2\beta_0}{\beta+5\beta_0}}$. The proof smears over Heisenberg time $|t|\le\epsilon$, cuts frequencies at $\Omega$ (because $1/h(\omega)$ diverges), applies Cauchy–Schwarz against $g h$, and optimises $\epsilon,\Omega$. "Roughly, this means that an operator changing slowly under the Lindbladian must nearly commute with the jump $A_a$."
6. **Time averaging** (Cor. VII.1): $\mathcal E_A(\mathcal R^\dagger_{A,t}[X])\le2/t$. Chaining 1–6 gives $|A|^22^{2|A|}r\,t^{-\text{exponent}}$.
7. **Quasi-locality** (App. A): Lieb–Robinson for the dissipative part (purified jump $V=\int\hat A(\omega)\otimes|\omega\rangle d\omega$; Lemma A.2) and for the coherent part (taming the $1/t$ singularity of $b_2$; Cor. A.1–A.2).

### 8.8 Where $e^{|A|}$ comes from (Mine, read off §8.7 and [CR] §XI.A)

The twirl in step 1 is a *normalised* average ($4^{|A|}-1$ strings against the prefactor $2^{-2|A|-1}$), so by itself it contributes no exponential. What it does is force the proof to handle Pauli strings of every weight $w\le|A|$. The exponential then comes from two per-string costs, neither of them spectral:
- the Gibbs-conjugation bound $\|\rho_{\beta_0}S\rho_{\beta_0}^{-1}\|\le(1-2d\beta_0)^{-w}=2^{w}\le2^{|A|}$ at $\beta_0=1/4d$ (Cor. IX.1), paid when the outer commutator is peeled (Lemma IX.5 for $\beta>4\beta_0$, Lemma IX.4 for $\beta\le4\beta_0$);
- the $2^{w}$ Leibniz/Gibbs-conjugation cost of reducing $[S,\cdot]$ to single-site commutators (Cor. VIII.1), i.e. of walking a word of length $w\le|A|$ in the Pauli group on $A$ with single-site generators.

Together they give the $|A|^22^{2|A|}$ prefactor of Theorem III.1.

[CR] themselves attribute the exponential to "the slow, inverse polynomial decay of the Dirichlet form": the Hölder exponents are small, so $t$ must be $e^{\Theta(|A|)}$.

## 11. Open problems listed by the sources

**[Y] §VII (Source).**
1. "Obtaining algebraic decay for unrestricted-support interactions throughout $D<\zeta\le2D$, improving the distance exponents, and establishing stronger boundary dependence remain open."
2. Global Markovianity: "For finite-range interactions, the goal is to obtain exponential or stretched-exponential CMI decay with at most polynomial region-size dependence beyond the regimes covered by existing high-temperature, clustering, or rapid-mixing assumptions. 3. Bosons: "Bosonic lattices pose a different challenge because their local Hilbert spaces are infinite-dimensional and their interactions are typically unbounded. A possible route is to combine thermal bounds on local occupation numbers with spatial localization and modular spectral estimates, while controlling entropy errors through energy-constrained continuity bounds [20]. Establishing estimates that remain uniform as the local occupation cutoff is removed would be a key step toward applying the static approach to interacting bosonic Gibbs states."

## 12. Testing the user's intuition (all Mine unless a source is named)

### 12.1 Verdict

The intuition is **partly right, in a precise way, and wrong in one important way.**
- *Right:* the theory of Gibbs/Markov structure meets expander theory through one object, the **gap of local, state-symmetric restriction–co-restriction dynamics** (Glauber or block heat-bath classically; [CR]'s $\mathcal R_{A,t}\circ(\tau_A\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_A)$ quantumly). [CR] says that a *uniform local gap* would turn the proved local Markov property into the open global one (Cor. B.2; Remark III.1.2: "combined with a faster mixing time or spectral gap analysis"). [Y] §VII (open problem 2) is weaker on this point: it lists rapid mixing, with high temperature and clustering, among the *existing* routes to polynomial size dependence and asks for global Markovianity beyond them.
- *Wrong:* the Markov property itself is **not** an expansion property. Classical Gibbs measures are exactly Markov at phase transitions, where expansion fails ([Y] §I; [CR] Fig. 1). Both all-temperature proofs are explicitly gap-free: [CR] by Cesàro averaging, [Y] by modular-cocycle localisation. What drives local Markovianity is **locality (Lieb–Robinson) plus a sufficiency/recovery mechanism**, and its natural language is Tomita–Takesaki modular theory, which is the measure-theoretic layer of NCG.
- *The hidden structure in the "time-averaged, detailed-balanced, single-Pauli-jumps-on-A" construction* is the HDX down-up walk read quantumly. Restriction is "forget $A$". Co-restriction is "re-thermalise $A$ with state-symmetric dynamics generated by generators of the forgotten algebra". Time averaging makes the co-restriction exist without a gap, at the cost of the $e^{|A|}$ that an expander-type local gap would remove.

### 12.2 Bridges, each labelled

**B1 — THEOREM (elementary).** *On expander interaction graphs the boundary improvement is void.* If the interaction graph is an $h$-edge-expander ($|\partial_eA|\ge h|A|$ for $|A|\le n/2$) and every edge carries a term with $\|\cdot\|\ge J_{\min}>0$, then $J_{\min}h|A|\le g_{A|A^c}\le dJ|A|$. [Y]'s $e^{Cg}$ is then $e^{\Theta(|A|)}$, the same order as [CR]. Moreover, a bounded-degree expander has diameter $O(\log n)$, while the bound needs $r\gtrsim g$, so it says something only for $|A|=O(\log n)$. The improvement from volume to boundary is therefore an isoperimetric (polynomial-growth, amenable) phenomenon, the opposite of expansion. *Proof:* combine $g\ge J_{\min}|\partial_eA|$ with $g\le dJ|A|$ ([Y] §IV.B) and the diameter bound.

**B2 — KNOWN-LINK (sources).** *Markov ≠ mixing; gap = local-to-global upgrade.* All-temperature local Markov with no gap: [CR] Thm III.1 and [Y] Thms II.1–II.3. Gap or mixing hypotheses give global Markov with polynomial size dependence: [CR] Cor. B.2 (uniform local gap $c|A|^{-c'}$); [RSCA] Thm 3.3 (global rapid mixing plus frustration-freeness); Bakshi et al. (Dobrushin). So the expander mechanism belongs exactly at the open global Markov problem.

**B3 — KNOWN-LINK (cited by [CR]; text of [LMRRW] read).** *Time averaging is the quantum "locally stationary distribution".*
- [LMRRW] Def. 1.2: $\nu$ is $\varepsilon$-locally stationary if $\mathcal E(f,\log f)\le\varepsilon$.
- Lemma 3.1: $\mathbb E_{t\sim[0,T]}\mathcal E_P(f_t,\log f_t)\le\mathrm{KL}(\nu\|\pi)/T$. This is gap-free, and the cycle graph shows that $O(1/T)$ is tight for $T=o(n^2)$ without more structure (§1.4).
- Lemma 3.5: if Glauber restricted to every pinning $(W,x_W)$ has MLSI $\ge C$, then a locally stationary $\nu$ has $\mathbb E_{x_W\sim\nu}d_{TV}(\nu|_{x_W},\pi|_{x_W})\le\sqrt\varepsilon/C$.
- Reading: [CR]'s Cor. VII.1 ($\mathcal E\le2/t$) is the quantum Lemma 3.1, and [CR]'s Lemma X.4 plus the Pauli twirl replaces [LMRRW]'s pinned MLSI by a kernel argument with polynomial loss. [LMRRW]'s authors work in the HDX / spectral-independence community; [CR]'s introduction cites CLV21 (*entropy factorization via high-dimensional expansion*) and AJK+22 (*entropic independence … down-up random walks*) for the static/dynamic tie.

**B4 — ANALOGY (strong).** *$\mathcal R_{A,t}\circ(\tau_A\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_A)$ is a quantum block down-up step, and [CR] Thm III.1 says $\rho_\beta$ is an approximate fixed point of it with a quasi-local co-restriction.* Classically, the heat-bath update of block $A$ (forget $A$, resample from $\pi(\cdot|\partial A)$) is an exact local recovery; that is the Markov property. [LMRRW] Def. 2.11's "associated Markov chain $x\to z|x\to x'|z$" is the same restriction–co-restriction shape as prong 3's row 2 (down-up walk, Glauber, $\delta^*\delta$). [CR]'s *uniform* local gap (Def. B.1: "for any restricted Gibbs state $\rho_X$") plays the role of spectral independence's "for $\mu$ and all of its pinnings" (prong 3 §2.4) and of [LMRRW] Lemma 3.5's "for every choice of W and $x_W$". **Guard:** quantum truncation $H\mapsto H_X$ is not conditioning, and marginals of quantum Gibbs states are not truncated Gibbs states ([Y]: "a marginal with an arbitrary unobserved exterior is not silently identified with a local Gibbs state"). The hereditary row of prong 3's table is exactly what fails quantumly, and CMI measures that failure.

**B5 — KNOWN-LINK, partly from memory.** *Where $e^{|A|}$ sits relative to a Poincaré inequality.* [CR]'s route from single-site commutators to triviality on $A$ walks words in the Pauli group of $A$ (Leibniz, $2^w$) and pays a Gibbs-conjugation cost $2^{w}$ per string; the normalised twirl over $4^{|A|}$ elements forces strings of every weight up to $|A|$ (§8.8). In the *tracial* $L^2$ norm, the single-site depolarising expectations $E_i=\frac14\sum_PP_i\cdot P_i$ commute and tensorise variance (Efron–Stein: $\|X-E_AX\|_2^2\le\sum_{i\in A}\|X-E_iX\|_2^2$, from memory), with no exponential loss. The loss is created by the KMS weighting of a noncommuting Gibbs state, under which the $E_i$ are not $\rho$-symmetric. The fix the literature uses is *approximate tensorization* in KMS/relative-entropy form ([BCGLPR] (31), with the mixing operator $h(\sigma_{A^cB^c})$; reference [10] there, "Approximate tensorization of the relative entropy for noncommuting conditional expectations"). An expander-type improvement of [CR] must therefore be a KMS-weighted Efron–Stein / approximate-tensorization inequality on $A$, i.e. [CR]'s uniform local gap.

**B6 — KNOWN-LINK (identification is mine; both definitions retrieved).** [Y]'s $\delta_R$ equals the conditional relative entropy $D_E$ of Capel–Lucia–Pérez-García as written in [BCGLPR]. Approximate tensorization, "global $D(\rho\|\sigma)$ ≤ const × sum of local conditional relative entropies", is the entropy form of a local-to-global inequality. It has the same logical shape as Garland-type and entropy-factorisation statements in HDX (prong 3 §2), and the constant is controlled by a two-region mixing quantity $\|h\|$ instead of link spectra.

**B7 — KNOWN-LINK (NCG; standard identification, mine).** *[Y]'s Lemma III.4 is a quantitative Petz sufficiency theorem.* Note 1 §6 quotes Jenčová–Petz Thm 1: a subalgebra is sufficient iff the Connes cocycles lie in it, iff relative entropy is preserved by restriction. [Y]'s $\sigma^{it}\rho^{-it}=(D\sigma{:}D\rho)_t$ and $\eta_R(t)$ is its distance from the subalgebra, so (3.14)/(3.15) bound the relative-entropy loss by the cocycle's leakage with an explicit kernel; $q_R=0\Rightarrow\delta_R=0$ is the qualitative theorem. Two numerical checks already in this directory, written by a parallel run (`check_2609_subalgebra_identities.py` and `check_2609_subalgebra_bound.py`, not written by me), test the generalisation from $B(\mathcal H_R)\otimes1$ to an arbitrary $N=\bigoplus_iM_{k_i}\otimes1_{m_i}$ with trace-preserving $E_N$. Running them here gave:
- $W_N$ is an isometry to $5\cdot10^{-15}$;
- $W_N^\dagger\Delta W_N=\Delta_N$ to $6\cdot10^{-14}$;
- $\max j(s)/[(1+s)q^2/s^2]\le0.362$ (0.312, 0.240, 0.362 for the three block structures; rerun in the verification pass);

So the inequality appears to hold for general finite-dimensional inclusions. That is still a numerical observation, not a proof.

**B8 — Derivation candidate for prong 1 (Mine; THEOREM-level once checked).** *A quantitative coboundary criterion.* Take Note 1's inclusion $D_0\subset D$ (face functions inside history functions; or $D_0\subset$ the corner algebra for states with coherences), the trace given by counting complete histories, and two members $\mathbb P_\beta,\mathbb P_{\beta'}$ of the Gibbs family. Then:
- $E_{D_0}$ is the uniform average over the $h_\tau$ histories that end at $\tau$, where $h_\tau$ are the heights of Note 1 §3.4;
- the cocycle is $\big(\mathbb P_{\beta'}(\mu)/\mathbb P_\beta(\mu)\big)^{it}=e^{it[(\beta-\beta')F(\mu)+c(s(\mu))]}$, with $c$ a normalisation that depends only on the origin face;
- since $|e^{ix}-e^{iy}|\le|x-y|$, $\eta(t)\le|t|\cdot\max_\tau\mathrm{osc}_{\mu\to\tau}\big[(\beta-\beta')F(\mu)+c(s(\mu))\big]$, which is $|\beta-\beta'||t|\max_\tau\mathrm{osc}_{\mu\to\tau}F$ when there is a single origin face, as in dictionary v1, or when the subalgebra is that of the pair (input face, output face).

B7 then bounds the sufficiency defect $\delta$ of the barycentre by a power of the oscillation of the accumulated action $F$ over histories with the same endpoint, i.e. by the sup-distance of $F$ from coboundaries. In the commutative case this also follows from the chain rule. The B7 form is the one that survives when the state carries coherences across re-routings (Note 1 §3.2, third resolution). It passes the drag test: it is stated for any finite inclusion of algebras with a trace, so it applies equally to a Bratteli diagram or a tiling AF algebra.

**B9 — ANALOGY → SPECULATION (NCG).** *The Dirichlet form is a squared Connes commutator.* [CR] Lemmas X.2–X.3 write $\mathcal E=\sum_a\iint g\,h\,\|[\hat A^a(\omega,t),X]\|_\rho^2$, the KMS-weighted energy of a derivation. For jumps that are exact eigenoperators of the modular flow, the frequency and time integrals factor out: $\hat A(\omega,t)=e^{i\nu t}\hat f(\omega-\nu)A$, so $\mathcal E_a(X)=c_\beta(\nu)\|[A,X]\|_\rho^2$, with $c_\beta(\nu)=\tfrac12\int h(\omega)|\hat f(\omega-\nu)|^2d\omega=\tfrac12e^{-\sigma^2\beta^2/8}\,\mathbb E_{\omega\sim N(\nu,\sigma^2)}e^{-\beta|\omega|/2}$, which is $\asymp e^{-\beta|\nu|/2}$ up to a factor depending only on $\sigma\beta$. This is my computation from Lemma X.3, using $\int g=\tfrac12$ and the fact that $|\hat f|^2$ is the $N(0,\sigma^2)$ density. In Note 1 the matrix units $e_{\mu\nu}$ of the complete-history corner are such eigenoperators, with Bohr frequency $F(\mu)-F(\nu)$. So the arrow Dirichlet form is $\sum c_\beta(F(\mu)-F(\nu))\|[e_{\mu\nu},X]\|^2_{\mathbb P_\beta}$, a KMS-weighted squared Lipschitz seminorm for the quiver Dirac operator of Note 1 §8. Its Poincaré constant is a spectral gap; Rieffel's compact quantum metric spaces tie Lip-norm Poincaré bounds to finite diameter (from memory). This is the precise place where "expander gap", "Connes distance" and "KMS weighting" become one inequality.

**B10 — KNOWN-LINK (from memory: Takesaki's theorem) + Derivation (mine).** A state-preserving conditional expectation onto a subalgebra exists iff the subalgebra is invariant under the state's modular flow. In Note 1 the modular flow of $\mathbb P_\beta$ is $\alpha^F_{-\beta t}$, which fixes $p_\sigma$ and $s_\mu s_\mu^*$. So $D_0$ and $D$ are modular-invariant, the co-restriction is *exact*, and Note 1 §4.2's conditioning by partial isometries never needs a recovery map. For noncommuting lattice Gibbs states the algebra of $A^c$ is not modular-invariant. That is the root of approximate-Markov theory: [Y] measures the leakage of the modular cocycle, and [CR] builds the co-restriction dynamically. **Guard:** Note 1's KMS family is the *commuting* (diagonal-Hamiltonian) case, where [Y] and [CR] are trivial (exact Markov along depth).

**B11 — SPECULATION for prongs 1–2 (classical part a KNOWN-LINK, from memory: thermodynamic formalism).** *Memory as range, and the right memory measurement.*
- mlp-bridge E2 measures $I(\sigma_{\ell+1};\sigma_{\ell-j}|\sigma_\ell)$ with a *single-layer* buffer at varying lag. The quantity of this literature is the CMI against **buffer width**, $I(\sigma_{\ell+1};\sigma_{\le\ell-k}\,|\,\sigma_{\ell-k+1},\dots,\sigma_\ell)$ as a function of $k$: the "Markov length" ([RSCA] §1).
- For chains, a positive law with memory $k$ is a Gibbs measure for a range-$(k+1)$ depth potential. [KB] proves the quantum 1D version: small CMI in all splits ⟺ approximately Gibbs for a short-range Hamiltonian.
- This answers prong-1 question (a) of mlp-bridge §5 in the abstract. The extension of the cocycle formalism to laws with memory is the groupoid cocycle of a finite-range (or summable-variation) potential on the path space. It is not additive over single arrows, and its KMS states are still given by Renault/Neshveyev (Note 1 §5.2). It is stated for path spaces of Bratteli diagrams, so it is not realisation-specific.
- **Guard:** Hammersley–Clifford needs positivity, and the realised history laws have forbidden transitions.

**B12 — ANALOGY (prong-3 U1, U4).** [CR] Cor. III.3 (local Markov plus local indistinguishability, patched over a tiling) reconstructs a global state from quasi-local patches with additive error. That is a quantum instance of U4's "a state from linearly many restrictions", and its certificate is uniform clustering, not agreement expansion. [CR] App. B is the quantum instance of U1: a uniform local gap gives global structure from local data (polylog preparation, global Markov).

**B13 — SPECULATION (prong 1, extension beyond commuting).** *Memory as noncommutativity.* If the "energy" on the history algebra contained re-routing terms (off-diagonal $s_\mu s_\nu^*$, i.e. coherences), its KMS state would be a noncommuting Gibbs state. Its CMI across depth would then decay at a rate set by how fast the interaction-picture cocycle of the cut spreads in depth ([Y]'s mechanism), instead of being exactly zero. What must be true: a depth-locality (Lieb–Robinson) bound for the arrow dynamics, and a tensor-like decomposition of the history algebra across a depth cut. **Guard:** CMI bounds do not survive pinching to the diagonal (data processing does not act on the conditioning system), so this would not by itself explain the *classical* memory measured in mlp-bridge.

**B14 — ANALOGY.** *The modular-time cutoff.* [Y] beats exponential Lieb–Robinson growth $e^{v\beta|t|}$ in modular time with the KMS-strip kernel $1/|\sinh\pi t|$, giving rates of the form $\pi\mu_{LR}/(\pi+\beta v)$ (Lemma IV.1) and $\pi(\zeta-D)/(\pi+\beta v)$ (Lemma VI.5). [CR]'s counterpart is the time cutoff in the quasi-locality of its Lindbladian (App. A): Gaussian filter tails and the $1/\cosh(2\pi t)$-containing coherent weight $b_1$ against factorial path-counting growth, giving the rate $c'/(d\beta)$. [CR]'s Dirichlet-form kernel $1/(\beta\cosh(2\pi t/\beta))$ is used only near $t=0$ (§6). In Note 1, modular time *is* weighted depth (§5.2). The analogue of the rate would be a light-cone speed in depth for whatever noncommuting dynamics B13 posits; for the diagonal $\alpha^F$ the speed is zero, which is again exact Markov.
