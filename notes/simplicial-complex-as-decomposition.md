# What the simplicial complex is

### Nerves, coherence, and decompositions that do not lift

*Research note, 2026-10-01. Second note in the barycentre-arrows thread; builds on [the conditional arrow algebra](conditional-arrow-algebra.md). Same labelling: **Fact** (cited), **Derivation** (proved here), **Expectation** (proposal).*

---

## 0. The question and the answer

The question raised after the last pass: what object, role or interpretation should the simplicial complex have, if "find a correspondence for projections" is not fruitful directly? Could the vertices be something like pure states, the faces something like state decompositions, with the ordering coming from complete positivity and from inclusions of operator systems, where a state decomposes in the small system but the decomposition does not lift to the big one, so that "being a simplex" is a property of an inclusion?

The answer developed here is that this intuition is exactly right, and that it has three precise forms which coincide classically and separate noncommutatively.

1. A simplicial complex is the **nerve** of a family of restrictions: a set of vertices is a face iff the restrictions can be jointly satisfied. In an algebra, "restriction" means a projection and "jointly satisfied" means **jointly certain in one state**; for commuting projections this is nonzero overlap, for non-commuting ones it is nonzero meet, which is smaller. Every classical use of simplicial complexes (Čech nerves, simplicial approximation, Vietoris–Rips, Bratteli diagrams, measurement contexts, ReLU support patterns) is an instance.
2. For an inclusion $C(V)\subset A$ of a commutative "frame" algebra into a bigger algebra, the **coherence complex** $K(C(V)\subset A)$, whose faces are the supports of restrictions to $C(V)$ of pure states of $A$, is a simplicial complex; it is the nerve of the cover of the spectrum of $A$ by the supports of the vertex projections; it is trivial when $A=C(V)$, the full simplex when $A$ contains a matrix algebra over $V$, it grows with $A$, and every finite complex arises this way. Its faces are exactly the **decomposition patterns that do not lift**: a pure state of $A$ whose restriction to the frame decomposes into the vertices of the face.
3. The two readings are dual: in the commutative picture a face is a set of **overlapping coarse tests** that are true together; in the noncommutative picture a face is a set of **mutually exclusive fine tests** that one pure state coherently superposes. The completely positive map that passes between them is the pinching (measurement) of the frame, which is majorization-decreasing and collapses the coherence complex to its vertex set. "Simpliciality is an inclusion" is the statement that the complex is an invariant of $C(V)\subset A$ destroyed by passing to the relative commutant.

Operator systems fit in precisely: they can fail the downward-closure property, because conditioning (compression to a face) leaves the system, and the failure is computable. One level up, the same constructions applied to the history algebra of the previous note produce the **history complex**, whose vertices are the barycentres and whose simplices contain the histories as chains, which is the algebraic shadow of barycentric subdivision.

## 1. Three exact constructions

Throughout, $V$ is a finite vertex set and $K\subset 2^V\setminus\{\emptyset\}$ an abstract simplicial complex (non-empty, downward closed).

### 1.1 The face algebra is the universal algebra of the complex

**Derivation.** Let $D_0(K)$ be the universal unital C\*-algebra generated by commuting projections $\{e_u\}_{u\in V}$ subject to
$$
\prod_{u\in F}e_u=0\ \ (F\notin K),\qquad \prod_{u\in V}(1-e_u)=0 .
$$
Then $D_0(K)\cong C(K)$, the functions on the set of faces, with $e_u$ the indicator of the open star $\{\sigma\in K: u\in\sigma\}$. *Proof.* A character $\chi$ is determined by $G=\{u:\chi(e_u)=1\}$; the first relation says every subset of $G$ is a face, i.e. $G\in K$ (downward closure), the second says $G\neq\emptyset$. $\square$

This is the C\*-version of the Stanley–Reisner ring $k[x_u]/(x_F:F\notin K)$: the complex is a presentation by vertex projections with **forbidden joint certainties**. States of $D_0(K)$ are probability measures on faces; $\omega(e_u)$ is the probability that $u$ is active; $\sum_u\omega(e_u)=\omega(d)$ is the mean face dimension. The face projections $p_\sigma=\prod_{u\in\sigma}e_u\prod_{u\notin\sigma}(1-e_u)$ are the atoms. This is the $D_0$ of the previous note.

### 1.2 The joint-certainty complex of any family of projections

**Derivation.** Let $\{e_u\}_{u\in V}$ be projections in a von Neumann algebra $M$ (no commutativity assumed). Then
$$
K_{\mathrm{cert}}=\Big\{F:\ \bigwedge_{u\in F}e_u\neq0\Big\}=\big\{F:\ \exists\ \text{a normal state }\omega\text{ with }\omega(e_u)=1\ \forall u\in F\big\}
$$
is a simplicial complex, and it is the nerve of the family of closed faces $\mathcal F_u=\{\omega:\omega(e_u)=1\}$ of the normal state space. *Proof.* $\omega(e)=1$ iff the support projection of $\omega$ lies under $e$, so $\omega(e_u)=1$ for all $u\in F$ iff the support lies under the meet; conversely a vector state in the range of the meet is certain of all $e_u$. Hence $\mathcal F_{u_1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal F_{u_k}=\mathcal F_{\wedge e_{u_i}}$, non-empty iff the meet is non-zero; nerves are downward closed. $\square$

For commuting projections the meet is the product, and $K_{\mathrm{cert}}$ of the vertex stars in $C(K)$ is $K$ itself (the classical fact that a complex is the nerve of its open vertex stars). For non-commuting projections the meet is smaller than any product: two non-commuting rank-one projections in $M_2$ have meet $0$. **Noncommutativity prunes the certainty complex.**

### 1.3 The coherence complex of an inclusion

Let $B=C(V)\subset A$ be a unital inclusion with the atoms $e_u$ of $B$ mutually orthogonal and $\sum_u e_u=1$ (a *frame*). For a state $\omega$ of $A$ write $\mathrm{supp}_B(\omega)=\{u:\omega(e_u)>0\}$.

**Derivation (coherence complex).** Define
$$
K(B\subset A)=\{\mathrm{supp}_B(\omega):\ \omega\ \text{a pure state of }A\}.
$$
Then:

1. $K(B\subset A)$ is a simplicial complex.
2. $K(B\subset A)$ is the nerve of the cover of the spectrum $\hat A$ (irreducible representations) by the open sets $U_u=\{\pi:\pi(e_u)\neq0\}$: $F$ is a face iff some irreducible $\pi$ has $\pi(e_u)\ne0$ for every $u\in F$.
3. $K(B\subset A)\subset K(B\subset A')$ whenever $A\subset A'$.
4. $K(B\subset B)$ is the discrete complex on $V$; $K(B\subset A)$ is the full simplex $\Delta(V)$ as soon as $A$ contains $M_V(\mathbb C)$ with $B$ its diagonal.
5. For finite-dimensional $A=\bigoplus_kM_{n_k}$ with $e_u=\bigoplus_ke_u^{(k)}$, $K(B\subset A)=\bigcup_k\Delta(S_k)$ with $S_k=\{u:e_u^{(k)}\neq0\}$: the nerve of the central decomposition.
6. Every finite complex $K$ on $V$ is $K(C(V)\subset A_K)$ for the **facet algebra** $A_K=\bigoplus_{\sigma\ \text{facet}}M_\sigma(\mathbb C)$, $e_u\mapsto\bigoplus_{\sigma\ni u}e^{(\sigma)}_{uu}$.

*Proof.* (2) A pure state is a vector state $\omega_\xi$ of an irreducible $\pi$, and $\omega_\xi(e_u)>0$ forces $\pi(e_u)\neq0$; conversely, given $\pi$ with $\pi(e_u)\neq0$ for $u\in F$, pick non-zero $\xi_u\in\mathrm{ran}\,\pi(e_u)$, which are orthogonal since the $e_u$ are, and $\xi=\sum_{u\in F}\xi_u$ normalised gives $\mathrm{supp}_B(\omega_\xi)=F$. (1) follows from (2), or directly: for $G\subset F=\mathrm{supp}_B(\omega_\xi)$ the conditioned state $\omega^{q_G}$, $q_G=\sum_{u\in G}e_u$, is the vector state of $\pi(q_G)\xi$, still pure, with support $G$. (3) Pure states of a C\*-subalgebra extend to pure states of the bigger algebra, and the extension has the same restriction to $B$. (4), (5), (6) are the finite-dimensional cases of (2). $\square$

So the coherence complex records **which atoms of the frame can be coherently superposed inside one pure state of $A$**. It is empty of higher simplices when there is no room to superpose ($A=B$) and complete when there is full room. It is an ordered function of the inclusion: a chain $B\subset A_1\subset A_2\subset\cdots$ gives a filtration of complexes, a "persistence" of coherence.

### 1.4 Overlap versus coherence, and the map between them

The same $K$ therefore has two algebraic carriers:

| | carrier | vertices | a face $\sigma$ is | how $K$ is read off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classical | $C(K)$ | coarse tests $e_u=$ star of $u$, commuting, overlapping | a set of tests that are **true together** | joint-certainty complex (§1.2) |
| coherent | $A_K=\bigoplus_{\text{facets}}M_\sigma$ | fine tests $e_u$, orthogonal, summing to $1$ | a set of tests one pure state **superposes** | coherence complex (§1.3) |

In the classical carrier a vertex is an *observable* and a face is a *point* (an atom $p_\sigma$). In the coherent carrier a vertex is a *pure state* $\chi_u$ of the frame and a face is a *pure state of $A_K$* restricting to a mixture of the $\chi_u$, $u\in\sigma$. The barycentre of $\sigma$ has a canonical lift: the **maximally coherent face state**
$$
\omega_\sigma=\omega_{\xi_\sigma},\qquad \xi_\sigma=|\sigma|^{-1/2}\sum_{u\in\sigma}\xi_u,
$$
whose restriction to the frame is the uniform barycentre $b_\sigma=|\sigma|^{-1}\sum_{u\in\sigma}\chi_u$.

The completely positive map between the pictures is the pinching by the frame,
$$
\Phi_B(a)=\sum_{u\in V}e_u\,a\,e_u ,
$$
the conditional expectation onto the relative commutant $B'\cap A$. **Derivation.** For a state $\omega$ of $A$:

- $\omega\circ\Phi_B=\sum_u\omega(e_u)\,\omega^{e_u}$ is an orthogonal decomposition into states supported on the atoms; it is the lift of the decomposition $\omega|_B=\sum_u\omega(e_u)\chi_u$.
- $\omega\circ\Phi_B=\omega$ iff $\omega(e_uae_w)=0$ for all $u\ne w$ and all $a$, i.e. iff $\omega$ has no coherences across the frame; for a pure $\omega$ with support of size $\ge2$ this fails.
- In finite dimensions the density of $\omega\circ\Phi_B$ is the pinching of the density of $\omega$, hence majorized by it (Schur–Horn): the pinching is entropy-increasing, and the decompositions along the frame sit at the bottom of this majorization order.
- $K(B\subset B'\cap A)$ is the discrete complex: pure states of the relative commutant are characters. **Decoherence of the frame collapses the coherence complex to its vertex set.**

This is the exact content of "simpliciality is an inclusion": the complex is an invariant of $B\subset A$, it is not seen by $B$ alone, and it is destroyed by the completely positive map that measures $B$.

## 2. The mechanism you described, made exact

Take a pure state $\omega$ of $A$ with $\mathrm{supp}_B(\omega)=F$. Then

- in the small system $B$, $\omega|_B=\sum_{u\in F}\omega(e_u)\chi_u$ is a genuine decomposition into pure states of $B$;
- in the big system $A$, $\omega$ has no decomposition at all;
- the decomposition does lift, but to the *pinched* state $\omega\circ\Phi_B$, not to $\omega$; the gap $\omega-\omega\circ\Phi_B$ is the functional $a\mapsto\sum_{u\ne w}\omega(e_uae_w)$ supported on the coherences;
- the set $F$ is a face of $K(B\subset A)$, and every face arises this way.

So **the faces of the coherence complex are precisely the non-liftable decomposition patterns**, which is the structure you were pointing at. The ordering is the one induced by inclusions of the big algebra (§1.3(3)) together with the majorization order under pinching (§1.4).

**Fact** (Bratteli–Robinson Thm 4.1.25, Tomita's theorem on orthogonal measures). Orthogonal decompositions of a state $\omega$ of $A$ correspond bijectively to abelian von Neumann subalgebras of the commutant $\pi_\omega(A)'$ of its GNS representation, and refinement of decompositions corresponds to inclusion of subalgebras; $\omega$ is pure iff the commutant is trivial. **Derivation.** The frame decomposition $\omega=\sum_u\omega(e_u)\omega^{e_u}$ holds iff the cyclic subspaces $[\pi_\omega(A)\pi_\omega(e_u)\xi_\omega]$ are mutually orthogonal, iff their projections form an abelian subalgebra of the commutant implementing the decomposition. This is the same criterion as $\omega\circ\Phi_B=\omega$, now placed in the Bratteli–Robinson order of decompositions. It is also the state-side form of the separation $f*g=\sum_if*e_i*g$ of the convolution by intermediate projections recorded in the last reading pass: the cross terms $\varphi(a_i^*a_j)$ vanish exactly when the state is invariant under the pinching by the $e_i$.

## 3. Vertices as pure states, barycentres, and the ReLU

In the coherent carrier the pieces of the original conjecture acquire literal meanings, with no coordinates:

- a vertex $u$ is the pure state $\chi_u$ of the frame; a face $\sigma$ is the block $M_\sigma$, i.e. one irreducible representation of $A_K$; the barycentre $b_\sigma$ is the restriction of the maximally coherent state $\omega_\sigma$;
- **exclusion of a vertex is Lüders conditioning**: $\omega_\sigma^{\,1-e_u}=\omega_{\sigma\setminus\{u\}}$ for $u\in\sigma$, since $(1-e_u)\xi_\sigma$ renormalised is $\xi_{\sigma\setminus\{u\}}$. The ReLU zero at $u$ maps the barycentre of $\sigma$ to the barycentre of $\sigma\setminus\{u\}$, exactly, with the "projection information" being the pair of supports;
- an arrow between barycentres has a minimal implementer, the rank-one partial isometry $v_{\tau\sigma}=|\xi_\tau\rangle\langle\xi_\sigma|$ with $v^*v=|\xi_\sigma\rangle\langle\xi_\sigma|$ and $vv^*=|\xi_\tau\rangle\langle\xi_\tau|$.

**Derivation (a fork in the modelling).** Compose two such implementers at the same layer: $v_{\tau\sigma}v_{\tau'\sigma'}=\langle\xi_\sigma,\xi_{\tau'}\rangle\,v_{\tau\sigma'}$ with
$$
\langle\xi_\sigma,\xi_{\tau'}\rangle=\frac{|\sigma\cap\tau'|}{\sqrt{|\sigma|\,|\tau'|}} .
$$
Arrows compose head-to-tail only when the faces are *orthogonal* as states; overlapping faces compose with the overlap cosine as weight. The previous note's groupoid algebra made faces orthogonal by fiat (distinct face projections $p_\sigma$ on histories), which gives exact categorical composition. The frame picture instead has a positive-definite **overlap kernel** $G_{\sigma\tau}=|\sigma\cap\tau|/\sqrt{|\sigma||\tau|}$ on faces, and the span of the arrows is then an operator system whose products pick up $G$. This is the quantitative version of the Connes–van Suijlekom tolerance relation "share at least $k$ vertices": the $0$–$1$ relation is replaced by the Gram matrix of the barycentre states. Which composition rule a given network obeys is an empirical question; the two are not the same algebra.

## 4. Where operator systems break the simplicial property

**Fact** (Connes–van Suijlekom, [Tolerance relations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02903) Prop. 3.11). For the operator system $E(R)\subset M_{|X|}$ of a reflexive symmetric relation $R$ on a finite set $X$, the restriction of a vector state is pure iff its support is $R$-connected; since pure states of an operator system extend to pure states of the ambient algebra, the pure states of $E(R)$ are exactly these restrictions.

**Derivation.** Define, for an operator system $S$ containing the frame $B=C(X)$, the family $\mathcal K(B\subset S)=\{\mathrm{supp}_B(\varphi):\varphi\ \text{pure on }S\}$ as in §1.3. For $S=E(R)$ it is the family of **$R$-connected subsets**, which is *not* downward closed: on the path $a-b-c$ the set $\{a,b,c\}$ is in the family and $\{a,c\}$ is not. The vector state of $(1,0,1)/\sqrt2$ restricted to $E(R)$ equals $\tfrac12(\omega_a+\omega_c)$ because $e_{ac}\notin E(R)$: the coherence between $a$ and $c$ is invisible, so the state decomposes.

The reason is structural. Downward closure in §1.3 was proved by compressing a pure state to a sub-face, $\omega\mapsto\omega^{q_G}$, which stays pure because $q_GAq_G\subset A$ (C\*-algebras are hereditary under compression). An operator system is a bimodule over its diagonal but compressions need not preserve purity. So **the simplicial property is the shadow of the hereditary property of C\*-algebras**, and "C\*-algebra or operator system" has a combinatorial fingerprint: the first gives simplicial complexes, the second gives connectivity systems, which remember the 1-skeleton (the graph of $R$) and forget higher faces. This matches Connes–van Suijlekom's own remark that $E(R)$ only uses the underlying simplicial complex in degrees $0,1$.

## 5. The same essence in different areas

Every construction above is a nerve, and the nerve is what the "fundamental essence" seen in different arguments is:

- **Čech nerves and the nerve theorem.** For a good cover (all finite intersections contractible), the nerve is homotopy equivalent to the union; the map to the nerve is $x\mapsto\sum_u\psi_u(x)[u]$ for a partition of unity $\{\psi_u\}$, landing in the open face of the sets that contain $x$. The barycentre of a face is the image of a point with equal weights, and barycentric subdivision is the nerve of a refined cover. Simplicial approximation works by refining covers until a map carries stars into stars. In the state picture a partition of unity is a POVM $\{h_u\}$, $\sum_uh_u=1$, and the barycentric coordinates of a state are the numbers $\omega(h_u)$.
- **Vietoris–Rips and persistence.** The flag complex of the tolerance relation $d(x,y)<\varepsilon$ is the Vietoris–Rips complex; Connes–van Suijlekom's $E(R_\varepsilon)$ is therefore the operator system of the Vietoris–Rips graph, with propagation number $\lceil\mathrm{diam}/\varepsilon\rceil$, and the filtration in $\varepsilon$ that persistent homology studies is a filtration of operator systems. The filtration of coherence complexes along $B\subset A_1\subset A_2\subset\cdots$ in §1.3 is the algebraic counterpart.
- **Choquet.** A Bauer simplex is the state space of a commutative algebra; its faces are the supports; "unique decomposition" is the statement that the coherence complex of $C(V)\subset C(V)$ is discrete. Noncommutativity is measured by how much coherence complex an inclusion adds.
- **Bratteli diagrams.** For the AF inclusion $A_\ell=\bigoplus_{\tau\in K_\ell}M_{h_\tau}\subset A_{\ell+1}$ of the previous note, the coherence complex of the centre $Z(A_\ell)=C(K_\ell)$ inside $A_{\ell+1}$ is the nerve of the incidence matrix: a set of layer-$\ell$ faces is a simplex iff they all feed a common layer-$(\ell+1)$ face. Connes's Penrose example has the full $1$-simplex at every level.
- **Measurement contexts.** In the sheaf-theoretic treatment of contextuality (Abramsky–Brandenburger, [1102.0264](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264)) a measurement scenario is a simplicial complex whose faces are the jointly measurable sets, and an empirical model is a family of probability distributions over the faces; for commuting projections joint measurability and joint certainty give the same complex.
- **ReLU support patterns, concretely.** For a concrete layer the sets $U_u=\{x: \text{unit }u\text{ active at }x\}$ cover the inputs that activate something, and the downward closure of the occurring support patterns is the Čech nerve of this cover. For the first layer the $U_u$ are open half-spaces, the cover is good, and the nerve theorem says the realisation $|K_1|$ is homotopy equivalent to the covered region; for deeper layers the cover sets need not be contractible and the nerve is an upper model. The "cells" of the earlier exact-slice experiment are the atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by the activation sets of all layers; the pattern complexes are the nerves of the same sets. None of this needs coordinates on the simplices.

## 6. One level up: the history complex

Apply §1.2 and §1.3 to the history algebra $C^*(\Lambda)$ of the previous note, with the face algebra $D_0$ (atoms $p_\sigma$, one per face of every layer) as the frame.

**Derivation.**

- *History complex.* On complete histories let $P_\sigma$ be the commuting projection "the history visited $\sigma$". Its joint-certainty complex $K_{\mathrm{hist}}=\{F:\prod_{\sigma\in F}P_\sigma\neq0\}$ has as simplices the sets of faces that lie on a common history; its facets are the face sequences of complete histories; it is the order complex of the reachability poset (chains $\sigma_0\leadsto\sigma_1\leadsto\cdots$), i.e. the nerve of the cover of the history set by the stars $\{\text{histories through }\sigma\}$.
- *Coherence complex.* $K(D_0\subset C^*(\Lambda))$ has as simplices the sets of faces reachable from a common input face: the nerve of the cover of $E^0$ by the reachable sets $R(\sigma_0)$, one facet per input face (pure states are vector states in one origin block, §3.1 of the previous note).
- $K_{\mathrm{hist}}\subset K(D_0\subset C^*(\Lambda))$, and the difference is exactly the coherence between non-comparable faces that share an ancestor.

On the random quiver of the check script (face counts $3,4,3,2$; script `notes/checks/graph_algebra_checks.py`):

| complex | facets | simplices | top dimension |
|---|---|---|---|
| history complex | 29 (distinct face sequences of the 95 complete histories) | 149 | 3 |
| coherence complex of $D_0\subset C^*(\Lambda)$ | 3 (one per input face) | 959 | 8 |

The joint-certainty characterisation of the history complex was verified on 170 sampled sets; containment holds.

This is the algebraic echo of barycentric subdivision, which was the original motivation from meshes: **the barycentres of the layer complexes become the vertices of the history complex, and the histories are its chains.** The arrow calculus of the previous note (objects = barycentres, arrows between them) is the passage to the subdivision, extended across layers. The sufficiency criterion of that note says when a vertex of the history complex (a face, a barycentre) carries all the information of the chain through it: exactly when the weight cocycle is a coboundary.

## 7. How to use the complex, concretely

- **Treat the layer complexes as nerves and compute their invariants from patterns alone.** Betti numbers of $K_\ell$ per layer, and the persistence of the history complex as layers are added, need no weights and no coordinates; for the first layer the nerve theorem makes the numbers homotopy invariants of the activation cover.
- **Measure the coherence excess.** The ratio of the sizes of the coherence complex and the history complex at each depth is a direct count of how far the face algebra is from a simplex inside the history algebra; it is $1$ exactly when every face has a unique ancestor history.
- **Decide the composition rule.** Whether arrows between overlapping faces compose head-to-tail (orthogonal carrier, groupoid algebra) or with the overlap cosine (frame carrier, operator system with the Gram kernel $G$) is a property of the network, testable on the pattern data by checking whether two-step transitions factor through single faces or through overlapping ones.
- **Use the facet algebra as the minimal noncommutative carrier of a layer.** Each facet is one irreducible representation; vertices are coherently superposable within a facet, never across; exclusion is Lüders conditioning; the barycentre is the maximally coherent state. What is *not* assumed is that any concrete linear map sends uniform superpositions to uniform superpositions; the abstract arrow is face-to-face, and $\omega_\sigma\mapsto\omega_\tau$ is its canonical lift, not a prediction about $W$.

## 8. What is exact here and what is not

Exact: §1.1–1.4, §2, §3 (including the overlap-cosine fork), §4, §6, with proofs or citations as marked, and the checks in the table. Interpretation: the dictionary in §5 and the proposals in §7. Not touched: any claim about the activation geometry of a particular network, or about which composition rule a trained network obeys. The one deliberate choice is to read "vertex" as a pure state of a commutative frame and "face" as a pattern of coherence relative to an inclusion; the alternative classical reading (vertex = coarse test, face = point) is recorded in §1.4 as the dual picture, and both are nerves.
