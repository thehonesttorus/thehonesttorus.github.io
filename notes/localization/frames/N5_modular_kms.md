## 0. Summary and verdict

**Verdict: negative for a new estimator component; the frame contributes an exact spectral reading of the
detector and two corrections to the pending experiment's design.**

       number operator.
   - Lap/2, |y|^2/2 and (E + n/2)/2 span sl(2, R): the O(n)-invariant part of the metaplectic representation. This is
     the one genuinely noncommutative object in the dynamics. Its content is [E, S] = -Lap S: smoothing does not
     commute with scaling.
   - It gives:
     - the eigenspaces of A (S of homogeneous functions);
     - the Euler-Stein ladder as the lowering action of Lap on the chaos grading;
     - the Mellin form of the Green measure.

     It gives no new computation.
2. **(i) KMS.** The truth is a ground state, the Green measure is a renormalized KMS weight, and everything is
   commutative or type I.
   - **Ground state (Proposition 3.2).** The truth is the vacuum (beta = infinity ground state) of N, read on the
     position algebra.
   - **Temperature is gauge.** Thermal quasi-free states restrict to N(0, coth(beta/2) I), and by homogeneity they
     carry exactly the same number. Localized laws are squeezed (pure) states, not thermal ones.
   - **The Green measure (Theorem 3.3).** Smoothed by the input noise, it is exactly the critical Poincaré series of
     gamma under scaling, i.e. the Gaussian state composed with the resolvent (1 - E)^{-1}.
     - Its Mellin symbol is 1/(1 - q) on degree-q functions, with the pole at the truth's degree q = 1.
     - Its two-sided completion is the conformal (KMS) weight of exponent -1 of the scaling groupoid,
       Gamma((n+1)/2)/(sqrt2 pi^{n/2}) |y|^{-n-1} dy, with transverse measure sigma.
   - **Type I.** The scaling groupoid is free and proper (type I). The modular cocycle h(r)/h(s) is the Doob
     h-transform (Proposition 3.4).
3. **(ii) Variational principle: it exists, but Newton is the Green formula (Propositions 3.4 and 5.1).**
   - **The exact variational form.** e(0)/truth = E_Q exp((1/2) int (delta/e)), with Q the size-biased
     localization. This is an exact Feynman-Kac / Gibbs (Boué-Dupuis) form in which the relative defect is the
     potential.
   - **The obstruction.** The problem is linear, with dual solution the Green measure. Every defect-descent step needs
     the defect of the previous correction, i.e. one more application of A, i.e. one more Laplacian. Step k is the
     tower T_k at about 2 n^k chain runs. Richardson is the only base-point step.
4. **(iii) Matrix controls are abelian (Proposition 6.1).**
   - In natural parameters (Lambda = Sigma^{-1}, theta = Lambda m), every linear-tilt localization is additive, and
     controls commute.
   - At the base Sigma = I every control commutes with the state.
   - There is no non-type-I leaf space.
5. **Where noncommutativity really binds (Theorem 7.2, proved, checked).** On the second-chaos algebra generated by
   the H_i:
   - the base-point detector is a **vector state** (open walks L^T H^k L);
   - localization at scale tau mixes in the **tracial state** at rate tau: E_{N(0,tau)} L(y)^T L(y) =
     L^T L + tau tr(H H) + ...;
   - the closed walks tr(H_i H_j H_k) enter the error only at tau > 0.

   Consequences:
   - **Visibility ceiling.** X* <= the vector-state share.
   - **Random leaves are Hutchinson estimation of these traces.** That is why they hit the variance floor.

   The CCR lift (the defect as a dephasing-Lindbladian expectation, Proposition 7.1, checked) is exact but
   lift-invariant, hence relabelling.
6. **The spectral detector theorem (Theorem 4.1, proved) and its measured failure away from the base (measured).**
   - **The theorem.** If the error field is an A-eigenfunction (eigenvalue 1/2 - p), then delta = -2p w *at every
     input mean*, and err = -delta(0)/(2p) exactly. A sum of eigencomponents gives class-resolved weights.
   - **Measured on GC, n = 64, 4 networks.**
     - The pointwise slope drifts monotonically from -0.34..-0.47 at tau = 0 to -0.275..-0.297 at tau = 4.
     - The network spread shrinks from 0.13 to 0.02, and the layer dependence disappears.
   - **Consequences.**
     - The error is not in one spectral subspace. Its local exponent *rises* with |y|, the opposite of what any
       positive mixture of eigencomponents allows.
     - err = -delta(0)/(2p) is a statement about the log-slope of the profile at s = 1, not about spectral purity.
     - The non-universality of the base coefficient comes from base-point class mixing. It cannot be removed from
       base-point data.
7. **An exact injection-transport identity, measured (Proposition 4.4).**
   - **The identity.** The Leibniz rule A(fg) = f A g + g A f + grad f . grad g splits each layer's defect into:
     - the transported defect;
     - an injected defect;
     - a gate-pinning (carré du champ) contamination 2 grad Phi . grad u + u Lap Phi.
   - **Measured on GC and MF (n = 64, 11 networks) and K3 (n = 48, 4 networks).**
     - The contamination is 3-15% of the injected defect, and removing it moves the slopes by under 3% (GC).
     - Transport preserves each injection's local slope: within about 25% (GC, MF) and 15% (K3) on the layers that
       carry the error.
     - But the local slope **steepens with depth**: GC -0.29 -> -0.44..-0.65; MF -0.49 -> -1.1..-1.3; K3
       -0.16 -> -0.24..-0.28. The K3 per-layer explained share falls from 0.97 to 0.62-0.83 at the last layer.
     - A layer-resolved merge gives no consistent held-out gain over one slope.
     - A late-truncated in-place merge removes roughly its injection share times X, but its slope transfers worse:
       on 1 of 3 L16 networks it raised the MSE.
   - **What this means for A-late.** The two premises of its in-place design (small contamination, slope-preserving
     transport) hold at small n. Its slopes must be fitted per late layer against that layer's injection.

**Cost.** No component of this frame adds to the bill. Everything it proposes is analysis of the pending X* and

## 1. Setting

As in F1: u(m, Sigma) = E F(m + Sigma^{1/2} Z), F the bias-free ReLU MLP, 1-homogeneous. An estimator E(m, Sigma)
is homogeneous and point-mass exact; e(y) := E(y, I) on the slice. The F1 objects used here are:
- the reduced defect delta = e - y.grad e - Lap e;
- the localization generator A = (Lap + y.grad)/2 (repulsive Ornstein-Uhlenbeck, log time T = log(1 + tau));
- the eigen-equation (A - 1/2) v = 0 for v(y) = u(y, I);
- the Green formula truth - e(0) = -<G, delta> with G = (1/2) int_0^inf (1+tau)^{-3/2} N(0, tau I) dtau
  (F1 Cor 2);
- the profile b(s) = E_{m ~ N(0,(1-s)I)}[u - E](m, sI), with b'(1) = -delta(0)/2.

The error field is w := v - e, so err = w(0). Since (2A - 1) v = 0, **delta = (2A - 1) w** identically.

## 2. The algebra of the localization dynamics

Operators on functions of the input mean y in R^n:
- E = y.grad (Euler, the generator of dilations: e^{tE} g(y) = g(e^t y));
- Lap;
- N = E - Lap (the number operator on L^2(gamma): N He_alpha = |alpha| He_alpha; N = -2 L_OU);
- A = (Lap + E)/2;
- S = e^{Lap/2}, (S g)(y) = E g(y + Z) (Weierstrass transform; on L^2(gamma) it is the finite-dimensional
  S-transform of white-noise analysis, which maps the k-th chaos to homogeneous polynomials of degree k);
- D_lambda g(y) = g(lambda y).

**Theorem 2.1 (intertwining; proved, checked).**
- (a) For every measurable g of polynomial growth and T >= 0: e^{TA} S g = S D_{e^{T/2}} g.
- (b) On polynomials: S E S^{-1} = E + Lap = 2A, and S^{-1} E S = N. Hence 2A = S^2 N S^{-2} = e^{Lap} N e^{-Lap}.
- (c) K_- = Lap/2, K_+ = |y|^2/2, K_0 = (E + n/2)/2 satisfy [K_0, K_+-] = +-K_+-, [K_-, K_+] = 2 K_0. This is
  sl(2, R), the O(n)-invariant part of the metaplectic (oscillator) representation, i.e. the Howe dual pair
  (O(n), sl(2)). A, N, E, Lap, the heat and the OU generators all lie in span{K_-, K_0, K_+, 1}.

*Proof.*
- (a) The repulsive OU transition is y_T = e^{T/2} y + sqrt(e^T - 1) Z'. So e^{TA} S g(y) =
  E g(e^{T/2} y + sqrt(e^T - 1) Z' + Z) = E g(e^{T/2} y + e^{T/2} Z'') = S D_{e^{T/2}} g (y).
- (b) [Lap, E] = 2 Lap and ad_Lap^2 E = 0 give e^{t Lap} E e^{-t Lap} = E + 2t Lap. Take t = +1/2 and t = -1/2.
- (c) Direct: [Lap, |y|^2] = 4E + 2n, [E, Lap] = -2 Lap, [E, |y|^2] = 2|y|^2. QED.

*Check* (`n5_sym.txt`): on random degree-5 polynomials in 3 variables, S E S^{-1} = E + Lap and S^{-1} E S = N
hold exactly. S^{-1} y^4 = He_4. (a) holds exactly for a degree-5 polynomial in 1D.

**Corollary 2.2 (eigenspaces of the localization generator; proved under a recession hypothesis).**
- **Forward.** If g is q-homogeneous then A(S g) = (q/2) S g.
- **Converse.** Let w have polynomial growth with A w = (q/2) w, and suppose the recession limit
  g := lim_{lambda -> inf} lambda^{-q} w(lambda .) exists in L^1_loc with polynomial domination. Then g is
  q-homogeneous and w = S g.

*Proof.*
- Forward: differentiate (a) at T = 0, using D_{e^{T/2}} g = e^{qT/2} g.
- Converse: e^{TA} w = e^{qT/2} w gives w(y) = E[lambda^{-q} w(lambda(y + sqrt(1 - lambda^{-2}) Z))] with
  lambda = e^{T/2}. Let lambda -> inf (dominated convergence). Homogeneity of the limit is immediate. QED.

So the truth v = S F is the eigenfunction with eigenvalue 1/2, and its boundary data is the network itself. A
point-mass-exact homogeneous e has recession limit F, because e(lambda x)/lambda = E(x, I/lambda^2) -> F(x).
Corollary 2.2 then re-proves F1's uniqueness: delta = 0 forces w = S(F - F) = 0.

**Proposition 2.3 (four characterizations of the truth; proved).** For 1-homogeneous F, truth = E F(Z) equals each
of:
- (a) <Omega, F>_{L^2(gamma)} = lim_{tau -> inf} (e^{-tau N} F)(y): the projection onto the ground state (vacuum)
  of N, i.e. Mehler ergodicity;
- (b) v(0), v = S F: the value at 0 of the unique 1/2-eigenfunction of A with boundary data F;
- (c) lim_T e^{-T/2} (e^{TA} e)(0) for any homogeneous point-mass-exact e: a Martin / Poisson boundary value (F1
  Theorem 1);
- (d) c_n int_{S^{n-1}} F dsigma, with c_n = E|Z| = sqrt2 Gamma((n+1)/2)/Gamma(n/2): the uniform transverse measure
  of the scaling foliation, since |Z| is independent of Z/|Z|.

**What is noncommutative here, and what it buys.**
- The commutator [E, S] = -Lap S is the whole content: smoothing does not commute with scaling.
- Formally, delta = (1 - 2A) e = S (1 - E) S^{-1} e. The defect is the homogeneity violation of the "deconvolved
  estimator" S^{-1} e (backward heat; it exists on polynomials, and no statement below depends on it).
- **The ladder.** In Fock language Lap = sum_j a_j^2 (with a_j = d_j) lowers the N-degree by two, and E = N + Lap.
  Homogeneity, (N + Lap - 1) F = 0, projected on chaos k, is the Euler-Stein rung (k - 1) c_k = -tr c_{k+2} (F1
  Theorem 3).
- **Where it stops.** sl(2) organizes the tower and the ladder. It supplies no relation that closes them: every
  rung needs the next chaos.

## 3. (i) The C*-dynamical and KMS picture

**Proposition 3.1 (KMS on the scaling system; standard, sketch).**
- (a) For a flow on a locally compact space X, a beta-KMS state of (C_0(X), alpha) with beta != 0 satisfies
  omega(a (alpha_{i beta}(b) - b)) = 0 for all a and entire b. So it is carried by the fixed points. The dilation
  of R^n \ 0 has none, so it has no KMS states.
- (b) On the transformation groupoid (R^n \ 0) x R_+ with cocycle c(y, lambda) = log lambda, the KMS weights
  correspond to conformal measures, homogeneous of exponent beta: mu(lambda B) = lambda^beta mu(B), i.e.
  mu = r^{beta-1} dr (x) nu with nu finite on S^{n-1}. Lebesgue measure is beta = n; Theorem 3.3's completed Green
  weight is beta = -1. The isotropy is trivial (the action is free), so no isotropy states enter (Neshveyev; Christensen,
  arXiv 2005.01792, for the étale setting; Thomsen, arXiv 1109.2336).
- (c) The action is free and proper, so C*(G) ~ C(S^{n-1}) (x) K(L^2(R)) (Green imprimitivity): type I. Every weight
  is a measure on the leaf space S^{n-1} times a radial factor fixed by beta.

**Proposition 3.2 (the truth as a ground state; temperature is gauge; proved).**
- **The CCR picture.** Take the CCR algebra over R^{2n} with fields x_j = a_j + a_j^* (vacuum variance 1). The
  gauge-invariant quasi-free beta-KMS state for the free dynamics e^{itN} restricts, on the position MASA, to
  N(0, coth(beta/2) I). The vacuum (beta = infinity) gives gamma.
- **Temperature is gauge.** By homogeneity omega_beta(F(x)) = coth(beta/2)^{1/2} truth: every inverse temperature
  carries the same number.
- **Localized laws are squeezed, not thermal.** The localized laws N(m, sI) with s < 1 are not thermal. They are
  position marginals of pure squeezed coherent states |m, s>. Each is the ground state of the Bogoliubov-transformed
  number operator U_{m,s} N U_{m,s}^*.

  So the localization is a path of ground states, and u(m, sI) = <m, s| F(x) |m, s>.

*Proof.*
- **Variances.** Thermal occupation 1/(e^beta - 1) gives Var x = 1 + 2/(e^beta - 1) = coth(beta/2).
- **Squeezed states.** The squeezed vacuum with position variance s has a Gaussian wavefunction with |psi|^2 =
  N(m, sI). QED.

**Theorem 3.3 (the Green measure is the critical Poincaré series of gamma under scaling; proved).** Let
S^* mu := mu * gamma (so <S^* mu, g> = <mu, S g>), and G~ := S^* G.
1. G~ = (1/2) int_0^inf e^{-T/2} (D_{e^{T/2}})_* gamma dT. For g with spectral support Re q < 1,
   <G~, g> = <gamma, (1 - E)^{-1} g>.
2. **Mellin symbol.** For q-homogeneous g with q < 1, <G~, g> = E g(Z)/(1 - q). The integral diverges at q = 1, the
   truth's degree: the truth sits at the pole.
3. **Conformal completion.** The two-sided series G~_inf := (1/2) int_{-inf}^{inf} e^{-T/2} (D_{e^{T/2}})_* gamma dT
   has density kappa_n |y|^{-n-1}, with kappa_n = Gamma((n+1)/2)/(sqrt2 pi^{n/2}).
   - It is exactly conformal: (D_c)_* G~_inf = c G~_inf. It is the KMS weight of exponent -1 of the scaling
     groupoid, with transverse measure proportional to sigma.
   - G~_inf - G~ = (1/2) int_{-inf}^0 (...) is concentrated near 0 with Gaussian tails. The tails match:
     G~(|y| > R) ~ G~_inf(|y| > R) = c_n / R, which is F1's heavy tail G(tau > T) = (1+T)^{-1/2}.
4. **Spectral form of the error.** If delta = S delta~ with delta~ = sum_q delta~_q (q < 1), then
   err = -sum_q E delta~_q(Z)/(1 - q).

*Proof.*
1. S^* N(0, (e^T - 1) I) = N(0, e^T I) = (D_{e^{T/2}})_* gamma, applied termwise to F1's G written in log time.
   Since D_{e^{T/2}} = e^{TE/2}, (1/2) int_0^inf e^{-T(1-E)/2} dT = (1 - E)^{-1} on Re q < 1.
2. E g(e^{T/2} Z) = e^{qT/2} E g(Z).
3. The substitution u = e^T gives the density (1/2)(2 pi)^{-n/2} int_0^inf u^{-(n+3)/2} e^{-r^2/(2u)} du =
   kappa_n r^{-n-1}. Conformality follows from the shift T -> T + 2 log c. The tail follows by integrating
   kappa_n r^{-2} |S^{n-1}| dr.
4. Combine 1 and 2 with err = -<G, delta> = -<G~, delta~>. QED.

*Reading.* This is the precise content of "the Green measure is a transverse KMS weight": it is the
Patterson-Sullivan-type Poincaré series of the input law along the scaling flow, taken at its critical exponent and
truncated at the origin.
- Its transverse part is sigma, the same as the truth's (Proposition 2.3(d)). O(n)-invariance makes the transverse
  measure unique, so the leaf space carries no information beyond sigma.
- The useful content is item 4: the error is a Mellin-resolvent of the defect at the truth's pole. Section 4 uses
  this.

**Proposition 3.4 (the modular cocycle is the Doob transform; Feynman-Kac and Gibbs forms; proved, integrability
sketched).**
- (a) **The cocycle.** M_T = e^{-T/2} v(y_T)/v(0) is a positive martingale. The multiplicative cocycle on path
  segments, e^{-(T-S)/2} v(y_T)/v(y_S), is the "modular-cocycle update D' = D h(r)/h(s)" with h = v. Under the tilted
  law Q the terminal input is size-biased: dQ/dP = F_i(X)/E F_i.
- (b) **Feynman-Kac.** Let e_i > 0. Then e_i(0)/truth_i = E_Q exp( (1/2) int_0^inf (delta_i/e_i)(y_S) dS ).
- (c) **Gibbs / Boué-Dupuis form.** log(e_i(0)/truth_i) = sup_u E_Q[ (1/2) int (delta_i/e_i)(y^u_S) dS -
  (1/2) int |u_S|^2 dS ]. The relative defect is the potential. The maximizer is the chain's own Doob transform:
  drift grad log e_i, optimal u = grad log(e_i / v_i).

*Proof.*
- (a) (A - 1/2) v = 0.
- (b) d(e^{-T/2} e(y_T)) = -e^{-T/2} (delta/2)(y_T) dT + dM, so e^{-T/2} e(y_T) exp(int_0^T delta/(2e)) is a local
  martingale. Its value at T = 0 is e(0); its limit is F(X) exp(int_0^inf delta/(2e)), because
  e^{-T/2} e(y_T) = E(m_t, Sigma_t) -> F(X). Divide by truth.
- (c) The Donsker-Varadhan / Boué-Dupuis formula on Wiener space. QED.

*Value.* To first order in delta/e (about 1e-4 relative for the production chain), (b) is F1's Green formula. The
variational form adds no computable information, because its optimizer needs v.

**Verdict on (i).** Yes in three precise senses, and all of them are commutative or type I:
- the vacuum (beta = infinity) of N;
- the Martin boundary value of A;
- the transverse measure sigma, with the Green measure as the origin-renormalized conformal weight of exponent -1.

The inverse temperature is pure gauge under homogeneity. The KMS formalism contributes the Mellin / resolvent form of
the error (Theorem 3.3(4)), which is the spectral statement exploited next.

## 4. The eigen-structure of the defect

### 4.1 The spectral detector theorem

**Theorem 4.1 (proved).** Suppose w = sum_c w_c with A w_c = (1/2 - p_c) w_c and p_c > 0. By Corollary 2.2,
w_c = S g_c with g_c homogeneous of degree q_c = 1 - 2 p_c. Then:
1. b(s) = sum_c s^{p_c} w_c(0), and E_{y ~ N(0, tau I)} w_c(y) = (1 + tau)^{1/2 - p_c} w_c(0).
2. delta(y) = -sum_c 2 p_c w_c(y) **for every y**, not only at the base.
3. One class: err = -delta(0)/(2p) exactly, and delta = -2p w everywhere. The detector slope is constant in the
   input mean.
4. Several classes: the base-point weights are 2 p_c (F5 / Synthesis Corollary 7). A combination with
   sum_c p_c w_c(0) = 0 is invisible at the base, and (1/2) Lap delta(0) = 2 sum_c p_c^2 w_c(0) sees it. That costs
   about 2n^2 runs.
5. The Green formula is consistent with all of this:
   (1/2) int (1+tau)^{-3/2} sum_c 2 p_c (1+tau)^{1/2-p_c} w_c(0) dtau = sum_c w_c(0).

*Proof.*
1. E g_c(sqrt tau Z' + Z) = (1+tau)^{q_c/2} E g_c(Z); and b(s) = sqrt s E_{N(0,tau)} w with 1 + tau = 1/s.
2. delta = (2A - 1) w.
3. and 4. Follow from 1 and 2.
5. int_0^inf (1+tau)^{-1-p} dtau = 1/p. QED.

**What this says.**
- F1's measured "err ~ -delta(0)/(2p) with one coefficient" is exactly the single-spectral-class statement at the
  base.
- The class weights of Corollary 7 (kappa_4 path 1, kappa_3 path 2, gain halves 1/0) are 2 p_c ratios.
- The theorem also predicts something testable away from the base: under a single class, the slope of w on delta is
  the same at every input mean. Section 4.6 tests this.

### 4.2 Moments are eigenfunctions; cumulants are eigenfunctions up to carré du champ

A is a diffusion generator. Its carré du champ is Gamma(f, g) = A(fg) - f A g - g A f = grad f . grad g. The y.grad
part is first order and drops out.

**Proposition 4.2 (proved).** On the slice, for the exact functional:
- (a) Every expectation of a degree-d homogeneous function of the input is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue d/2:
  - raw moments M_k = E[F_{i1} ... F_{ik}] satisfy (2A - k) M_k = 0;
  - gate probabilities P(z_a > 0) (d = 0) satisfy A P = 0.
- (b) Joint cumulants satisfy (2A - k) kappa_k = -sum_{(B, B') ordered, B u B' = [k]} Gamma(kappa_B, kappa_B'). For
  k = 2 this is (A - 1) C_ab = -grad mu_a . grad mu_b. At the base it is F1's radial identity
  C - L L^T = (1/2) Lap C.
- (c) Hence a chain's local defect of a table kappa_k^chain (F1 Proposition 8) is
  (2A - k) kappa_k^chain + sum Gamma(kappa^chain, kappa^chain): the spectral residual of the table at its own
  homogeneity degree.

*Proof.*
- (a) Corollary 2.2.
- (b) F1 Theorem 4 (Hopf-Cole, ordered pairs, as its referee confirmed), traced, plus homogeneity on the slice:
  tr d_Sigma kappa_k = (k kappa_k - y.grad kappa_k)/2. Then tr H kappa_k = (k - 2A) kappa_k / 2.
- (c) Definition. QED.

So the cumulant heat hierarchy is the Leibniz defect of the localization generator applied to the moment-cumulant
formula. Every table the chain carries has a homogeneity degree, hence a spectral equation, hence a local defect.

### 4.3 The injection-transport identity

Decompose the chain's error as DATA_FINDINGS does, but as functions of the input mean:

    w_l = Phi_l o (w_{l-1} W_l) + inj_l,

where Phi_l(y) = Phi(alpha_l(y)) is the chain's mean gate. This defines inj_l(y).

**Proposition 4.4 (exact, proved).** With u_l = w_{l-1} W_l and dinj_l := delta_l - Phi_l o (delta_{l-1} W_l):

    dinj_l = delta[inj_l] + 2 grad Phi_l . grad u_l + 2 u_l o (A Phi_l),     delta[f] := (2A - 1) f.

At the base A Phi = (1/2) Lap Phi, so the contamination is 2 grad Phi . grad u + u Lap Phi.
- grad u_l = (J_{l-1}^true - J_{l-1}^chain) W_l is the error of the chain's first-chaos map.
- grad Phi is the gate density times the first-chaos direction of the neuron.

So the contamination is "gate pinning": localization moves the downstream gates. It is a product of two errors: the
gate defect A Phi (zero for exact gates, by Proposition 4.2(a)) and the first-chaos error grad w.

*Proof.* Apply (2A - 1) to the definition. Use (2A - 1)(Phi u) = Phi (2A - 1) u + 2 u A Phi + 2 grad Phi . grad u,
and (2A - 1) u = delta_{l-1} W_l. QED.

**Corollary 4.5.** If each injection is a single spectral class, delta[inj_l] = -2 p_l inj_l, then the final error and
defect telescope:
- w_L = sum_l T_{l->L} inj_l;
- delta_L = sum_l T_{l->L} dinj_l.

Here T is the chain's own mean-gate transport. An injection keeps its slope -1/(2 p_l) under transport, up to the
transported contamination. This is the theoretical basis of A-late's "inject in place".

## 5. (ii) Variational principle and Newton-type correction

**Proposition 5.1 (Newton is the Green formula; iteration is the tower; proved).**
- (a) **Linearity.** The map e -> delta(e) = (1 - 2A) e is affine on homogeneous point-mass-exact estimators, and
  e(0) + <G, delta(e)> = truth for all of them. The exact Newton step is the Green formula. Its dual solution, the
  Green measure, is explicit and network-independent. What is missing is delta off the base point.
- (b) **Each defect-descent step costs one more Laplacian.** Consider a correction e' = e + c delta(e). Evaluating
  delta(e')(0) requires (1 - 2A) delta(e) at 0, i.e. Lap delta(0) = -Lap e(0) - Lap^2 e(0) (using
  Lap(y.grad e)(0) = 2 Lap e(0)). So the k-th step needs Lap^k e(0): about 2 n^k chain runs, or the k-th order of
  the analytic defect, whose cost class is the k-th walk class (closed walks from k = 2; Theorem 7.2).
- (c) **Iterating is a Richardson iteration in A, so it is the tower.** With classes as in Theorem 4.1, one merge
  step e' = e - delta(e)/(2 p_bar) maps each component w_c to (1 - p_c/p_bar) w_c. So k steps leave
  (1 - p_c/p_bar)^k w_c:
  - the iteration converges for 0 < p_c < 2 p_bar;
  - it is slow exactly on the near-invisible classes (p_c << p_bar);
  - its k-th iterate is a degree-k polynomial in A applied to e, i.e. a member of the T_k tower, at the cost in (b).

  The best base-point correction is one class-resolved step (F5's T_cr). Its classes must come from an analytic
  decomposition of delta, not from iteration.
- (d) **The Gibbs form (Proposition 3.4(c)) has the same limitation.** The relative defect is the potential, and
  the maximizing control is grad log(e/v), which contains the answer. At |delta/e| ~ 1e-4 its content is the linear
  Green formula.

*Proof.*
- (a) F1 Corollary 2.
- (b) delta(e') = delta(e) + c (1 - 2A) delta(e), and at the base (1 - 2A) f(0) = f(0) - Lap f(0).
- (c) Theorem 4.1(2).
- (d) Proposition 3.4. QED.

**Verdict on (ii).** A variational principle exists, and the defect is its gradient (the potential of the Gibbs form,
the residual of the linear problem). No defect-descent iteration is cheaper than the tower. Newton is one exact
step, but it needs delta on the support of G. The heavy tail of G (Theorem 3.3(3)) rules out Taylor quadrature, and
the variance floor (F5 Theorem 5.1) rules out random quadrature.

## 6. (iii) Matrix controls

**Proposition 6.1 (matrix-controlled localizations commute; proved).**
- **Additivity.** For any adapted control C_t, with observation dY = C^T X dt + dW, the posterior is N(m_t, Sigma_t)
  with Lambda_t = Sigma_t^{-1} = I + int_0^t C C^T ds and theta_t = Lambda_t m_t = int_0^t C dY.
  - Both are additive in increments.
  - For deterministic controls, the terminal (Lambda, theta) has the law (I + A, N(A X, A)) given X, with
    A = int C C^T, whatever the order in which the controls act.
  - So localizations by different controls commute. The set of reachable states is a translate of the cone of
    increments, an abelian semigroup acting by translation.
- **Leaf space.** The scaling quotient of {(m, Sigma)} is a smooth manifold (R^n x SL-shape space); its dynamics has a
  shape attractor and is transient in the reduced mean. There are no dense orbits: type I.
- **The base point.** At Sigma = I every control commutes with the state. The base-point detector of control Q is
  <D(0, I), Q>, linear in Q.
- **The isotropic choice.** Isotropic Q is the O(n)-symmetric choice. Choosing a better Q needs the full matrix D,
  i.e. n(n+1)/2 directional defects, about 2e6 chain runs at n = 1024. That is infeasible even offline.

*Proof.* Gaussian conjugacy (Kalman-Bucy); conditionally on X, theta is Gaussian with mean A X and covariance A.
QED.

**Verdict on (iii).** This is a clean negative. Noncommutativity of Sigma_t with C_t affects only how the error
integral is parametrized, never what it equals, and never the leaf space.

## 7. Where noncommutativity genuinely enters, and what it changes

### 7.1 sl(2): smoothing versus scaling

Section 2 covers it. It is genuine, elementary, and organizational: it gives the eigenspaces, the ladder and the

### 7.2 The CCR lift: the defect is a dephasing Lindbladian

Let psi_{m,Sigma} be the real Gaussian wavefunction with |psi|^2 = N(m, Sigma). Let A^ = Op^W(a) be an operator
with Weyl symbol a(c, p).

**Proposition 7.1 (proved; checked symbolically).**
- (a) E(m, Sigma) := <psi | A^ | psi> = E_{c ~ N(m, Sigma), p ~ N(0, (4 Sigma)^{-1})} a(c, p).
- (b) H E = -(1/8) Sigma^{-1} E[grad_p^2 a] Sigma^{-1} = (1/8) Sigma^{-1} <psi| [Q, [Q, A^]] |psi> Sigma^{-1}.
- (c) Along Eldan's localization the Duhamel integrand is
  <D, Sigma_t^2> = (1/8) sum_j <psi_t| [Q_j, [Q_j, A^]] |psi_t> = -(1/4) <psi_t| L_deph(A^) |psi_t>, independent of
  the scale of Sigma_t. Here L_deph(X) = -(1/2) sum_j [Q_j, [Q_j, X]] generates the position-dephasing quantum Markov
  semigroup (the noncommutative heat semigroup of the derivations i[Q_j, .]).
- (d) Exactness (H E = 0) holds iff the symbol is p-independent, i.e. A^ lies in the position MASA.

*Proof.*
- (a) Write the kernel in centre / difference variables. The product psi(x) psi(x') = N(c; m, Sigma)
  exp(-d^T Sigma^{-1} d/8), and the d-integral gives the Gaussian in p.
- (b) N(c; m, Sigma) solves the heat equation, so H only sees the p-smearing. Then
  d_Sigma E_{N(0,P)} = (1/2) E grad_p^2 . dP with dP = -4 P dSigma P. Finally [x_j, Op(a)] = Op(i d_{p_j} a).
- (c) and (d) follow. QED.

*Check* (`n5_sym.txt`): (b) holds exactly for four polynomial symbols, and the direct wavefunction integral for
-d^2 gives 1/(4s).

**Why it is relabelling.** A chain gives E only on the (m, sI) slice. Lifts exist formally (deconvolve in c, invert a
Laplace transform in |p|^2), but they are non-unique. The defect is the same for every lift, and equal to the
commutative expression. The NC form computes nothing new. It does say that **exactness is commutation with the
position MASA, and the defect measures the momentum (coherence) content of any operator realization of the chain.**

### 7.3 The second-chaos algebra: vector states versus the trace

Let L(y) := grad v(y) = E grad F(y + Z) be the true first-chaos field at input mean y. Let c_k = E D^k F, and
H_i = c_2 of output i (n x n, indefinite).

**Theorem 7.2 (proved; checked).**
- (a) For outputs i, k, with rho = tau/(1 + tau):

      E_{y ~ N(0, tau I)} <L_i(y), L_k(y)> = sum_{j >= 0} rho^j/j! <c_{j+1}^{(i)}, c_{j+1}^{(k)}>
                                          = L_i . L_k + tau tr(H_i H_k) + O(tau^2).

  The linear coefficient is exact because rung 1 of the ladder (Lap L(0) = 0) kills the cross term.
- (b) For an open walk:

      E_{N(0,tau)} [L_i(y)^T H_j(y) L_k(y)] = (1 - tau/2) L_i^T H_j L_k + tau [ tr(H_i H_j H_k) + (c_3 . H . L terms) ] + O(tau^2).

  Rung 2 (Lap H(0) = -H) gives the factor (1 - tau/2). Closed walks enter at first order in tau.

*Proof.*
- (a) E_y[grad F(y + Z) grad F(y + Z')^T] is a correlation of a 0-homogeneous function at two Gaussian points with
  variance (1 + tau) I and cross-covariance tau I. Rescale to correlation rho and apply Mehler's formula
  term by term.
- (b) E_{N(0,tau)} f = f(0) + (tau/2) Lap f(0) + O(tau^2). Expand Lap of the triple product, then use
  d_m L_i = H_i e_m, Lap L(0) = 0 and Lap H(0) = -H. QED.

*Check* (`n5_trace.txt`; n = 12, L = 4, 4e5 samples per rho). The fitted linear coefficient of
E<L(y), L(y)> - |L|^2 is 1.18, against tr H_i^2 = 1.23 from Stein Monte Carlo (agreement within the fit noise).

**Reading.**
1. **The base point sees a vector state.** The base-point defect of a Gaussian readout is built from vector-state
   functionals of the second-chaos operators: <L, H L> and <H L, H L> (F5 Proposition 5.4).
2. **Localization mixes in the trace.** Localization at scale tau replaces the vector state by
   vector + tau x (trace o Ad H). So the closed walks, the trace-state moments tr(H_i H_j H_k) that F5 found
   invisible at first order, enter the error only through the tau-dependence of the shifted open walks. They are
   made at tau > 0 under the heavy-tailed G.
3. **Visibility.** X* (base-point visibility) <= the vector-state share of the error. Seeing the tracial part needs
   Lap delta(0) (2n^2 runs) or the closed walks themselves (n^4 per source-layer, note XL's wall).
4. **Random leaves are Hutchinson.** At L(y) = L + H y the leaf y is a Hutchinson probe of tr(H X H), and its
   variance is F5 Theorem 5.1's floor.

This is the one place where noncommutativity changes a conclusion: it says what the detector cannot see, and why
no localization buys it cheaply.

### R0. Verdict

- **The algebra is correct.** Thm 2.1, Cor 2.2 (forward direction), Thm 3.3 (density, Mellin symbol, tail), Prop 3.4 (Feynman-Kac, Boué-Dupuis optimizer e/v), Thm 4.1(1-3,5), Prop 4.4 and Thm 7.2 all re-derive cleanly.

### R1. Mathematical errors and imprecisions (minor)

1. **Sign error in Prop 5.1(a).** It states "e(0) + <G, delta(e)> = truth". Section 1 and F1 Cor 2 give truth - e(0) = -<G, delta>, so the correct statement is truth = e(0) - <G, delta(e)>. The map e -> delta(e) is linear; it is the set of point-mass-exact estimators that is affine.
2. **Thm 4.1(4).** It writes (1/2) Lap delta(0) = 2 sum p_c^2 w_c(0). The general identity is (1/2) Lap delta(0) = -sum p_c w_c(0) + 2 sum p_c^2 w_c(0), because Lap w_c(0) = (1 - 2p_c) w_c(0). The stated form holds only on the invisible combination sum p_c w_c(0) = 0, which the text intends but does not say.
3. **Prop 6.1** says "localizations by different controls commute". This is shown only for *deterministic* controls. For adapted (feedback) controls the increments of Lambda depend on the path, and the terminal law depends on the order. The conclusion for the base point is unaffected.
4. **Prop 3.2, "the truth is the vacuum of N on the position MASA".** The free dynamics e^{itN} rotates x into p, so it does not leave the position MASA invariant. A KMS or ground-state condition therefore cannot be stated on the MASA. What remains is the restriction of a state, i.e. the Gaussian law, i.e. the definition of the problem.
   - The frame's question was about the *localization* dynamics e^{TA}. That is a non-conservative Markov semigroup, not a *-automorphism group. So the KMS question was answered for a different dynamics.
   - The honest KMS-type structure of e^{TA} is reversibility (KMS symmetry), which the author did not state. Checked: A is symmetric on L^2(e^{|y|^2/2} dy), with output `<Af1,f2> - <f1,Af2> = 0`. Its ground state there is the Gaussian density, with eigenvalue -n/2 (checked: `A e^{-y^2/2}/e^{-y^2/2} = -1/2`). Under the ground-state transform, 1 - 2A becomes the harmonic oscillator -Lap + |y|^2/4 + 1 + n/2, with spectrum >= n + 1.
   - So the "ground state of the localization dynamics" is the *input law* S(delta_0), not the truth. The truth and every point-mass-exact error field have polynomial growth and lie outside this Hilbert space. This confirms the frame's negative and is commutative.
5. **Thm 2.1(c) overstates the algebra.** A, Lap, E, N and the heat and OU semigroups lie in span{K_-, K_0, 1}, which is the solvable (ax+b) Borel subalgebra. K_+ = |y|^2/2 never enters any statement used. "Metaplectic / Howe dual pair" is correct but decorative. The content is [Lap, E] = 2 Lap, i.e. heat time scales like length squared.
6. **Citation.** Thomsen's groupoid KMS-weights paper is arXiv:1306.5118, not 1109.2336, which I could not confirm. Christensen arXiv:2005.01792 (Neshveyev's theorem for KMS weights) is correctly cited.
7. **Thm 7.2 check is weak but the theorem is right.** In the MC check, (lhs - |L|^2)/rho = 1.105, 1.076, 1.30, 1.26, 1.37. Mehler forces this ratio to be nondecreasing with every term >= tr H^2 = 1.23, so the first two points are noise. The theorem itself is exact. An analytic check: for F = sum_m a_m relu(theta_m . x), the exact value is sum a a' theta.theta' [1/4 + arcsin(rho cos_mm')/(2 pi)], whose rho-coefficient sum a a' (theta.theta')^2/(2 pi |theta||theta'|) is exactly <c_2, c_2>.

### R2. Overclaims about noncommutativity

1. **Thm 7.2's "vector state vs tracial state" is commutative at the order where it is used.**
   - For symmetric matrices tr(H_i H_j H_k) is fully symmetric in (i, j, k). Checked: tr ABC = tr ACB = -30.86188. The open walks L^T H L and L^T H^2 L are likewise symmetric tensor contractions.
   - Operator ordering first matters at degree 4: checked tr ABAB = 327 against tr AABB = 1655. Degree 4 is beyond what Thm 7.2 uses.
   - So "vector state vs trace on the noncommuting H_i" is the open-walk vs closed-walk distinction of notes XL/XLI and F5 Prop 5.4 under a new name. The formula itself is Mehler's expansion of the gradient field's covariance.
   - The "visibility ceiling X* <= vector-state share" is not derived from Thm 7.2. It restates SYNTHESIS Cor 7.
2. **Internal inconsistency: "seeing the tracial part costs Lap delta(0) (about 2n^2 runs)" (sections 0.5, 7.3(3), summary).**
   - F5 and SYNTHESIS assign closed walks the first-order weight 0, i.e. 2p_c ~ 0.
   - By the author's own Thm 4.1(4), Lap delta sees class c with weight p_c(2p_c - 1), which also vanishes as p_c -> 0. A p -> 0 class is seen by no local base-point probe of any order. Its Green weight int (1+tau)^{-1-p} dtau = 1/p is carried entirely by the heavy tail of G.
   - So "pay 2n^2 runs for Lap delta" is not a route to the tracial part. The only routes are computing the closed walks directly, or importance-sampling G's tail.
   - Also, an exact p = 0 class would contradict Green exactness (Cor 2.2 plus point-mass exactness), so "weight 0" is a first-order statement in the single-readout model, not an eigenvalue.
3. **Prop 7.1 (CCR lift).** The author correctly calls this relabelling. I agree. One fact survives: exactness means commuting with the position MASA.
4. **KMS, Poincaré series and modular cocycle (section 3).** These are correct names for a Laplace-transformed dilation semigroup (G~ = gamma o (1-E)^{-1}) and a Doob transform. None of them changes a computation, as the author says.
5. **Novelty.** The S-transform taking N to E is textbook white-noise analysis (Kuo: Gamma(lambda) <-> dilation), so Thm 2.1(b) is not new. Thm 3.3 is an exercise once that is known. Mehler, Leibniz (Prop 4.4), Doob and Feynman-Kac are standard. New-in-context: the Mellin reading err = -sum E delta~_q/(1-q), and Prop 4.4 as a bookkeeping identity.

### R3. The pointwise-slope finding is misread (substantive; checked)

Section 4.6 makes three claims:
- (2) no mixture fits a slope that flattens with tau ("proved");
- (3) the reading that "a p ~ 1 class mixes in at the base, a p ~ 2 class dominates away";
- (4) "cannot be exploited (proved)".

Two problems:
- **(a) Claim 2 holds only for same-sign (aligned) mixtures.** With an *opposed* fast class it fails.
  - Two radial eigenclasses w_c = S|x|^{q_c} in R^8 (A w = (q/2) w verified to 5 digits for q = 1, -0.6, -2.6, -7). p = (1.8, 4), amplitude ratio 4:1, opposite signs. Slope of w on delta, pooled over y ~ N(0, tau I):
    `opposed: tau=0 -0.469 | 0.25 -0.355 | 1 -0.299 | 4 -0.286 | 16 -0.278`
  - The same pair aligned: `-0.223 -0.239 -0.258 -0.266 -0.278` (steepens, as the author says).
  - The opposed mixture reproduces the measured GC s0 trajectory (-0.472, -0.400, -0.331, -0.282) to within the scatter between networks.
- **(b) The author's reading (3) contradicts Thm 4.1(1).** E w_c ~ (1+tau)^{1/2 - p_c}, so a p ~ 1 class *outgrows* a p ~ 2 class away from the base. "p ~ 2 dominates away, p ~ 1 mixes in at the base" is backwards.
  - The reading consistent with the data and the theorem: a dominant slow class (p ~ 1.8, the universal -0.28) plus a fast class (p ~ 4) of opposite sign.
  - The fast class partly cancels in w(0) but is amplified by 2p = 8 in delta(0). That is exactly what makes the base coefficient steep and network-dependent.
- **Consequence for (4).** "Cannot be exploited" is too strong.
  - Two signed classes are identifiable from two base-point functionals, delta(0) and Lap delta(0) (Thm 4.1(4) in its correct form, R1.2).
  - They are also identifiable from the radial profile b(s) at two s, since the per-class s^{p_c} are distinct.
  - Both are costly at n = 1024: 2n^2 runs, or random leaves. So the practical verdict stands. The diagnosis changes, though: the non-universality of the base slope comes from cancellation by an opposed fast class, not "base-point class mixing" of positive classes.
  - A cheap offline test (below) can check whether calibrating the slope at the dominant class's p (about -0.28 for GC) and treating the rest as a counterterm beats the fitted base slope out of sample.

### R4. The transport-slope evidence is partly tautological

- **"Transported slope" is a regression of T inj_l on T dinj_l with one fixed linear map T** (base-point mean-gate transport). If inj_l = a dinj_l + noise, transport preserves a up to how T reweights neurons. So P-N5-2 tests only how much the slope varies across neurons, not the in-place design.
- **The last layer is counted with T = identity.** Local equals transported exactly there (e.g. l15 -0.511/-0.511, l7 -0.50/-0.50), which inflates the "agreement" tallies.
- **On non-trivial heavy layers the agreement is marginal.** GC L16 s2 l9: -0.455 vs -0.321 (29%, share 6.3%). s1 l9: 22%.
- **The per-layer explained share falls to 0.49-0.79 at layers 13-15 in GC L16.** It is not only K3 that weakens.
- **Prop 4.4's "product of two errors" is wrong for the dominant term.**
  - In 2 grad Phi . grad u, grad Phi is the O(1) gate gradient and only grad u = (grad w_{l-1}) W_l is an error. So that term is first order, the same order as dinj itself.
  - Only u (A Phi) is second order.
  - So the 3-15% is an empirical ratio with no structural reason to stay small. It grew with depth at L8, and it was never measured at L16 or on K3 (as the text admits). P-N5-1's "reasoning" line should be withdrawn.

### R5. Cost and feasibility errors in E-N5

1. **G noise is underestimated about 50x.**
   - From DATA_FINDINGS: 2^20 samples, a first-chaos share of 0.215 at layer 15, and E h^2 ~ 1.7 Var h.
   - Per-neuron relative noise: |G_i noise| / |G_i| ~ sqrt(n/2^20) sqrt(1.7/0.215) ~ 0.09. The text's "1e-3" is the relative noise of the *variance* statistics, not of G.
   - The signal grad w is the chain's first-chaos error, ~1e-3 relative (the chain's variance error is 1.5e-3 at layer 15).
   - So the contamination's dominant term is unmeasurable per neuron. Its projection onto grad Phi_i has a noise-to-signal ratio of at least about 2 (aligned) and up to about 60 (random alignment).
   - A raw "upper bound" would exceed 0.3 from noise alone and spuriously fire the decision rule "lower A-late expectations".
2. **Fix (cheap, for AWS).**
   - Rerun the MC cache for network 0 with G stored as two independent halves (2 x 2^20, the same job as mcc1).
   - Estimate |contam|^2 with the split-half cross product <contam(G_a), contam(G_b)>, summed over neurons.
   - Report the noise floor from contam(G_a - G_b).
   - Decide P-N5-1 only if the cross estimate exceeds 3x its floor standard error. Otherwise report "unresolved", not an upper bound.
3. **The kink.** The smoothness pilot found a ~1e-7 kink from layer 9-10 that dominates second differences. E-N5 needs Lap Phi_l and delta_l by second differences at exactly layers 9-15. Unless "mask-frozen" provably removes the tier-2-compression kink, which it is not stated to do, E-N5 must require (h, h/2) stability of delta_l and Lap Phi_l at each of layers 9-15 (corr > 0.99) before any slope is read.
4. **Unit arithmetic checks out:**
   - 2n^2 runs x 208 units ~ 4.4e8 units;

### R7. Strongest surviving idea, corrected

- **(i) Spectral reading of the detector, corrected.** The pointwise slope data fit a dominant slow class plus an opposed fast class. The base slope's network dependence is cancellation, not positive mixing.
  - Testable offline at small n on the existing `n5_pw_gc.pkl`: fit two signed classes per network on tau in {0, 0.25, 1, 4}.
