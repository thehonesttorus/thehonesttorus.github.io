# The free-group-factor construction read into v56: conditioning, the crossed-product state, and where the error is

Working note XLVII. It applies the free group factor paper (note XLVI), the user's two-mover refinement, and the
crossed-product reading of the proof study to the production estimator. Each section is a faithful test of one
structural claim. Runs: Modal (`infra/modal/mrun.py`); networks 0-1, where the 1.6e7-input truth exists.

## 2. Conditioning: the two-mover prediction on the chain's own matrices

The refinement says:
- one moving generator gives the gapless free Poisson(1) law and needs a spectral cutoff;
- pooling two independent sources gives free Poisson(2), which is gapped.

On the chain's matrices (network 0):

| layer | active fraction | C_pre: λ_min/mean, eigenvalues < 1e-3 mean | full K_post: λ_min/mean | K_AA (α > -2.5): λ_min/mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.90 | 1.5e-4, 147 (104 dead) | 2.6e-8 | 2.5e-3 |
| 14 | 0.80 | -1.3e-4, 249 (210 dead) | 5.7e-15 | 1.7e-3 |

**What holds.**
- The pre-activation covariance is W_A K_AA W_A^T with a rectangular W_A of about 0.8 n columns. Its near-null space
  is the dead upstream neurons.
- On the active upstream block, the post-activation covariance is gapped, as predicted.
- C_pre is not even positive at layer 14.
- The Schur hub inverts C_pre with a cutoff ε = 1e-3 mean(var), while genuine range eigenvalues reach about 2e-5.

**The output-level test** (`outputs/hub_cutoff_net0.txt`). Varying ε moves the raw MSE by at most 0.5%:
ε = 1e-5 gives -0.17%, 1e-4 gives -0.24%, 1e-2 gives +0.55%. Y carries almost no content in the near-null
directions, so the gapped upstream formulation (u = W_A^+ y, then u^T K_AA^-1 u) is worth at most about 0.3%. It was
not built. The structure is confirmed; this component is not a lever.

## 3. The crossed-product (Wiener-chaos) state (`code/chaos_state.py`, `outputs/chaos_state_net{0,1}.txt`)

**The reading.** L(F_(n+1)) is the free-product crossed product N ⋊ F_n, with N generated by the conjugates
A_g S A_g^*. The network analogue:
- the input x is the free system;
- each fold (m, a) is a rank-one second-chaos source (1/2) J2 ((l_a . x)^2 - |l_a|^2), with J2 the wall density;
- the sources are conjugated forward by the first-jet transports and carried as histories (no compressed basis);
- D3 and D21 are exact Wick contractions of the state.

**Unrenormalized** (arms U = L^l l_s from the input's first chaos):

| network 0 | layer 2 | layer 8 | layer 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| D21 R2 vs truth | 84% | 37% | 24% |
| scale needed | 1.4 | 3.9 | 6.5 |
| covariance explained by the first chaos | 90% | 61% | 43% |

At depth most variance lives in higher chaos. This is EFPTZ's point: the Wick map must be taken against the *true*
law. The renormalized arms are the transported full covariance, Cov(z^l, z^m_a). With them, the state is algebraically
the chain's A/P legs, uncompressed.

**v56's own slices against the same truth.** D3 R2 is 99.6-100%, D21 R2 99.4-99.9% at every layer. A small multiple
(-0.04) of (chaos - chain) explains 15-26% of the chain's D21 residual. That residual is 0.6% of the slice energy.

**Reading.** The histories-not-modes structure is what the chain already implements for the third cumulant. Rebuilding
it natively reproduces the chain; the slice representation is not where the score is.
