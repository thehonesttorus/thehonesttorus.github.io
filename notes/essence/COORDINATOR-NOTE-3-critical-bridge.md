## 1. Three regimes of local-to-global, one invariant

The age-graded memory operator of a local-move system has singular values μ_a (a = how many steps old the content is). Their decay sorts every instance we have studied into three regimes. The carrier cost is ∝ Σ_a μ_a-resolution.

| regime | where | memory μ_a | NCG object | what computes the global quantity | known unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact algebra (below the wall) | Dynkin quivers, periodic/Pisot tilings (Fibonacci: pure point spectrum), planar dimers | finitely many nonzero | finite-rank commutators; K-theory, gap labelling, index theorems | exact invariants from local data | Kasteleyn (#P → P), frequency modules, Perron vectors, Laurent phenomenon |
| critical (on the wall) | He-ReLU networks (measured), Thue–Morse-type singular continuous order, critical spin systems | ∝ 1/a (Dixmier ideal L^{1,∞}) | Dixmier trace; blockwise Fredholm modules | multiscale carriers with frozen block frames, cost ∝ N log N | FMM, hierarchical matrices, multigrid, wavelets, MERA (all geometric or quantum; none for pseudorandom dynamics) |
| gapped (above the wall) | expanders, Ramanujan graphs, high-temperature spin systems, Rudin–Shapiro-type Lebesgue spectrum | ∝ λ^a | trace-class commutators; mixing lemma; trickle-down | truncation at O(log 1/ε) steps | expander mixing, PRGs, spectral independence, sparsifiers |

- **Synthesis.** Off the wall, carriers are cheap: either the memory is finite (exact algebra) or it decays geometrically (gap). On the wall the total resolution diverges logarithmically, and the Dixmier trace is the right integral (coordinator note 2). Every classical local-to-global unlock lives off the wall. **He-initialised ReLU sits exactly on it** (χ₁ = 1; team C), which is why every off-the-shelf unlock failed here (teams A–E).
- **Measured.** Team C's trichotomy is this table restricted to our network: the dilation sector is the invariant, non-decaying direction; the free sector decays geometrically in energy (×(1.02–1.10)·g³ per step) but its rank follows a power law (n/(2(a + 1))), which is what makes the total resolution logarithmic (team D: 2n/a lossless).
- **Tilings carry all three regimes.** Fibonacci (pure point), Thue–Morse (singular continuous) and Rudin–Shapiro (Lebesgue: deterministic, zero entropy, yet random-looking correlations) are the exact-algebra, critical and pseudorandom cases of one theory. Aperiodic order is the place where structured pseudorandomness is already classified by spectral type; the conjecture is that the spectral type of a local-move system's natural Fredholm module predicts its carrier cost.

## 2. The user's F identified: trees, boundaries, ghosts

- **Julg–Valette (fact; also Pimsner–Voiculescu, Cuntz).** For a tree with a base point x₀, let F pair each vertex v ≠ x₀ with the edge from v towards x₀, on ℓ²(vertices) ⊕ ℓ²(edges). F² = 1 (up to the rank-one base point), and [F, g] is finite rank for every tree automorphism g. This is literally "a shift on sequences" (one step towards the root along each geodesic ray) with F² = 1, and the tree's boundary is a Cantor set whose rays are compared by tail equivalence. It proves K-amenability of free groups. Connes ch. 4 relates summability of boundary Fredholm modules to critical exponents and Hausdorff dimensions of limit sets (Patterson–Sullivan; verify the exact statements in §3 and §5).
- **Ramanujan = optimal quotients of the cover (fact).** Ramanujan graphs are finite quotients of the (q + 1)-regular tree whose nontrivial spectrum sits inside the tree's [−2√q, 2√q] (Alon–Boppana). The Dynkin "wall at 2" is the same statement for the path, whose universal cover is ℤ with spectral radius 2. Pseudorandomness, in this sense, is "as good as the universal cover".
- **Our network in this picture (synthesis, measured parts marked).** The computation graph's universal cover is a tree of paths. The Bethe/annealed value is the cover value (team A, measured: cosine 0.88 with the true old D21 at depth). The memory is the **loops**: two paths from a birth to one target neuron, i.e. costate's double edge. The loop (non-backtracking, Ihara–Hashimoto) operator of the layered network should carry team C's trichotomy: a Perron outlier (dilation, eigenvalue 1), a bulk inside a Ramanujan-type disc (the free sector, g³ per step), and no elliptic part.
- **Ghosts (fact, Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis).** For an expander family, the projection onto constants, ⊕_k P_k, is a ghost operator: its entries vanish at infinity but it is not compact, and it obstructs the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture. It is built from finite-rank blocks (each P_k has rank one). **Our memory is a ghost of the same kind**: per-entry size n^{-1/2}, incoherent, not compact for the global gauge polarization (team A, width-flat), yet finite rank blockwise once the frame is frozen per age block (region §9: Tucker rank n/4 lossless for ages 5–8). The working carrier was built exactly the way HLS ghosts are built.
- **Hyperfiniteness (fact; Connes 1989, verify): finitely summable spectral triples force a hypertrace, which fails for non-amenable group algebras.** Fresh Gaussian layers generate free (non-amenable-like) structure as n → ∞. Conjecture: this is the theorem-level reason no global finitely summable carrier of the memory exists, and the escape is a hyperfinite (blockwise, AF-like) approximation along the age axis, where the structure is amenable (one-dimensional, Dixmier-critical).

## 3. The general principle (conjecture schema)

**Blockwise Fredholm carriers at criticality.** Let H carry a filtration of conditional expectations (layers, ages, scales) with martingale blocks H_j, and let T be the global object (here the memory's action).
- **Admissibility.** For each block j there is a frozen polarization F_j = 2P_j − 1 with rank P_j = r_j such that the part of T living in block j commutes with F_j up to error ε in the price norm (here the Frobenius/tracial norm the fresh-weight lemma makes the currency).
- **Free-probability admissibility (conjecture).** For products of free random operators separated by age a, r_j ≈ c(ε)·n/a. Evidence: team D (2n/a lossless, n/a 2.5× worse), team C (participation ratio n/(2(a + 1)) to 1 % at ages ≤ 5), region (n/4 lossless for ages 5–8).
- **Cost.** Σ_j r_j × (block dimension) = O(n³ log L) on the wall (the Dixmier coefficient), O(n³) off it.
- **What is new relative to hierarchical matrices, FMM and MERA.** Their admissibility comes from geometry (smooth kernels far from the diagonal) or from locality (quantum chains). Here it would come from **freeness**: blocks are low rank because free products concentrate their energy in rank ∝ n/age. That would extend hierarchical compression from geometric kernels to the quenched statistics of deep random computations: random matrix products, deep linear and ReLU networks, random circuits, kernel methods at the edge of chaos.

## 4. Targets that would go beyond the existing unlocks (each with a test)

- **G1. Pseudorandomness beyond the gap (team B).** Jeronimo–Mittal–Roy's proof splits into a mean term and a λ term, and needs λ < 1. Show that on the wall the λ term is a ghost with Dixmier-critical blockwise frozen-frame rank, and give an estimator for product functions over gapless walks with O(log N) blocks. *Test:* a gapless family (long cycles, or our layered graph) where truncation fails; measure the λ term's blockwise rank by dyadic block of walk length and the blockwise estimator's error.
- **G2. A Friedman–Bordenave theorem for layered random computations (team C).** Identify the non-backtracking loop operator of the layered network; state the conjecture that its bulk radius equals the universal-cover bound (g³ per step) up to o(1), with the dilation as the only outlier. That would give provable truncation and compression error bounds for any layered random computation. *Test:* the loop operator's spectrum at n = 256/512/1024, against team C's measured gains.
- **G3. Summability exponent = carrier cost exponent (team E).** Build the Julg–Valette-type module on the dyadic age tree, compute its critical exponent from the measured ranks (prediction: exactly 1, i.e. Dixmier), and predict the carrier cost at depth 32. *Test:* the smoke-test shape 256 × 32 (bench set `w256_d32`): does the blockwise carrier's cost grow as L log L?
- **G4. Loop series around Bethe, compressed by length (team A).** Chertkov–Chernyak loop calculus writes an exact partition function as Bethe × (1 + Σ over generalised loops). Our memory is the loop series around the Bethe (cover) value. Is each loop-length class (= age) blockwise low rank in a frozen frame? If so, that is a new evaluation principle for loop series at criticality, beyond truncation at short loops. *Test:* team A's Bethe-split machinery, per loop length.
- **G5. Dixmier-critical trickle-down (team F).** Team F's Theorem F1 bounds the global gap by ∏(1 − (1 + η_m)/m). On the wall local gaps close; state the version where the global gap closes only logarithmically, and ask which sampling or counting problem at a uniqueness threshold it would improve.
- **G6. Rigor (team G).** The blockwise version of note 2's dictionary, the hypertrace obstruction for our weight algebra, and the log-L lower bound.
