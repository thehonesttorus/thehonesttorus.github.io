# "An isomorphism of the free group factors" (OpenAI, 23 September 2026): the argument, checked step by step

Working note XLVI. A reading of the paper, reconstructed in my own words. Each step was checked by hand on a full
read; this is not a formal verification. The last section is what its constructs say to our estimator. The note was
written at the user's request after an expert in the field called the paper correct.

## The claim

- L(F_n) ≅ L(F_(n+1)) for every n ≥ 3 (Theorem 1.1).
- Hence L(F_2) ≅ L(F_3) (Theorem 1.2).
- By the Dykema-Rădulescu dichotomy, all interpolated free group factors, L(F_∞) included, are isomorphic and have
  fundamental group R_>0.
- Corollary: none of Voiculescu's free entropy dimensions (δ, δ_0, δ*, δ⋆) is an invariant of the von Neumann algebra.
  Each takes every value n ≥ 2 on generating tuples of L(F_2).

## The idea in one paragraph

Start with free Haar unitaries (A_1, ..., A_n, C) generating M = L(F_(n+1)). The goal is to swallow the extra
generator C: find n free Haar unitaries that still generate all of M.

The paper does it with a trace-preserving automorphism that moves every A_j by at most ε in operator norm while
making some long word A_w in the moved tuple approximate C. A Nielsen move then trades C for the word. Iterating with
shrinking budgets produces a norm-convergent n-tuple, and the limit is free Haar and generates M.

The quantitative heart: a word with m occurrences of x_1 at freely independent prefixes spreads the required
displacement over m free translates. Each needs only O(1/√m). This works because a sum of m free Haar unitaries,
scaled by 1/√m, is circular, and its |.|^2 spectrum (Marchenko-Pastur with ratio 1) has no atom at zero.

## The construction, step by step (with what was checked)

**1. Conjugates of a bounded logarithm are free (Lemma 2.3).**
- Put S = arg C, self-adjoint, ‖S‖ ≤ π, τ(S) = 0, e^{iS} = C, and S_g = A_g S A_g^* for g ∈ F_n.
- The algebras A_g W*(C) A_g^* form a free family.
- Check: this is the group fact that the normal closure of c in F_(n+1) is freely generated by its conjugates
  g c g^(-1), g ∈ F_n (Reidemeister-Schreier), transported to the group algebra.

**2. Free sums have support-independent norm (Lemma 2.5).**
- ‖Σ k(g) S_g‖ ≤ 3π ‖k‖_ℓ2. This comes from ‖Σ z_j‖ ≤ 2 (Σ‖z_j‖_2^2)^{1/2} + max‖z_j‖ for centred free self-adjoint z_j.
- Check: left multiplication splits into creation, annihilation and diagonal blocks on the reduced-tensor
  decomposition of L^2. The creation blocks have orthogonal ranges, which gives the square-root sum. The diagonal
  blocks act on orthogonal subspaces, which gives the maximum. This is standard free Khintchine (Ricard-Xu).

**3. A trace-preserving flow (Section 3).**
- Choose real finitely supported coefficients h_j ∈ ℓ^2(F_n). Define the Fox-type cocycle D_(x_j) = h_j,
  D_(gb) = D_g + λ(g) D_b.
- Flow: dA_j/dt = i s_t(h_j) A_j, with s_t(k) = Σ k(g) A_g(t) S A_g(t)^*, and S fixed.

The flow preserves the trace, in three steps:
- (a) *A universal first-order identity at t = 0 (Lemma 3.2).* On the free *-algebra the flow is a derivation δ with
  δ z_g = i[σ(D_g), z_g]. Monomials with nonidentity group label have zero trace at t = 0; identity-label monomials
  are polynomials in the z_g. For each g, δ rotates S_g by a unitary built from the other S_b, which are free from S_g.
  Conjugating by a unitary of an algebra free from S_g preserves the joint law, so each term's derivative vanishes.
  Hence τ ∘ ev_0 ∘ δ^r = 0 for all r ≥ 1.
- (b) *Analyticity with a radius uniform in t (Lemma 3.4).* The derivative bounds use only unitarity and ‖S‖. So
  f_p(t) = τ(ev_t(p)) is real-analytic, with radius bounded below independently of t.
- (c) All derivatives vanish at 0, so f_p is constant near 0, and therefore on all of R by continuation.

Notably, freeness is used only at t = 0, never along the flow. Isometry follows from ‖b‖ = lim τ((b*b)^k)^{1/(2k)}.
Surjectivity comes from running the flow backwards. Normal extension follows.

Two estimates come out:
- ‖β_t(A_j) - A_j‖ ≤ |t| 3π ‖h_j‖;
- ‖β_t(A_w) - e^{itS} A_w‖ ≤ |t| 3π ‖D_w - δ_e‖.

So at t = 1, a word with D_w ≈ δ_e is moved to approximately C A_w.

**4. A small cocycle with a prescribed word value (Lemma 4.1).** This is the combinatorial core.
- Take a = x_1 and b_j = x_2^j x_3 x_2^(-j). Then p_j = (a b_1)...(a b_j) are free generators: this uses n ≥ 3, and
  the free inverse automorphisms φ, ψ of Lemma 4.2.
- For w_m = p_m a, with only h_1 = h nonzero, the cocycle is D_(w_m) = T_m h with T_m = 1 + Σ λ(p_j).
- τ((T_m T_m^*)^r) = Cat_r m^r + O(m^(r-1)). So the spectrum of T_m T_m^*/m tends to Marchenko-Pastur(1),
  (2π)^(-1) √((4-x)/x) on (0, 4), which has no atom at 0.
- Cut the spectrum below d·m and invert above it: h = T_m^* R_m δ_e gives ‖h‖^2 ≤ 1/(dm) and
  ‖T_m h - δ_e‖^2 = μ_m([0, d]) → ν([0, d]), which can be made small.
- Check: the moment count is the free central limit theorem for m free Haar unitaries. Every pair that survives free
  reduction is matched noncrossingly with opposite exponents. Real coefficients follow from commutation with complex
  conjugation, and the finite truncation is harmless.

**5. One-step absorption (Proposition 5.1).**
- With β = β_1 and the Nielsen automorphism γ: A_j ↦ A_j, C ↦ C A_w, set α = γ^(-1) β.
- Then ‖α(A_j) - A_j‖ < ε and ‖α(A)_w - C‖ < ε: the old C is now nearly a word in the new tuple.
- The new completion is C' = C A_w^*, and the tuple is still freely generating Haar.
- Check: γ^(-1) fixes the A_j, sends C A_w to C, and is isometric.

**6. The adaptive limit (Section 5.2).**
- At stage k:
  1. approximate the first k targets y_j of a dense sequence by polynomials F_(j,k) in the current (A, C);
  2. pick the tolerance ε_k from their Lipschitz constants;
  3. absorb C into a word w_k;
  4. substitute that word for C, giving G_(j,k) polynomials in the A's only;
  5. only then fix the future budget r_k from the G's Lipschitz constants.
- The A's converge in norm. The limit is free Haar, since word traces are norm-continuous. The y_j lie in the L^2
  closure of its word algebra, so the conditional expectation onto the generated algebra is the identity, and the
  limit generates M.
- The completions C^(k) need not converge.
- Check: the budget ordering is exactly what makes the word-length growth harmless, and the inequalities (5.4)-(5.9)
  close.

**7. From n = 3, 4 to n = 2.**
- L(F_3) ≅ L(F_4) ≅ L(F_5). Amplify by √2, using N_s^t ≅ N_(1+(s-1)/t^2): N_3^√2 = N_2 and N_5^√2 = N_3.
- Note that n = 2 is never handled directly.

**Verdict.** I found no gap. The steps that carry the weight are 3(a), 4 and 6, and each closes:
- the label reduction and the free-conjugation invariance in 3(a);
- the Marchenko-Pastur no-atom fact in 4;
- the ordering of budget choices in 6.

The proof is short because it never tracks freeness along the deformation. It needs:
- one exact infinitesimal identity at the free point;
- a uniform analyticity radius;
- spectral invertibility of a free sum;
- adaptive bookkeeping.

## Why it reverses thirty years

- The programme to distinguish the factors sought W*-invariants, chiefly free entropy dimension. Corollary 7.1 shows
  those quantities depend on the generating tuple: the same factor carries generating tuples of every dimension n ≥ 2.
- The positive direction needed none of the heavy machinery. It needed:
  - Voiculescu-type trace-preserving polynomial flows (cyclomorphy);
  - Fox calculus to compute how a word responds to the flow;
  - one free-probability spectral fact.
- The expert's remark that "this idea can solve other related problems" fits the shape of the method. It applies
  wherever an extra free generator can be made a limit of words in a perturbed tuple. Shlyakhtenko's recent
  identifications of Fuchsian group factors use a related "free complement plus limiting generation" pattern.

## What its constructs say to our estimator

There is no direct computational transfer: the paper is about isomorphism classes of II_1 factors, not about computing
moments. Three of its constructs coincide structurally with objects we already use, and I state the correspondences
without claiming more.

1. **The cocycle is the same algebraic object as our error cocycle.**
   - Theirs: D_(gb) = D_g + λ(g) D_b, i.e. b(gh) = b(g) + R_g b(h).
   - Ours (stage 15): δ_L = Σ_l T_L ... T_(l+1) β_l, with births β_l transported by the first jet.
   - Their Lemma 4.1 solves the *inverse* problem: choose small per-occurrence inputs h whose transported sum hits a
     target. It works because the transport sum T_m has an atom-free bulk spectrum and can be inverted above a cutoff.
   - Our transport T = Φ ⊙ W is a product of He matrices with gates, whose W^T W has the ratio-one Marchenko-Pastur
     spectrum (note XXV). The analogous statement for us: many small per-layer corrections can add coherently to an
     O(1) output effect, which is why fitted counterterms have leverage at all.
   - The no-free-lunch corollary of stage 15, together with note XLV's Howe-duality reading, says that leverage
     reaches only the isotropic sector.
2. **Invariance from one point.** The proof pattern "exact first-order identity at the reference, plus a uniform
   analyticity radius, gives exact invariance along the whole flow" proves that a deformation preserves a law without
   controlling the deformed state. For us, it is the right way to certify a *task-preserving* change of
   representation (the branching note's Σ I_α^*(A_α) = A): verify the identity at the Gaussian reference, then
   control the derivative bounds. This is a proof tool, not a FLOP saving.
3. **Free CLT invertibility.**
   - The fact that makes small coefficients enough is that a free sum of m unitaries has |.|^2 spectrum with no atom
     at zero.
   - In our setting, the analogous object is the transport sum over source ages that the legs compress. Its spectrum
     governs how much each age must be resolved.
   - The measured cliff of the ranks (note XXIV) and the "a fifth of directions damped tenfold" of note XXV are
     statements about the small-spectrum end that the paper truncates.
