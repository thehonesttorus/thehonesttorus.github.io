# Faces design (fresh slate): barycentric face closure

*Design stream "faces", v0, 1 Oct 2026. Principle: faces, barycentres and conditional expectations. Follows notes/fresh-slate/BRIEF.md. Status labels as in the programme: **Exact** (proved here, checked numerically in `check_exact.py`), **Gluing** (an approximation, with its error term named), **Prediction** (to be measured).*

---

## 1. The principle, stated precisely

Network: $a_0=x\sim N(0,I_n)$, $z_l=a_{l-1}W_l$, $a_l=\mathrm{ReLU}(z_l)$, $g_l=\mathbf 1\{z_l>0\}$ (the gate, or face, variable of layer $l$).

**P (faces and barycentres).** Let $\mathcal G_l=\sigma(g_l)$ be the σ-algebra generated by the face variable of layer $l$. On each face $\{g_l=g\}$ the activation is $a_l=g\odot z_l$, linear in $z_l$; the conditional expectation onto $\mathcal G_l$ is the barycentric projection $Y\mapsto\sum_g \mathbf 1\{g_l=g\}\,\mathbb E[Y\mid g_l=g]$. The arrow (ReLU at layer $l$ followed by $W_{l+1}$) acts on barycentres linearly and exactly:
$$\mathbb E[z_{l+1}\mid \mathcal G_l]=\mathbb E[a_l\mid\mathcal G_l]\,W_{l+1}.$$
The programme's local-to-global principle says the exponentially many faces need not be enumerated: **local face data** (per neuron: the mass and barycentre of its own half-face; per pair of neurons: the joint face masses and the cross-barycentres) are glued into the global answer by a rule whose error is a *connected* higher-order face interaction, which is small when the face law is weakly dependent (the measured bounded spectral independence of the gate law, BRIEF §3).

Where P is used essentially: (i) the state is face data, not moments of activations; (ii) the non-Gaussianity of the next layer is produced *only* by the gate field (Section 3); (iii) the gluing error is a face-sufficiency defect (Section 5), the finite form of the coboundary criterion of conditional-arrow-algebra.md §6.

## 2. Exact identities used

**E1 (arrow transports barycentres).** For any event $A$ in the σ-algebra of layer $\le l$: $\mathbb E[z_{l+1}\mathbf 1_A]=\mathbb E[a_l\mathbf 1_A]W_{l+1}$. Exact.

**E2 (barycentres are facet integrals; mean = Laplacian mass).** For a polyhedral cone $F$ with outward unit facet normals $\nu_e$: $\mathbb E[x\mathbf 1_F]=-\sum_{e}\nu_e\,\gamma_{n-1}(e)$, $\gamma_{n-1}$ the Gaussian surface measure (Gaussian integration by parts). For any positively homogeneous network output $f$ (degree 1), Euler $x\cdot\nabla f=f$ and Stein give
$$\mathbb E f=\mathbb E[\Delta f],\qquad \Delta a_{L,j}=\sum_{l\le L,\,i}\delta(z_{l,i})\,\|\nabla_x z_{l,i}\|^2\,\frac{\partial a_{L,j}}{\partial a_{l,i}},$$
so the mean of every neuron is a sum over upstream *facets* (codimension-1 faces, the kinks) of facet density × gradient norm × downstream Jacobian, a conditional expectation on each facet. In Gaussian closure the facet term of neuron $j$ is $\sigma_j\varphi(\mu_j/\sigma_j)$ and the rest is $\mu_j\Phi(\mu_j/\sigma_j)$; E2 is what tells us which face measures are the vertex coefficients of any closure: masses $\mathbb E[\mathrm{ReLU}'(z)]$ (codim 0), facet densities $\mathbb E[\mathrm{ReLU}''(z)]$ (codim 1), and their derivatives (higher codimension).

**E3 (barycentric decomposition of an activation).** With $m_k=\mathbb E[z_k\mid g_k=1]$ (barycentre of the own half-face) and $r_k=g_k(z_k-m_k)$:
$$a_k=m_k g_k+r_k,\qquad \mathbb E[r_k\mid g_k]=0 .$$
Exact. Hence, with $p=\mathbb E g$, $\Gamma=\mathrm{Cov}(g)$, $X=\mathrm{Cov}(g,r)$, $\Xi=\mathrm{Cov}(r)$:
$$\mathbb E a=p\odot m,\qquad \mathrm{Cov}(a)=D_m\Gamma D_m+D_mX+X^\top D_m+\Xi .$$

**E4 (the next pre-activation, decomposed by the face field).**
$$z_{l+1}=\mu'+\Lambda^\top(g_l-p)+\tilde\varepsilon,\qquad \Lambda=D_mW+\Gamma^{-1}XW,\qquad \mathbb E[\tilde\varepsilon\,(g_l-p)^\top]=0,$$
where $\Lambda$ is the linear regression of $z_{l+1}$ on the face field (the "face barycentre shifts") and $\tilde\varepsilon$ the residual. Exact as a decomposition (it defines $\tilde\varepsilon$); $\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde\varepsilon)=S=W^\top(\Xi-X^\top\Gamma^{-1}X)W$.

## 3. The estimator: barycentric face closure (BFC)

**State at layer $l$** (all local face data): $p$ (face masses, $n$), $m$ (own-half-face barycentres, $n$), $v=\mathrm{Var}(z_k\mid g_k=1)$ ($n$), $\Gamma$ (pair face masses, $n\times n$), $X$ (cross-barycentres of residuals on faces, $n\times n$), $\Xi$ (within-face residual covariance, $n\times n$). Readout at every layer: $\mathbb E a_l=p\odot m$, the barycentre of the own face times its mass (E3, exact given the state).

**Per-layer operation** (layer $l\to l+1$):
1. Arrow (exact, E1/E3): $\mu'=W^\top(p\odot m)$, $C'=W^\top\mathrm{Cov}(a)W$, $\Lambda$, $S$ as in E4.
2. Gluing G1 (residual is Gaussian and independent of the face field): $\tilde\varepsilon\sim N(0,S)$ independent of $g_l$. Then *all* non-Gaussianity of $z_{l+1}$ sits in the gate-field functionals $U=\Lambda^\top(g_l-p)$.
3. Gluing G2 (pairwise face closure of the gate field): cumulants of $U$ are computed from 1- and 2-body face data only. For a binary field every cumulant with $\le 2$ distinct indices is a function of $(p,\Gamma)$, e.g. $\kappa(g_i,g_i,g_k)=(1-2p_i)\Gamma_{ik}$, $\kappa_3(g_k)=p_k(1-p_k)(1-2p_k)$; all-distinct connected face correlations are set to zero (v0) or to their tree (star) value (v1).
   $$\kappa_3(U_j)=\sum_k\Lambda_{kj}^3\kappa_3(g_k)+3\sum_{i\ne k}\Lambda_{ij}^2\Lambda_{kj}(1-2p_i)\Gamma_{ik},$$
   similarly $\kappa_4(U_j)$ and the mixed $\kappa(U_j,U_j,U_m)$.
4. New face data: the marginal of $z'_j$ (mean $\mu'_j$, variance $C'_{jj}$, $\kappa_3,\kappa_4$ from step 3) gives $p'_j,m'_j,v'_j$ by the facet formula $\int h\,\mathrm{He}_k\varphi=\int h^{(k)}\varphi$ (E2 again: the Edgeworth terms of a ReLU readout are facet densities $\sigma\,\mathrm{He}_{k-2}(-t)\varphi(t)$); pairs $(z'_j,z'_m)$ give $\Gamma',X',\Xi'$ by bivariate orthant integrals with the mixed gate-field cumulants as corrections.

**Readout**: $\mathbb E a_{L,j}=p_{L,j}m_{L,j}=\sigma t\Phi(t)+\sigma\varphi(t)-\frac{\kappa_3 t}{6\sigma^2}\varphi(t)+\frac{\kappa_4(t^2-1)}{24\sigma^3}\varphi(t)+\frac{\kappa_3^2(t^4-6t^2+3)}{72\sigma^5}\varphi(t)$, $t=\mu/\sigma$.

## 4. Cost at n = 1024 (units of 2^31 FLOPs; prices of notes/streams/costmodel/cost.py)

| step | operation | units / layer |
|---|---|---|
| arrow | $\mathrm{Cov}(a)W$, $W^\top(\cdot)$ (Gram-symmetric second product) | 1.5–2 |
| regression | $\Gamma^{-1}X$ (Cholesky solve) and $\Lambda=(\cdot)W$ | 1.3 |
| G2 cumulants | $\Gamma\Lambda$, then $n^2$ elementwise | 1 |
| mixed cumulants for pairs | two products $(\Lambda^{\circ2}\odot\cdot)^\top\Lambda$ | 2 |
| new face data | $n^2$ bivariate orthant integrals, ~100 FLOPs each | 0.05 |
| **total** | | **≈ 6 / layer, ≈ 90 for L = 16 (0.09 B)** |

So BFC sits at the 0.1 B floor: adjusted MSE = 0.1 × raw MSE. To beat the bar (1.6e-9 adjusted) it needs raw < 1.6e-8 at n = 1024.

## 5. Error mechanism and predicted scaling

The three gluing errors, each a named face quantity:

- **G1 defect, face-sufficiency of the own barycentre.** $\mathbb E[z_k\mid g_l]\ne\mathbb E[z_k\mid g_k]$: other faces carry information about $z_k$ beyond its own face. The linear part is absorbed exactly by $\Gamma^{-1}X$; what remains is the nonlinear dependence of residuals on the face field and residual heteroscedasticity $\mathrm{Var}(\tilde\varepsilon\mid g)$. This is the finite version of "the barycentre is sufficient iff the cocycle is a coboundary": here the defect is measurable directly.
- **G2 defect, connected 3-body face correlations** $\kappa(g_i,g_k,g_m)$, all distinct. Spectral independence (η bounded) controls the influence of one pinning on the rest, hence these connected correlations are $O(\eta\cdot\text{pair})$ products, and their tree part is computable by matrix products (v1).
- **Truncation**: cumulants of order ≥ 5 of $U$, and old (> 1 layer) non-Gaussian content in $\tilde\varepsilon$, which G1 discards at every layer.

**Prediction.** Per-neuron third cumulants of $z_{l+1}$ are $O(n^{-1/2})$ coherent terms; G2 keeps all terms with ≤ 2 distinct indices, so the dropped part of $\kappa_3$ is the all-distinct content. If that content is mostly tree-like (star via a hub's facet, as the first-order "gate diagram" fact suggests) v1 recovers it. The G1 discard of old content is the largest risk: BRIEF §3 says ≈ 40 % of the (2,1) slice feeding a layer is > 1 layer old. A memoryless closure therefore cannot be better than the "memoryless" rungs of the oracle ladder; v0 predicts a raw MSE between the Gaussian-closure 4e-5 and the true-variance oracle (≈ 0.6 × 4e-5) unless the face representation carries old content through $\Gamma$ (pair face masses do carry history: they are computed at each layer from the transported $\Lambda$, which contains the full $\Gamma$ of the previous layer).

## 6. Cheapest falsification test (run first)

**One-step oracle test (T1).** From a large sample at layer $l$ (truth), compute the exact state $(p,m,v,\Gamma,X,\Xi)$; apply one BFC step; compare with the true layer-$(l+1)$ quantities: (a) $\kappa_3(z'_j)$ predicted by G2 vs measured; (b) the one-step readout error of $\mathbb E a_{l+1}$ vs the one-step error of Gaussian closure fed the exact covariance. If BFC's one-step error is not ≪ the Gaussian one-step error, the gate field does not carry the relevant non-Gaussianity and the design is dead at this dictionary.

**Full propagation (Stage Q).** Widths 64/128/256, depth 16, 4 MLPs, truth from `whest dataset bake` (N = 2e7 / 1e7 / 5e6).

Where the skew lives (T1b): in the face representation $a=mg+r$ the gate-field functional $U=\Lambda^\top(g-p)$ carries a third cumulant of rms 0.003 at layer 2 (true 0.266) and 0.048 at layer 8 (true 0.213). At layer 2 this is forced: $p=1/2$, so the Bernoulli field is symmetric and $\kappa(g_i,g_i,g_k)=(1-2p_i)\Gamma_{ik}=0$.

**Charge to the dictionary.** The naive point was G1: "the within-face residual is Gaussian and independent of the face". The skew of $a_k=g_kz_k$ is a product effect of the gate and the within-face magnitude (the hinge). It sits in $\kappa(g,r,r)$, $\kappa(g,g,r)$, $\kappa_3(r)$, which G1 discards. Codimension-0 face data (masses, barycentres) are the wrong carriers of non-Gaussianity. By E2 the carriers are the **facets** (codimension 1): the hinge's curvature $\mathbb E\,\mathrm{ReLU}''=$ facet density. Dictionary v1 therefore moves the state from faces to facets.

### 7.2 T1c (hub gluing of the all-distinct κ3, width 64, N = 1e6) — `t1c_hub.py`, logs `t1c_gauss64.log`, `t1c_sphere64.log`

Gluing rule: conditional expectation onto the hub's σ-algebra, $\mathbb E[\tilde a_i\tilde a_k\tilde a_m]\approx\mathbb E[\tilde a_k\,\mathbb E[\tilde a_i\mid z_k]\,\mathbb E[\tilde a_m\mid z_k]]$ summed over the three hub choices. Two variants: 'lin' (linear in $z_k$) and 'face' (linear on each half-face of $k$, i.e. span$\{z_k,a_k\}$). Only pair data and 1-body hub moments are used.

| z-layer | all-distinct κ3 rms | lin: corr / rel. err | face: corr / rel. err |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.068 | 1.00 / 0.06 | 0.97 / 0.25 |
| 4 | 0.082 | 0.65 / 0.77 | 0.76 / 0.74 |

| 12 | 0.111 | 0.96 / 1.32 | — |

At the Gaussian layer the linear hub rule is exact (it is the leading Wick tree). At depth it has the right direction (corr 0.96–0.99) but 2–3× the size. The face-wise projection does not fix this. The likely cause is strong correlation at depth: with $\rho\to1$ the three hub terms each reproduce the same structure ($\rho=1$ gives exactly 3×). A second cause is the hub's own transported skew. **Radial hypothesis tested and falsified**: by homogeneity $a(x)=R\,b(\theta)$ exactly (check C3), so the radial mode could have been the over-counted latent hub. With sphere inputs the over-count is unchanged (layer 16: 1.72 vs 1.74).

### 7.3 T2 (facet births transported by face-averaged arrows) — `t2_births.py`, log `t2_n64.log`

**E5 (exact telescoping).** With face-averaged slopes $\beta_s=\mathrm{Cov}(a_s,z_s)/\mathrm{Var}(z_s)$ and births $\nu_s=\tilde a_s-\beta_s\tilde z_s$ (the non-linear part of the activation, born at the facet):
$$\tilde z_l=xP_{x\to l}+\sum_{s<l}\nu_sP_{s\to l},\qquad P_{s\to l}=W_{s+1}D_{\beta_{s+1}}\cdots D_{\beta_{l-1}}W_l .$$
This is exact (telescoping error 2e-4 in float32, T3). It is the face principle in pure form: on each face the arrow is linear, $P$ is the face-averaged arrow, and everything non-linear is born at facets. Tree gluing gives $\kappa_3(z_{l,j})\approx6\sum_{s<l}\sum_k(P_{s\to l})_{kj}c_{s,k}K[k,j]^2$, with $c$ = facet coefficient and $K$ the covariance carrying the two legs.

| legs $K$ | rel. err of κ3 at z-layers 3 / 8 / 12 / 16 | corr |
|---|---|---|
| true cross-layer covariance Cov($z_s,z_l$) | 0.38 / 2.6 / 6.2 / 10.5 | 0.96–0.99 |
| birth-layer covariance transported, $C_sD_\beta P_{s\to l}$ | 0.43 / 2.2 / 5.0 / 9.1 | 0.94–0.98 |
| Gaussian input legs only, $P_{x\to s}^\top P_{x\to l}$ ('lin', a quadratic chaos in $x$) | 0.42 / 0.53 / 0.56 / 0.35 | 0.89–0.96 |
| memoryless: only $s=l-1$, true legs | 0.46 / 0.25 / 0.40 / 0.44 | 0.89–0.99 |

Accumulating births over depth with realistic legs grows linearly with depth, while the true κ3 stays flat (rms 0.2–0.25). Later facets fold away old skew. A tree transport along face-averaged arrows cannot see this; the folding is the cross-birth cumulant $\kappa(\nu_s,\nu_{s'},\cdot)$.
