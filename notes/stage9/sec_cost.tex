\label{sec:cost}
The score multiplies the error by $\max(0.1,\,C/B)$ with $C$ the metered count of the grader's own numpy wrapper
(\texttt{flopscope} 0.12.1) and $B=2^{41}$. We read the wrapper's rules off its source and measured the bills of
the chain's building blocks at $n=1024$ (\texttt{notes/compute/FLOPSCOPE.md}); the facts that decide a design are
these.

\paragraph{Rules.} Every call bills $\lfloor\text{textbook count}\times\text{dtype rate}\times\text{weight}\rfloor$.
The count is the textbook one, not what BLAS does: a matmul $(m,k)(k,n)$ is $2mkn-mn$ whatever algorithm the
library uses, so a Strassen level written out of seven smaller \texttt{matmul} calls \emph{is} billed at $7/8$.
The dtype rate is $1$ for float32 and $2$ for float64. The weight is $0$ for views, transposes, \texttt{zeros};
$1$ for arithmetic, reductions, contractions, copies, \texttt{sqrt}, \texttt{stats.norm.cdf/pdf} (whose counts are
$48$ and $27$ per element, computed in float64), \texttt{polyval} ($2d$ per element) and all of \texttt{linalg};
$4$ for \texttt{where}, gathers and sorts; $16$ for \texttt{exp}, \texttt{log} and every random sampler. A
contraction whose output the engine can prove symmetric is billed on the unique entries: the sandwich
$WCW^\top$ written as one \texttt{einsum('ij,jk,lk->il', W, C\_sym, W)} with the same array $W$ in both outer
slots and $C$ tagged \texttt{as\_symmetric} bills $3n^3$ in float32 ($6n^3$ in float64) against $4n^3$ ($8n^3$) for
two matmuls; the saving is lost if the two products are separate calls. \texttt{eigh} is $9n^3$, \texttt{cholesky}
$n^3/3$, \texttt{inv} $2n^3$, \texttt{solve} $\tfrac23n^3+2n^2r$.

\paragraph{Budget.} The floor $0.1B=2.2\cdot10^{11}$ equals $205\,n^3$ over the $16$ layers, i.e.
$12.8\,n^3$ per layer: six float32 $(n,n,n)$ matmuls, or four float32 symmetric sandwiches, or three float64
matmuls. Nothing is gained below the floor, so a chain should spend exactly it. The Gaussian pair chain,
one sandwich per layer, costs $0.024B$ in float32 (one quarter of the floor) and $0.047B$ in float64.
Elementwise work on $n\times n$ arrays is negligible unless repeated dozens of times per layer: the Hermite series
of the pair moment to order $K$ is $K$ Hadamard products, $2Kn^2$, i.e.\ $0.002\,n^3$ per term.

\begin{proposition}[Pair floor]\label{prop:floor}
Any estimator that forms the pre-activation covariance of every layer from that of the previous one pays at least
one sandwich per layer: $3n^3$ in float32 with the symmetric engine, $L\cdot3n^3=0.024B$ at $n=1024$, $L=16$.
Every further $n\times n$ object transported through $W$ (a $\kap_3$ slice, a $(2,2)$ slice, a gate-correlation
matrix) costs another $2n^3$ to $3n^3$ per layer, i.e.\ $0.016B$ to $0.024B$ over the network. Under the floor a
chain may therefore carry the pair plus about four such objects per layer; the leaders' bills ($0.105B$,
$0.144B$) correspond to five to seven float32 sandwich-equivalents per layer, and the open-source third-cumulant
chain at $0.25B$ to a float64 implementation of the same count.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Naive counts mislead.} The count our chain returns (FMA $=2$, no dtype rate, no symmetry) overstates
the float32 symmetric bill by $2.7\times$ and understates a float64 plain-matmul bill by $2\times$. The
\emph{pair floor} and the \emph{per-object price} above are the only two numbers a design needs; every proposal in
Section~\ref{sec:dictionary} is costed in units of ``$n\times n$ objects transported per layer''.

\paragraph{Precision.} float32 arithmetic (relative $10^{-7}$ per operation) propagates the covariance through
sixteen layers of $1024$-term sums to a relative $10^{-6}$, far below the $10^{-4}$ rms of a $10^{-8}$-MSE
estimator; but the quantities that are small differences of $O(1)$ numbers (the Euler--Stein defect
$e-\Delta e$, differences of nearly equal slices) must be formed in float64 or arranged to avoid cancellation.

\subsection{The memory of the third cumulant and the transport cocycle}
The organisers' chain keeps the all-distinct part of $\kap_3$ in factored form $\mathrm{Sym}\sum_rA_{ir}B_{jr}C_{kr}$ and adds
$2n$ columns to each leg at every layer (the star block of the births and the residual block that makes the
$(2,1)$ and $(3)$ slices exact), so the rank at layer $l$ is $2ln$ and the transport and readout cost $18\,l\,n^3$ per
layer, $1.2B$ in float32 over the network: $93\%$ of the reference's own count. The theory says where to cut. A
block born at layer $l'$ is, at layer $l$, the image of its birth legs under the gated transport cocycle
\[
T_{l\leftarrow l'}=W_l\,\diag(g_{l-1})\,W_{l-1}\,\diag(g_{l-2})\cdots W_{l'+1}\diag(g_{l'}),
\]
a product of $l-l'$ independent gated random matrices. Such products contract onto their leading Oseledets
directions, and the leading directions are the collective mode of Section~\ref{sec:central}: in the stage-8 language
the old sources live on the leaf space, and the shared basis of their transport is the transverse measure's
support. On network $0$ (\texttt{scripts/diag\_cocycle.py}) the effective rank $\|T\|_F^2/s_1^2$ of $T_{l\leftarrow l-a}$
is $164,92,65,47,29,23$ at ages $a=1,2,3,4,6,8$ (independent of $l$ to a few percent), and a random block
transported for $a$ ages has $51,70,81,87,94,96\%$ of its energy in the top $128$ left singular directions and
$77,92,95,98.5,99.7,99.8\%$ in the top $256$. Sources of age $\ge2$ can therefore be merged into a \emph{Tucker tier}
$\mathrm{Sym}((Q\otimes Q\otimes Q)S)$ with one orthonormal basis $Q\in\R^{n\times k}$ shared by the three legs and a
symmetric core $S\in\R^{k\times k\times k}$ (\texttt{whest/kprop3c.py}): the basis is transported as $W\diag(g)Q$ and
re-orthonormalised, the core rotated by the $k\times k$ factor, and an aged block is folded in by refitting the basis
to the dominant directions of $[Q\,\diag(\nu),A,B,C]$ and projecting. Its cost per layer is $2nk^3+4n^2k$ for the two
slice readouts and $2nk^3$ for a merge, i.e.\ $19\,n^3$ at $k=128$ with no young tier, against $18l\,n^3$ dense;
at $k=256$ the cubic core ($2nk^3=32n^3$ per readout) is already too expensive, which is the statement that a
generic order-three tensor on a $k$-dimensional leaf space has $k^3/6$ degrees of freedom and cannot be read for
less. The young tier (ages $<w$) costs $16\,n^3$ per source-age. Table~\ref{tab:tier-cost} gives the resulting bills
under the grader's rules; the measured accuracy of each configuration is in Section~\ref{sec:measure}.

\subsection{Why the memory resists Frobenius compression}
The measured accuracy of the two-tier chain (Section~\ref{sec:measure}) is far worse than the energy fractions
of the cocycle suggest, and the reason is structural. The slices are read through \emph{Hadamard} products of the
legs, $(A\circ B)C^\top$: for a residual block transported for $a$ ages the $(2,1)$ slice is
$\tfrac13[\,2((TR)\circ T)T^\top+(T\circ T)(TR)^\top]$ with $T=T_{l\leftarrow l-a}$. Projecting a leg on the top-$k$
subspace of $T$ controls $\|T-QQ^\top T\|_F$, but the Hadamard square $(QQ^\top T)\circ(QQ^\top T)$ is not the
projection of $T\circ T$: the square of a rank-$k$ matrix has rank up to $k(k+1)/2$ and lives in a different
subspace. A leg truncation that loses a fraction $\epsilon^2$ of the energy therefore loses a fraction $\sim2\epsilon$
of the slice, and by the sensitivity law of Proposition~\ref{prop:sens} the slices of the sources that matter must
be right to $10^{-3}$: the leg energy must be captured to $10^{-6}$, which the cocycle spectrum ($s_{256}/s_1=0.5,
0.3,0.18,0.17$ at ages $1$--$4$) is nowhere near. Compression of the kind that works for a \emph{linear} transport
(keep the dominant directions of $T$) does not work for a cubic readout, except for sources old enough that
their whole contribution is small: at age $\ge4$ the sources are worth a factor $6$ in MSE, so a $5\%$ slice error
on them costs $\sim10\%$, which is the regime in which the open-source chain's shared basis ($k=384$, window $4$)
loses its reported $3.5\%$. The arithmetic of the pair floor then says that an exact-first-order chain cannot be
brought near $0.1B$ by compressing its memory: the young window alone, four ages at $16$--$24\,n^3$ each, exceeds
the whole budget. The birth blocks have identity legs, so each source-age needs only three transported matrices ($T$, $TX_1$, $TR$,
with $X_3=X_1\diag(w)$ a column scaling of $X_1$) and three readout products once the two terms with $T^\top$ on the
right are summed first: $12\,n^3$ per source-age instead of $24$, $8.3$ with three Strassen levels. The
exact first-order chain then costs $12\sum_{l}l\,n^3\approx0.7B$ ($0.5B$ with Strassen) in float32, and a window of
$w$ ages $12w\,n^3$ per layer plus whatever represents the rest; a $k$-dimensional CP tier costs about $11n^3$ per old
source-layer at $k=384$, no less than the dense form, because the projection helps the transport and not the
Hadamard readouts. What a competitive chain at $0.1B$ must do instead is keep \emph{fewer} ages and recover the
systematic part of what is lost by calibration on the public networks; Section~\ref{sec:measure} measures how
much of the loss is systematic (a scale of the old sources' slices) and how much is shape.
