\begin{abstract}
We apply the stage-8 principle (a localization process is a positive martingale in the commutant, whose central part is
the transverse measure on the leaf space) to the WhestBench problem: the per-neuron mean $\E\,h_L$ of a bias-free
He-initialised \textsc{ReLU} multilayer perceptron with standard Gaussian input, $n=1024$, $L=16$. Results. (A) A
defect--path theorem: the error of any estimator exact on point masses is minus its expected drift along any complete
localization of the input, i.e.\ its second variation against the scheme's driving covariance; on Eldan's path this
is the Euler--Stein defect $\delta=e-\Delta e$, and for the Gaussian closure at full scale the exact $\delta$ explains
$89\%$ of the error ($4.1\cdot10^{-6}\to4.4\cdot10^{-7}$), with the parameter-free midpoint $(e+\Delta e)/2$ nearly as
good, and for the organisers' exact first-order chain it explains $81\%$ ($3.5\cdot10^{-8}\to6.6\cdot10^{-9}$, below the
leaders' raw accuracy). (B) Input tilts have no capacity: $\delta$ is a $2\%$ residual of an $O(1)$ identity, random probes need $10^9$
samples, and only the $2n+1$ coordinate sum resolves it. (C) The index-shift lemma $d_k'=d_{k+1}$ and the Gram
theorem $\delta=-\langle\nabla^2\mathcal G,\Gamma\rangle$: births, arms, slices and gate covariances are the derivatives
of the Gaussian moment map, and the organisers' 36-diagram first-order chain is this calculus. We port that chain to
numpy, match it to $10^{-15}$, and find its accuracy ($3.5\cdot10^{-8}$) rests on two things: the first-order term table
and one radial $\kap_4$ scalar per layer, the scale mixture of the collective mode (a factor $16$); our own chain had
the first and lacked the second. (D) Its cost is the memory of $\kap_3$: $12\,n^3$ per source-age, $0.5$--$0.7B$ in
float32 under the grader's measured rules, against a floor of $0.1B$; the gated transport cocycle has slowly decaying
rank but the slices are Hadamard products of the legs, so Frobenius compression of young sources fails (a factor $26$
at $k=128$) while a four-age window with a $384$-dimensional shared basis is lossless and still costs $0.5B$; the old
content is a new shape at every layer (correlation $0.3$--$0.5$ with the young content), so it cannot be regenerated
either. Every identity is checked by the accompanying scripts.
\end{abstract}
