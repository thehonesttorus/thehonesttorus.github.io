# Results

## Characteristics of Included Studies

The systematic search identified three studies addressing the computation of expectations of neural network outputs over Gaussian inputs. Notably, none of the three studies directly investigates stochastic localization, Gaussian conditioning via linear tilts, gate/sign pinning, or common-mode conditioning as deterministic quadrature or variance-reduction devices. None reports results on conditional independence of ReLU gates given a low-rank common mode, Gaussian orthant probabilities, or Mehler expansions of gate covariances. Instead, all three studies develop layer-by-layer moment-propagation or cumulant-propagation methods for computing expectations through piecewise-linear (and other) activation functions under Gaussian input assumptions.

All three studies are computational/methodological in nature with no human-participant component[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026; Wilson Wu et al., 2026; Oren Wright et al., 2024]. Each addresses the problem of characterizing neural network output distributions when the input (or weights) follow a Gaussian distribution, but through distinct mathematical machinery. Kuang & Lin (2026) derive exact closed-form moment-matching formulas[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026], Wu et al. (2026) use cumulant and Hermite-expansion representations of activation distributions[Wilson Wu et al., 2026], and Wright et al. (2024) provide a general analytic covariance solution for elementwise nonlinear activations[Oren Wright et al., 2024].

Kuang & Lin (2026) report the largest accuracy gains, with KL divergence improvements of up to six orders of magnitude over popular alternatives on random networks[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026]. Wu et al. (2026) demonstrate a complementary advantage: for sufficiently wide networks, their cumulant-propagation estimator achieves a target MSE with asymptotically fewer FLOPs than Monte Carlo, with particularly strong performance in the tail-probability regime[Wilson Wu et al., 2026]. Wright et al. (2024) show that their analytic covariance propagation yields estimates closer to Monte Carlo reference values than the piecewise-linear approximation (PL-DNN), though the benefit in downstream Bayesian neural network training is modest and dataset-dependent[Oren Wright et al., 2024].

Wu et al. (2026) also demonstrate that cumulant propagation can be used for model training via mechanistic distillation, although this was found to be slightly less efficient than standard stochastic-gradient distillation[Wilson Wu et al., 2026].

## Synthesis

The research question specifically asks about stochastic localization, Gaussian conditioning (linear tilts), gate/sign pinning, and common-mode conditioning as deterministic quadrature or variance-reduction techniques, including conditional independence of ReLU gates given a low-rank common mode, Gaussian orthant probabilities, Mehler expansions of gate covariances, and the cost-versus-bias/variance tradeoff of such conditioning. None of the three included studies addresses any of these techniques or concepts directly[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026; Wilson Wu et al., 2026; Oren Wright et al., 2024].

The studies do, however, occupy the broader space of computing expectations of piecewise-linear (and related) activation functions over Gaussian inputs without resorting entirely to Monte Carlo sampling. All three confirm that exact or high-accuracy deterministic alternatives to sampling are feasible for this class of problems, with substantial accuracy or efficiency advantages in their respective settings[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026; Wilson Wu et al., 2026; Oren Wright et al., 2024]. The mathematical tools they employ—closed-form moment matching[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026], Hermite expansions and cumulant algebra[Wilson Wu et al., 2026], and analytic activation-covariance integrals[Oren Wright et al., 2024]—are related to (but distinct from) the Mehler expansions and orthant-probability calculations referenced in the research question.

A key distinction among the three approaches concerns their treatment of inter-neuron correlations. Kuang & Lin (2026) propagate the full covariance matrix layer by layer, maintaining joint second-order statistics[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026]. Wright et al. (2024) similarly propagate covariance matrices and provide an analytic solution for the covariance of random variables passed through nonlinear activations[Oren Wright et al., 2024]. Wu et al. (2026), by contrast, use a factorized estimator that exploits width to reduce computational cost from \$\Theta(n^2/\varepsilon^2)\$ to \$O(n/\varepsilon^2)\$, but this factorization introduces a depth-dependent tradeoff in FLOP cost[Wilson Wu et al., 2026]. This factorization strategy is conceptually adjacent to, but not identical to, the conditional-independence structures (e.g., conditional independence of ReLU gates given a common mode) specified in the research question.

The theoretical guarantees also differ in kind. Kuang & Lin (2026) provide a smooth-distance error bound showing that moment matching removes leading low-variance errors under regularity assumptions[Simon Kuang & Xinfan Lin, 2026]. Wu et al. (2026) provide asymptotic FLOP comparisons under a small-error condition for wide networks[Wilson Wu et al., 2026]. Wright et al. (2024) provide exact analytic expressions but report that downstream training benefits are inconsistent across datasets[Oren Wright et al., 2024]. None of these guarantees addresses the closure bias or variance that would arise from the specific conditioning or pinning operations described in the research question.

In summary, while these three studies demonstrate that deterministic or semi-analytic computation of expectations through ReLU (and related) networks over Gaussian inputs is both feasible and advantageous compared to Monte Carlo, the specific variance-reduction devices of stochastic localization, gate pinning, and common-mode conditioning—and their associated cost-bias-variance tradeoffs—remain unaddressed by the retrieved literature.
