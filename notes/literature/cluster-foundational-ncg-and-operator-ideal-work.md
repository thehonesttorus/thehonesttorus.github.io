# Cluster: Foundational NCG and operator-ideal work

## Cluster verdict: Foundational NCG and operator-ideal work — nothing here transfers

**Six papers, six not-relevant rulings, all six surviving adversarial challenge.** This is a negative result about the whole family, not six independent coincidences, and the reason is structural enough to state once.

### What this body of work is actually for

These papers are *existence and classification theory for infinite-dimensional operator algebras*. Their characteristic move is: fix an algebra $A$ and an unbounded operator $D$ acting on a Hilbert space; grade the compact operators by how fast their singular values decay ($\mathcal{L}^p$, $\mathcal{L}^{1,\infty}$/Macaev, Lorentz, general symmetrically-normed ideals); then prove that *some structure exists* (a trace, a hyperfinite approximation, a metric on states, a duality with a groupoid, a wave operator, an optimal lattice) or that it *cannot exist* (non-amenability, property (T), Voiculescu's $k_I(\tau)$ obstruction). Every theorem in the cluster is an equality, an equivalence, or an existence/non-existence statement in a limit. Across all six papers there is no error functional, no truncation parameter, no rate, no cost model, and no algorithm evaluable on a $1024\times1024$ matrix. Connes 1988/1989 build traces and state-space metrics; Voiculescu 2017 proves an exact equivalence of a.c. parts under ideal perturbation; Bertozzini–Conti–Lewkeeratiyutkul prove a duality of categories; Vancliff surveys exact recovery of graded algebras from point schemes; Faulhuber–Strohmer optimise a two-sided frame bound over lattices. A reader who knows our problem can apply the test in one line: *we need a quantity that gets smaller when we spend more FLOPs, and these papers contain no such quantity.*

### The single strongest idea, and its price

The best candidate in the cluster is Connes' trace theorem read on the Gaussian triple our own notes already construct (`finite-resolution-question-algebra.md` §4.1: $L^2(\gamma)\otimes\Lambda(\mathbb{R}^n)$, $D_\gamma = d + d^*_\gamma$, $D_\gamma^2 = N$ the OU number operator with Wiener-chaos eigenspaces). It gives an alternative representation of the *scored quantity itself*: $\mathbb{E}_\gamma[h_{16}]_a \propto \mathrm{Tr}_\omega(M_{(h_{16})_a}|D_\gamma|^{-d})$, componentwise over $a$. That is a shared mathematical object, not shared vocabulary, and it defeats the naive "the paper produces scalars, we need a vector" objection.

Computationally at $n=1024$, $L=16$ it costs the following, and this is why it dies. Evaluating $\mu_k(M_{h}|D_\gamma|^{-d})$ requires the matrix of $M_h$ in the Hermite basis — *every* Wiener-chaos coefficient of $h_{16}$ — where the deployed pipeline needs only the degree-zero one. It converts one number into an infinite expansion: strictly harder, not easier. $\mathrm{Tr}_\omega$ is a Banach limit; it is choice-independent only on "measurable" operators and carries no rate, so there is no finite-$N$ error and nothing to price against the 2.34%-per-sandwich / 6.25%-per-exact-source-layer budget we actually spend. And obstruction 4 caps the prize independently: this is a readout representation, and the readout defect (1.9e-9 with fourth cumulants against 8.7e-7 total) is ~500× below our error. Even a free, exact version of this idea buys under a percent. The binding constraint is propagation, and no paper in this cluster contains a composition law for how a state pushes forward through a nonlinear map.

### The structural mismatch, precisely

Three axes, any one of which is fatal.

1. **Phase-blind grading versus a cancelling sum.** Every functional in this cluster is unitarily invariant — a function of singular values alone — hence structurally blind to the relative sign between terms of a sum. Our obstruction 1 is exactly a phase phenomenon: coherence $\|\sum\|/\sum\|\cdot\| = 0.089$–$0.111$, sign agreement 0.506 against chance 0.500. `results.txt` measures the consequence directly: the 8-layer transport needs only 175 singular directions for 99% of its *energy*, yet rank-128 truncation costs 2.4–3.1× and rank 16 is no worse than rank 64 — damage flat in the truncation parameter because it is a bias, not a resolution loss. Singular-value magnitude is the wrong grading for this problem and it is the only grading these papers possess. Corollary worth keeping: *the difference between two candidate answers to our problem is a finite-rank operator, so any Dixmier-trace-valued objective has exactly zero gradient with respect to our estimate.*
2. **Approximating a fixed space in a limit versus closing a dynamical propagation under a budget.** These are theorems about what exists as $N\to\infty$, with no finite-$n$ construction. They cannot in principle distinguish rank 16 from rank 64, which is the actual question our diagnostics pose.
3. **A priori seminorm versus observable-induced metric.** Connes' distance formula, and the metric-morphism notion Bertozzini et al. build on it, both require $D$ given in advance and evaluate on *pure* states. Our error must be measured in the units a fixed downstream MSE cares about, on a *mixed and non-positive* functional. Wrong direction on both counts.

A fourth, blunter point: our carrier is $M_N$, finite-dimensional. There every normed ideal is $B(H)$, every operator is trace class, $A''=A$ is automatically hyperfinite, $k_\Phi \equiv 0$, and weak convergence is norm convergence. Connes' Theorems 8/11/17/19 and Voiculescu's Theorems 2.1/3.1 are all *trivially true and empty* for us — Voiculescu 3.1 reduces to "0 is unitarily equivalent to 0". The only escape hatch is the infinite-dimensional OU triple above, and that is priced out by obstruction 4.

### Papers whose negative results predict our obstructions

**None — and this is itself the informative finding.** The obvious candidates are Connes 1989 (hyperfiniteness/amenability as an obstruction to finite-dimensional approximation with almost-central expectations) and Bertozzini et al.'s object-bijectivity hypothesis (any morphism collapsing the object set falls outside the duality). Both would "predict" our failed Cartan corner $1_Y A 1_Y$, which is literally a conditional expectation onto a hereditary subalgebra. **Our own data refutes that explanation.** `groupoid-carrier-and-cartan-truncation.md` lines 174–180: the Cartan-*compatible* corner does *slightly worse* than the Cartan-*breaking* spectral truncation at every setting (top-25% 6.77e-7 vs. importance-weighting-free 6.84e-7; top-50% 6.32e-7 vs. spectral rank-128 5.46e-7), and reweighting which units are kept barely moves it — verbatim, "the failure is not about which subalgebra the truncation respects." Connes' and Bertozzini's obstructions are *entirely* about which subalgebra structures can exist. That is precisely the axis we measured to be irrelevant. So a theory that appears to retrodict obstruction 1 in fact predicts along a coordinate our data shows is flat, which is disconfirmation, not corroboration.

Two further near-misses recorded so they are not re-searched. Connes 1989 Lemma 9 looks like an aggregate bound (obstruction 2's shape) but its identity (1) is a telescoping sum bounded *term-by-term* by Hölder plus the triangle inequality — an anti-example, valid only because each term is individually small. Voiculescu's Kuroda lemma $|X^{\oplus j}|_\Phi = j^{1/p}|X|_\Phi$ is genuinely a sublinear aggregate bound with an exponent, but a *direct sum is not a sum*: nothing cancels, singular values are merely redistributed across orthogonal supports, and our metric (MSE) is exactly additive on orthogonal pieces. Faulhuber–Strohmer's remark that the hexagonal lattice maximises the minimum "without knowing the minimizer" is the correct slogan for obstruction 2, but the method behind it (Janssen/symplectic Poisson resummation) needs a discrete abelian index group with a Pontryagin dual, and our path sum is indexed by (source unit, source layer) of an i.i.d. He-init network — no group. The resummation our problem *does* support is already shipped as the factorised diagram form; coherence 0.09 is what remains *after* it.

### What to read in full

For method: nothing. Reading all six in full (which the challengers did, obtaining full text for four; Connes 1988 and 1989 have no arXiv version — 1989 was recovered as OCR, 1988 could not be retrieved at all and was assessed from its zbMATH reference list and citation graph) produced zero live routes and inverted two arguments that looked promising in summary. That inversion is the case *for* full-text reading of this cluster generally, but the yield is now spent.

One concrete, non-zero item: **Connes 1989, Propositions 1 and 4 (two pages)** are the primary source for the distance formula invoked unsourced at `finite-resolution-question-algebra.md` line 89; the reference appendix (line 186) cites Connes–van Suijlekom and Rieffel but not this paper. Prop. 1 is the commutative case, Prop. 4 the full state space. That is a citation fix that changes no number in the estimator.
