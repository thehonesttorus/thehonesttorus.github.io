# Cluster: Dynamical closure, Koopman and transfer operators, data assimilation

## Cluster verdict: Dynamical closure, Koopman and transfer operators, data assimilation

**Nothing in this cluster transfers.** Five papers, five independent adversarial challenges, five surviving `not-relevant` rulings. The reason is structural and uniform, not paper-by-paper bad luck, and it is worth stating once because it also tells us which future papers to skip on sight.

### What this body of work is actually for

Four of the five papers (Freeman–Giannakis–Mintz–Ourmazd–Slawinska 2206.13659; Freeman–Giannakis–Slawinska 2208.03390; Giannakis–Montgomery 2603.20102; Giannakis et al. 2501.07419) are one research program. Its problem is: you have an **unknown** autonomous, invertible, measure-preserving, ergodic flow on a compact space, and a long trajectory of samples from it. You want to forecast observables. The program's answer is to work with the unitary Koopman group instead of the nonlinear flow, learn a Hilbert basis by kernel-eigendecomposition of an N×N Gram matrix over trajectory samples, compress operators as `Π_L A Π_L` (which is completely positive by conjugation, so the scheme never leaves the state cone), carry a density operator as the state, optionally amplify to a Fock space so that products of retained eigenfunctions are also retained, and optionally add a Lüders-type analysis step to fuse observations. Correctness is always a **consistency theorem in an iterated limit** — `L→∞`, `N→∞`, `τ→0+`, `m→∞` — with error measured in an a priori, observable-agnostic RKHS or trace norm. Karasev 1404.1790 is from a different tradition (adiabatic normal-form averaging: split an operator into its commutant part `T^&` and the range of `ad_S`, conjugate the rest away, everything asymptotic in `ε`) but has the same shape: an exact structural split validated in a limit, with no finite-dimensional realization anywhere in the paper.

Read against our problem, the mismatch is total before any technique is discussed. We have **known** weights, a **non-autonomous** 16-step composition of `x ↦ ReLU(Wx)` that is not invertible, preserves no measure, admits no invariant measure or ergodicity, and whose composition operator is neither unitary nor normal. We have nothing to learn from data and no budget to learn it with — sampling is what the task states outright cannot win.

### The single strongest idea, and what it would cost

The one genuinely transplantable object is **purification / factor propagation** (2206.13659 App. B.8, Γ: p ↦ √p): carry a factor `S` with `C = S Sᵀ` rather than `C` itself, so the propagated covariance is positive semidefinite by construction and no eigenvalue floor is ever needed. Concretely at n=1024, L=16: a Cholesky re-factorization after each layer's closure step is `n³/3 ≈ 3.58e8` FLOPs, ×16 layers ≈ `5.7e9` = **0.26% of 2^41**, i.e. free under obstruction 6 (we already spend 2.34% on the covariance sandwich). Cost is not the objection.

The objection is that it is **capped by our own measurement at ~2%** (obstruction 3; results.txt §7 shows the 1e-6 floor already captures it, 1e-4 actively hurts), and worse, our single negative eigenvalue (~-1e-4 of trace) originates *entirely* in the two-point cumulant correction — the one channel worth 3.1x. Forcing that additive correction into a CP/density-operator form would most plausibly shrink or drop it, costing more than the 2% recovered. And the third-cumulant path sum is an additive perturbative correction, not a state; it does not embed in a density-operator picture at all, so purification cannot touch the channel that binds.

The runner-up idea — reframe the 2400-row MC channel as a noisy *observation* and fuse it with the analytic prior via the Lüders update instead of the current linear blend at 0.75 — points at real money (results.txt §19 shows the two-point coefficient's across-seed sd is 0.031–0.105 against across-network sd 0.049–0.122; MC noise is the same size as all remaining network-dependent signal). But that is one line of textbook precision-weighted shrinkage. The paper supplies no shrinkage estimator, no finite-sample variance analysis, and no bias-variance tradeoff; its only data-driven statement is `N→∞` consistency. Attributing "shrink the MC toward the analytic prediction" to this cluster would be exactly the manufactured verbal connection the provenance warning flags.

### The structural reason it does not transfer

Three precise mismatches, in increasing order of decisiveness.

1. **Approximating a fixed object in a limit vs. closing a propagation under a budget.** Every accuracy claim in the cluster is an iterated limit. A systematic grep across all five full texts found *zero* finite-L rates, zero non-asymptotic bounds, zero complexity accounting outside one qubit gate count. A limit theorem with no rate is unusable when L is pinned by FLOPs.

2. **A priori seminorm vs. seminorm induced by the output functional.** Their sharpest quantitative object, `tr(ρ − σ_L)` in trace norm, gives `|ω(A) − ω_L(A)| ≤ ‖ρ−σ_L‖₁ ‖A‖` — uniform over *all* observables, via the triangle inequality. Our coherence is 0.09, so that inequality is ~11x loose on exactly this object. Our one working device controls no norm whatsoever: the rank-one memory kernel is fitted to the single linear functional we read out. Worst-case-over-observables versus fitted-to-the-aggregate *is* the difference between our four failures and our one success.

3. **`Π_L A Π_L` is term-preserving, and we killed it four times.** Their central discretization keeps a subspace exactly and discards the rest. Our failed (a) spectral/SVD truncation of the transport *is* two-sided compression onto top spectral modes; our failed (b) Cartan corner `1_Y A 1_Y` *is* `Π_L A Π_L` with diagonal `Π_L`; their state compression `ρ_L = Π_L ρ Π_L / tr` is our failed (c)/(d). All share the measured signature — immediate bias, then flat in the parameter (rank 16 no worse than rank 64; polarized R=1024 at 2.018e-6 vs R=512 at 2.073e-6, 5% for an eightfold rank increase). A dynamics-adapted basis does not repair this (we used the transport's own SVD, the most adapted single-step basis available: 2.4–3.1x). Neither does a target-adapted one (2501.07419's `c_j = |⟨ξ_j, f⟩|` ranking is our `site_pow`: 6.772e-7 / 6.791e-7 / 6.844e-7 at pow 3/1/0 against 2.316e-7 exact — the ranking moves ~1%, the truncation costs 2.9x).

Two further hard stops worth recording because they generalize. **The kernel basis degenerates at n=1024.** Measured: pairwise `d²` concentrates at 2n with relative sd 0.044; the Gaussian kernel integral operator's top eigenvector is the constant, eigenvectors 1–5 are pure degree-1 (R² = 1.000 on the degree≤1 span) with eigenvalues identical to three digits. So the "learned data-driven basis" is a sample-noised estimate of the exact Hermite basis we already propagate in closed form — strictly less information — and the top-L selection rule is *degenerate inside each degree block*, supplying no truncation criterion at all. Dimension counting is brutal: L must exceed `n(n+1)/2 = 5.25e5` to touch second moments and `C(1026,3) = 1.79e8` to touch the third cumulants obstruction 5 says bind, putting true cost near **1e5 × budget** — this is the honest kill for the QMCl family, not the "68x" figure obtained by importing their climate-scale hyperparameters. Separately, **the Fock/Leibniz remedy buys multiplicativity with dimension**: the grade-3 symmetric power at n=1024 is ~1.8e8 components and transporting it is ~`3n⁴` per layer, outside budget outright by obstruction 6.

### Negative results that predict our obstructions

**None — and one of them mispredicts us, which is itself worth knowing.**

- **Freeman et al. 2206.13659, the abelian no-go** (verified verbatim, ~lines 1515–1530): `M_S f = χ_S f` is a valid positivity-preserving discretization iff `μ|_S` is supported on finitely many atoms — a condition the paper calls "highly restrictive." Our 1024 neurons under counting measure are **purely atomic**, so the condition *holds*, and the theorem predicts our Cartan corner `1_Y A 1_Y` should be a perfectly good discretization — precisely where we measured 2.4–3.1x failure with the bias-then-flat signature. Any prior credit for this "explaining obstruction 1" should be **withdrawn**: applied honestly at finite n it gets the sign wrong. The diagnostic generalizes: the cluster's entire positivity-of-discretization anxiety is an artifact of infinite-dimensional continuous measure spaces and has **no content at finite n**, where every coordinate-subset projection is trivially CP. That is the cluster's headline structure-preservation contribution, and it is vacuous for us.
- **Giannakis–Montgomery §4.2, the Leibniz defect** — a compressed operator can converge in strong resolvent sense while products of its approximate eigenfunctions fail to be approximate eigenfunctions — is a real shared *predicate* with obstruction 4's "marginals do not determine the joint" (7.07e-7 oracle). But it is a naming, not an explanation: their mechanism is loss of multiplicativity on products of eigenfunctions, ours is incoherent cancellation across a path sum (sign agreement 0.506 vs chance 0.500). Same word, different failure, and the remedy is priced out.
- **QMCl §9.2** (Palmer / Poincaré–Bendixson: deterministic dimension-reducing parameterizations flatten dynamics, so expand rather than reduce) points the same direction as our measurements, but by a topological obstruction to chaos in low-dimensional ODE flows, unrelated to linear-algebraic cancellation — and its prescription (L=1500 for a 3-dimensional system) is exactly the regime we measured flat.
- **Karasev's `T^&`/`T^#` split** looks like obstruction 2 upgraded to a theorem, but the gauge freedom `T^& = 0` is freedom in an *unphysical intermediate* (the quantum action). Our per-source-unit terms are not gauge: each is an individually computable summand of an exactly-defined third cumulant, and their sum is small because independent contributions cancel, not because a group orbit average annihilates them. There is no group. Also: the paper's `S = Σ_k k P_k` routes through eigenprojections, so any realization lands on the transport-SVD truncation we already measured to fail. And the generic content of the split — commutant part renormalizes the retained sector, complement is conjugated away — we already deploy in its Feshbach form with measured coefficients (results.txt §8: omitted sector is 64% of the third cumulant by layer 16, a two-parameter renormalization recovers ~80%, corr 0.879).

### What is worth reading in full

Almost nothing, and the two exceptions are targeted section reads, not papers.

- **2501.07419 §4.7/§8 and Fig. 6** (~30 minutes). It is the cleanest controlled evidence in any adjacent field for our operating principle: with the *identical* 101-mode eigenbasis and eigenfrequencies held fixed, representing the target as a degree-4 polynomial in those modes beats linear projection onto their span at every prediction time. That is "nonlinear function of a coarse basis dominates linear projection onto the same coarse basis," with the basis-quality confound eliminated. It is morale and a design principle we already follow, not a method — the machinery is a readout representation theorem, and obstruction 4 puts our readout defect ~500x below our total error.
- **2603.20102 §4.2** (one page) for the Leibniz-defect statement, if you want the cleanest available phrasing of "closeness of the compressed operator does not imply closeness on the algebra it generates." Note the paper is a survey chapter of the authors' own prior work with no new theorems and no numerics of its own.

**Do not read further in this family.** The reason is not hyperparameters: it is that every method in the Giannakis program is built on a kernel-eigenfunction basis learned from trajectory samples, and that basis degenerates at n=1024 into a noisy copy of the Hermite basis we already have in closed form, with a selection rule that is degenerate inside every degree block. That kills the family regardless of tuning.
