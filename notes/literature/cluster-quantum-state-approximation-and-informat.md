# Cluster: Quantum state approximation and information geometry

## Cluster verdict: Quantum state approximation and information geometry — **nothing here transfers**

**What this body of work is for.** Every paper in this cluster answers a *static* question: given a target state ρ in a fixed, usually tiny state space, and a fixed achievable set (a convex hull of dictionary states, a parametric manifold, an abelian corner), find the nearest element and characterise the residual. The variation across the six is in the *metric* — fidelity (Zhou/Gao/Yan; Zhang/Yu/Yu), trace norm, BKM = Hessian of von Neumann entropy (Schwarz et al.), quantum Bures–Wasserstein (Becker–Li), Amari's D_γ family (Kostecki) — and in what geometric invariant is extracted (CR volume ratios, homothety constants, lift diameters). What none of them contains is a *propagation*: no memory kernel, no Mori–Zwanzig/Nakajima–Zwanzig projection, no closure remainder, no error-accumulation bound across a composition of maps, and — in five of six — no complexity model of any kind. Where a dynamics appears at all (Schwarz Prop. 4.16, Becker–Li Prop. 3.1, Balachandran §IV) it is *exact by construction* and is the object of study, not an approximation of something larger. If your problem is "I have a state and I must shrink it," this literature is for you. If your problem is "I must close a hierarchy that does not close, 16 times in a row, under a FLOP budget," it is not.

**The single strongest idea, and what it would cost.** It is not any paper's contribution but a cited lemma: the Carathéodory/Steinitz reduction invoked in Zhang–Yu–Yu Thm. 5. It is the only object in the cluster that is *aggregate-preserving by construction* — the axis (results.txt §17) that separates all our successes from all four failures. Sign-absorbed, Σᵢcᵢvᵢ = S·Σᵢ(|cᵢ|/S)·[sgn(cᵢ)vᵢ] with S = Σ|cᵢ|, so the theorem literally says our depth-8 third-cumulant path sum — ~16,384 per-(unit, source-layer) contributions in R^1024 — is reproduced **exactly** by ≤1025 of them with re-solved nonnegative weights. It drops terms and *reweights survivors*, so the sqrt(f·n)-injected-error model of results.txt line 55 does not cover it, and obstruction 1 as written does not kill it. It dies on three other counts. (i) It is post-hoc: it takes the terms as input, and producing them is the entire cost — one exact source layer is 6.25% of B, while summing the terms once produced is O(n·N) ≈ 1.7e7 FLOPs ≈ 0.0008% of B. It compresses the storage of a quantity we have already fully paid to compute. (ii) The surviving subset is found by Gaussian elimination on one network's realised terms; it transfers to no other network and no other layer — the exact inverse of the shipped kernel, whose across-network coefficient spread is below MC-seed spread. (iii) Exactness is load-bearing: at measured coherence 0.089–0.111, S/‖Σ‖ ≈ 11, so a relative ε in the weights becomes ≈11ε in the answer — obstruction 1's tenfold amplification re-entering through the back door. The method is aggregate-preserving only in the regime where it saves nothing. The runner-up is Becker–Li's Bures Lyapunov map L_Σ(Q) = ∫e^{−Σt}Qe^{−Σt}dt: genuinely O(n³), genuinely PSD-preserving, and capped at ~2% by obstruction 3 (one eigenvalue at −1e-4 of trace; cone projection already buys that; a larger floor hurts). Schwarz et al.'s cheaper log-Euclidean chart is worse than free: a symmetric eigendecomposition of a dense 1024×1024 per layer is ~10–25n³ × 16 ≈ 1.7–4.3e11 FLOPs, i.e. 8–20% of B = 2.199e12, against 2.34% for our *entire* covariance sandwich. It lifts us off the 0.1 multiplier floor and multiplies the score by 2–4× to buy 2%.

**The structural mismatch, precisely.** Three axes, all the same way round.
1. *Fixed space in a limit vs. closing a propagation under budget.* Their error is a distance evaluated at one point of state space. Ours is a defect that compounds through 16 compositions of W∘ReLU, where the Hermite expansion couples cumulant order k to every order above it. Schwarz's Prop. 4.16 lift closes exactly because Lemma 4.14 makes S_i depend on ρ only through a *linear* function of log ρ; it supplies no criterion for a system that is not already log-linear, and no theorem about when such a lift exists. Becker–Li §4.3 explicitly places nonlinear activation pushforwards outside the framework.
2. *A priori metric vs. readout-induced seminorm.* Fidelity, trace norm, BKM, quantum W₂ and D_γ are all fixed before the problem is posed — W₂ by the choice of {V_j} and the invariant state σ, BKM by entropy. Our seminorm is induced by future observables: the layer-ℓ error that matters is J_{16←ℓ} applied to it. No paper here constructs a task-induced pullback; Zhou/Gao/Yan merely *compare* two externally fixed metrics by an eigenvalue inequality (and Appendix D returns "cannot judge" on the overlap). And re-metrising is a measured null in our setting anyway: site_pow ∈ {0,1,3} gives 6.844 / 6.791 / 6.772e-7, a 1% spread, because *any* metric induces a norm, a norm is sign-blind, and our deficit is signed cancellation between terms (sign agreement 0.506, coherence 0.089).
3. *Convex/positive-cone machinery vs. a signed incoherent sum.* All KKT tractability in the two "optimal approximation" papers flows from pᵢ ≥ 0, Σpᵢ = 1 through the complementarity conditions λᵢpᵢ = 0; Kostecki's ℓ_γ(ω) = ω^γ/γ and the Bures/BKM charts require positivity as a *prerequisite*. Our target sits at ~9% of its terms' absolute mass — near-total cancellation is exactly what a convex hull cannot cheaply express. The formalism does not merely fail to scale; it points the wrong way in principle. Scale confirms it: closed forms exist at d = 2 (two papers), the largest worked example is Hilbert dimension 4 (Becker–Li), the authors' own stated ceiling is c ≤ 100 (Schwarz), the lift-ambiguity formula Δρ_D = 2√(λ₁λ₂) is N = 2 only with no general-N algorithm (Balachandran), and Becker–Li's G_W(θ) requires inverting an (n²)×(n²) ≈ 1e12-entry superoperator, past obstruction 6's n³-affordable/n⁴-not line.

**Papers whose negative results predict our obstructions: none.** This is the honest answer and I checked the two candidates. Balachandran §III.8 (abelian restriction loses information, maximally at the most-mixed diagonal) is the nearest miss and was argued hard, but it predicts *generic resolution loss*, which is true of any projection; it cannot distinguish our observed failure from ordinary rank truncation, because it contains no notion of conditioning, cancellation, or coherence of a sum, and therefore predicts neither the ~10× amplification, nor the immediate-bias-then-flat signature, nor the non-monotonicity (rank 128 → 5.461e-7 but rank 16 at K0=3 → 4.567e-7 — *more* truncation scoring *better*). A genuine prediction of obstruction 1 requires a coherence functional; nothing in the cluster has one. Worth recording the one explicit no-go in the cluster and why it misfires: Balachandran's Appendix VI.27 is a Hausdorff–Young/Babenko–Beckner entropic uncertainty relation, i.e. a no-simultaneous-concentration statement, which would bear on us only if our obstruction were spectral spread (results.txt §5: 283 directions for 90% of transport energy). Our data rules that reading out — spectral spread predicts monotone improvement with rank, and we measure flat and non-monotone. The cluster's most transferable-sounding negative result targets a mechanism we have measured to be absent.

**Worth reading in full: nothing in this cluster.** Two pointers instead. First, the extension-ambiguity thread (Balachandran §II–III) is the cluster's best structural match to our largest error component — ~81% of our 8.675e-7 survives perfect marginals at every layer (oracle 7.07e-7) — but `notes/finite-resolution-question-algebra.md` lines 69–73 already carries a strictly stronger form: worst-case ambiguity exactly 2·dist(a,S) by order-unit duality, plus the tighter state-specific interval [sup{s(b): b ≤ a, b ∈ S}, inf{s(b): b ≥ a, b ∈ S}], general-dimension, versus their N = 2 sup over the whole unit ball. Reading the paper would be reading our own note in a special case. Second, if the aggregate-preserving reduction is ever pursued, go to Carathéodory (1911), Steinitz (1913) and Maurey/empirical-approximation lemmas directly, and to Koch–Lubich dynamical low-rank (2007) for the dynamic-consistency idea that Becker–Li's Prop. 3.1 restates — both outside this cluster, and neither adds anything by way of these papers.

**Provenance.** Confirmed by full-text search on all six: none cites, critiques, engages or predates-critiques arXiv:2605.05179 (Wu, Lecomte, Winer, Robinson, Hilton, Christiano), and none touches cumulant propagation or any ML literature. Two of the six list entries were themselves corrupt — the Zhou/Gao/Yan title does not exist (it is a paraphrase mashing together two halves of arXiv:2105.07336), and the Schwarz et al. author list is garbled. Treat the calibration accordingly.
