# Cluster: Moment hierarchies, SDP relaxations, noncommutative optimisation

## Cluster verdict: Moment hierarchies, SDP relaxations, noncommutative optimisation — **nothing transfers**

### What this literature is actually for

These four papers all solve the same shape of problem: *an unknown state or unknown operators are constrained by exact algebraic relations, and you want a certified bound on a functional of them.* NPA-style hierarchies (Flora et al.; d'Alessandro–Roch i Carceller–Tavakoli; Araújo–Garner–Navascués) replace "optimise over all states/operators consistent with the relations" by "optimise over moment matrices that are PSD", which is an outer relaxation: it produces a two-sided bracket that tightens as you climb levels. Xu–Schwonnek–Winter does the same for a fixed operator set, bounding the joint numerical range of Pauli strings by graph parameters of their exact (anti)commutation pattern. Every deliverable in the cluster is a **sup over an uncertainty set**, and every convergence guarantee runs in the level/size parameter, not under a cost meter. Our problem has no uncertainty set: weights exact, input law exactly N(0,I), one determined vector E_x[h_16] wanted, and the whole difficulty is that we must *close a 16-step dynamical propagation* under 2^41 FLOPs. The cluster answers a feasibility question we do not have, and is silent on the closure question we do have.

### The single strongest idea, priced

The best candidate is the block-matrix/localising-moment construction of d'Alessandro et al., reduced to its one budget-feasible instantiation: skip the n-variable level-2 matrix (side 1+n+n(n+1)/2 = 5.25e5, ~1.4e17 FLOPs, five orders over budget) and instead impose the **bivariate 6×6 Hamburger moment-matrix condition on each pair (z_a, z_b)**, built from mu, C_ab and the propagated two-point tensor K21_ab. Cost at n=1024: ~5e5 pairs × ~200 FLOPs ≈ 1e8 FLOPs = 0.005% of 2^41 — free under the max(0.1, C/B) floor, and it constrains the *aggregate* tensor entry rather than the cancelling per-source terms, so it structurally evades obstruction 1.

It is still not worth running, for a measured reason. Our error points **into** the cone, not out of it. Truncating source depth *under*-estimates kappa_3 — the omitted tail is 64% of the full third cumulant by layer 16 (results.txt §8) — and shrinking cumulants toward zero moves the state toward Gaussian, which is strictly interior to the moment cone. A one-sided positivity constraint is inactive against excess Gaussianity. Section 7 measures exactly this: the single negative eigenvalue at −1.0e-4 of trace comes *entirely from the two-point correction we add*, not from the 64% we omit; projecting into the cone moves 5.183e-7 → 5.092e-7 (2%), and a 1e-4 floor actively hurts (→ 8.538e-7). A projection moves an estimate by at most its infeasibility; the infeasibility is 1e-4 of trace against a 64% missing sector. And the family has a hard ceiling above us regardless: any per-layer moment relaxation carries only marginal information, and the published oracle for *perfect* marginals at every layer is 7.07e-7 raw against our 8.675e-7 — a free, exactly-converged, level-infinity hierarchy of this type is worth at most 1.23x and still lands ~36x short of the leader's 1.93e-8 raw.

### The structural mismatch, stated precisely

Three distinct mismatches, each independently fatal:

1. **Bound versus estimate, at our accuracy scale.** MSE 1e-9 means per-neuron absolute error ~3e-5 against scored-layer sigma ≈ 0.28, i.e. relative ~1e-4. Araújo et al.'s brackets at their *best* (1D nearest-neighbour chain, n=18–25, level k=2) have width ~1.2e-3 on an O(1) observable — relative ~1e-3, already 10x too loose. At n=1024 only level 1 is affordable, and a level-1 moment matrix in (z,h) with the standard ReLU encoding (h≥0, h≥z, E[h²]=E[hz], PSD of the 2049-side Gram) does not know z is nearly Gaussian: its bracket on E[h_a] has width O(sigma) ≈ 0.3, four orders too wide. There is no intermediate regime — level 2 is a 525825² matrix (2.2 TB). Certified intervals only help when tighter than the estimator they certify.
2. **Static algebra versus dynamical closure.** In all four papers, the hierarchy parameter K is *monomial degree in a fixed algebra*, not depth. None contains a transport map, so none says anything about closure. Obstruction 4 establishes that propagation is binding and readout representation is ~500x smaller (S_4 readout defect 1.9e-9 against total 8.7e-7). Xu et al.'s calculus (join → max, disjoint union → sum, lexicographic product → multiplicative) is graph surgery on the term set, not transport — and our source contributions correlate 0.22–0.59 and need 8 singular components for 95% of energy, so they are neither a join nor a disjoint union of anything.
3. **Exact relations versus epsilon-relations, and coherent versus cancelling sums.** Xu et al. require S_iS_j = ±S_jS_i *exactly*; Prop. 1's realization-independence is representation-theoretic rigidity of a finitely presented quasi-Clifford algebra. Our own measured noncommutativity is max ||[e_c, e_c']|| ≈ 0.08 — an epsilon-commutator, with no robust/perturbative version anywhere in the paper. Round it to the nearest exact structure and you get commuting, empty frustration graph, beta = n, bound sum_i ⟨S_i⟩² ≤ 1024, i.e. |E[h_16,i]| ≲ 32.

One correction to the record that *strengthens* the verdict: Xu et al.'s Thm. 26 **does** handle signed combinations, (sum a_i⟨A_i⟩)² ≤ min_w (sum a_i²/w_i)·beta(G,w) — so the "sum of squares, no cancellation" reading was wrong. But the proof is Cauchy–Schwarz, saturated exactly when the terms are *coherent*. Our coherence is 0.0887–0.1106 with sign agreement 0.506, so the bound is loose by ~11x before any other slack. The one published technology in this cluster that looks like "bound the aggregate without computing the terms" is precisely the technology our measured incoherence defeats.

### Negative results that predict or explain our obstructions

Two are genuinely valuable as corroboration, even though neither yields a method:

- **Flora et al. → obstruction 1.** Their Fig. 1/3: greedy moment selection sits on an almost-flat plateau near NPA1 for k≤5 and does not reach NPA2 until k≈12, while the best k-subset reaches it at k=8. Their diagnosis — per-item importance scoring is uninformative under higher-order synergy — independently reproduces our measured fact that site_pow = 0, 1, 3 give 6.844e-7 / 6.791e-7 / 6.772e-7 at 25% retention, i.e. the importance ranking barely matters. "Immediate bias, then flat in the truncation parameter" is a greedy plateau in their language too. Their mechanism (constrained-optimum cooperation) differs from ours (cancellation), so this is corroboration of the *signature*, not of the cause.
- **Araújo et al. → obstruction 6.** Their honest ceiling — n=18 with MOSEK, n=25 with a first-order solver, after abandoning interior point specifically for scale — is independent confirmation that SDP-class methods do not reach n=1024. Redone in their friendliest setting, one eigendecomposition of the 2049-side Gram is ~9e10 FLOPs, so the entire per-network budget buys ~24 iterations at *one* layer; SCS needs 1e2–1e3, and we have 16 layers.
- Their Theorem 2 Markovian splitting / neighbourhood monomial set M(G, k_sigma, k_t) is structurally identical to our failed Cartan-compatible corner restriction 1_Y A 1_Y, and degenerates to "all degree-≤k monomials" on He-initialised dense weights where G(H) is complete. Their level-2 sharpness is evidence that low-order hierarchies work for *local* dynamics — which mildly corroborates obstruction 1's diagnosis rather than offering a way past it.

### One open item this cluster surfaced but cannot serve

Reviewing Flora et al. did sharpen the strictly warranted statement of obstruction 1: what we have measured is *"no importance-ranked and no low-rank/mode-selected compression of the terms works."* "No term-subset works" is an inference from incoherence, not a measurement — nothing in results.txt or either note is a **search over supports**. The live target is results.txt §19: the two-point channel at depth is worth 3.7x but costs ~49% of budget. That is a real open experiment, and re-summing a candidate subset from the age-1 term bank (computed once at 6.25%) is an O(n²) reduction at ~0.00% of budget, so thousands of subset evaluations are genuinely free for us.

But the experiment that would close the gap is **reweighted** sparse approximation — OMP/NNLS against the exact aggregate at one age, then testing whether support *and weights* transport across ages and networks — and that is aggregate-fitting, which comes from obstruction 2. Flora et al.'s decision variable is strictly binary with fixed Hamming weight (the entire Gumbel top-k apparatus exists to enforce ||x||_1 = k); no retained moment is ever reweighted, and it cannot be — a moment is an entry of the moment matrix or it is not. Binary include/exclude is term-preserving by definition, i.e. their search space is a strict subset of the one we have already measured to fail, minus the reweighting that would make it aggregate-preserving. Their two technical contributions are also answering a question we hold in closed form: our objective is f(S) = ||sum_{a in S} T_a − v||², quadratic in a linear map of the indicator, so their synergy diagnostic collapses to Delta(S_k) = (1/k)(sum_i ||T_i||² + 2 r·sum_i T_i) — determined entirely by the term Gram matrix, which our coherence/sign-agreement/top-q%-mass table already summarises more sharply, at zero samples. The right algorithm is column subset selection, not parallel tempering or an RBM/REINFORCE policy; those exist for oracles you can only query, and we have the matrix. Crediting this paper for that experiment would be exactly the fabricated verbal connection the provenance warning describes.
