# Cluster: Quantum metric spaces, Gromov-Hausdorff, Lipschitz seminorms

## Cluster verdict: Quantum metric spaces, Gromov–Hausdorff, Lipschitz seminorms

**Ruling: nothing in this cluster transfers.** Ten papers, all read in full, all ruled not-relevant, all surviving adversarial challenge. No paper contradicts, refines, or predicts a number in any of the six obstructions, and no experiment in the cluster is worth a FLOP. Provenance note: none of the ten mentions arXiv:2605.05179 or any machine-learning literature; six of them predate it by two decades. The claimed critique relationship is fabricated throughout.

### What the literature is for

Given a C*-algebra `A`, choose a seminorm `L` on its self-adjoint part (Connes' `||[D,a]||`, or `sup_x ||alpha_x(a)-a||/l(x)` for a compact group action). Its Monge–Kantorovich dual `rho_L(mu,nu) = sup{|mu(a)-nu(a)| : L(a) <= 1}` is a metric on the state space `S(A)`. The programme asks: when does `rho_L` metrize the weak-* topology (Rieffel 1998, 1999); what is the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between two such objects (Rieffel 2000, Kerr 2002, Kerr–Li 2004, Wu 2004); and does a sequence of finite-dimensional algebras — fuzzy tori, fuzzy spheres, matrix algebras — converge to a fixed infinite-dimensional space in that distance (Latrémolière 2003 and 2026, Aguilar–Kaad–Kyed 2021)? Two later papers extend the state-space-metric idea to trace spaces (Jacelon 2021) and to quantum permutation groups via optimal transport (Anshu–Jekel–Landry 2025). The deliverables are limit statements (`dist_q -> 0`), well-definedness and completeness theorems, and counterexamples separating levels of data. There is no dynamics anywhere in the cluster: no semigroup, no transfer operator, no time index, no closure, no unresolved sector, no memory kernel, no complexity or cost statement, and — with one exception — no rate.

### The single strongest idea, and what it would cost

The exception is **Rieffel, math/0011063, Theorem 8.2**. For an ergodic action of a compact group with faithful representation `pi` and length function `l`, let `B_n` be the hard isotypic truncation to components in `pi^{⊗n}`. Then `dist_q(A, B_n) <= delta_n = ∫_G phi_n(x) l(x) dx`, with `phi_n = chi^n/||chi^n||_1` an explicit Fejér-type approximate identity, and `delta_n` **depending only on `pi` and `l`, not on `A` or `alpha`**. That is an explicit, instance-universal error schedule for a hard spectral cutoff — formally the object our obstruction 1 says does not exist for us, proved by comparing the cutoff against a smoothed operator, which is formally the aggregate-vs-terms distinction of obstruction 2. (The first-pass reading that Thm 8.2 is rate-free is wrong and should not be repeated; the refutation is hypothesis-mismatch, not absence of a rate.)

Computationally at n=1024, L=16 it is worth exactly nothing, for three independent reasons. (i) There is no group. The rules hand us the exact weights of one fixed network; orthogonal invariance lives in the He ensemble, not the instance. With no `G` there is no `chi`, no `phi_n`, no `B_n`. (ii) The bound works because the truncation index and the seminorm are the *same* grading — the discarded modes are by construction the `L`-expensive ones, and faithfulness of `pi` is what concentrates `phi_n` at the identity. We have measured that no such alignment exists in any grading we have: ranking source units by `|p_beta| * ||U_beta||^s` gives 6.844 / 6.791 / 6.772e-07 at `s = 0/1/3` against exact 2.316e-07, i.e. the choice of ranking moves the answer ~1% while the truncation costs 3x, exactly as coherence 0.0887 and sign agreement 0.506 predict. (iii) The only transferable residue is "taper instead of cut", which is Fejér 1904 and self-destructs on FLOPs: a taper over all n singular directions saves zero compute (our spectral truncation exists only to buy budget — rank-256 transport is 0.39%, one exact source layer 6.25%, and we sit at 9.79% against a 10% free floor), while a taper over the r retained modes strictly increases dropped mass without touching the tail. It is also excluded a priori by the flatness measurement: rank 16 is no worse than rank 64, and a Cesàro reweighting along a flat axis is a convex average of equal scores.

The runner-up idea, **Aguilar–Kaad–Kyed Thm 3.10** (`L^max(beta_N(x)) <= L^max(x)`: a provably non-amplifying compression, via Prop 3.9's intertwining `delta_j beta_N = beta~_N delta~_j`), is the only positive template in the cluster and is worth knowing as a shape. Every ingredient — the isotypic spectral projection, `P_N D_j ⊂ D_j P_N`, Tomita–Takesaki `J`, the first-order condition, the coproduct — is a consequence of `S^2_q` being a quantum homogeneous space, and the paper contains no existence statement for intertwiners without symmetry. Worse, it is a wrong compass: ported naively `beta_N` *is* our failed compression (a), and it cannot even describe the rank-one aggregate-fitted kernel that works.

### The structural mismatch, precisely

Three axes, each of which alone is fatal.

1. **Fixed space in a limit vs dynamical closure under a budget.** All content in this cluster is in the `N -> infinity` behaviour of approximating one fixed infinite-dimensional algebra. Our algebra is already finite-dimensional and there is no limit to take; our problem is composing sixteen non-commuting nonlinear maps within 2^41 FLOPs. Instantiate the theorems on `M_1024` and they become true and empty: in finite dimensions all norms are equivalent, every seminorm is lower semicontinuous, every bounded set is totally bounded, every operator is compact, and the closed convex hull in Rieffel's bipolar theorem is the identity. A finite-dimensional operator system is 1-exact and sits at `dist_op = 0` from a matrix algebra, so Kerr–Li's headline theorem certifies that what we already have approximates itself.

2. **Seminorm fixed a priori vs induced by future observables.** Every `L` in the cluster comes from the object's own static geometry — a Dirac commutator, a group action with a length function, a bimodule norm on the universal calculus, `u Lip_1(u*Au) u*`. The weighting we need on a layer-`l` perturbation is the pulled-back linear-response transport to layer 16, which is not a metric-geometric datum of layer `l` at all. This is not a philosophical objection: it is measured, in the flatness of the importance exponent above. Relatedly, `rho_L` is a *signed* dual metric on states — the same shape as our score, and calling the cluster's distances "term-wise" is imprecise — but the supremum runs over the entire Lipschitz unit ball, which reinstates worst-case-over-observables, and every conversion of ball-closeness into structure in the cluster is a triangle-inequality sum (Kerr–Li Lemma 6.1 with an explicit factor `2n`; Anshu–Jekel–Landry's `||u...u||_Lip <= 2nl`; Wu's axiom `L_{m+n}(v ⊕ w) = max{L_m(v), L_n(w)}`). At coherence 0.0887 any bound of that shape is ~11x loose before it starts.

3. **Matrix tests vs joint distributions.** Kerr, Kerr–Li and Wu exist *entirely* to detect the gap between order-unit (scalar-state) data and matrix-level data. Our observable algebra is commutative — functions of the Gaussian input — so by Stinespring/Arveson positive implies completely positive and the whole hierarchy collapses identically; `CS_n(V)` is an `n^2`-fold vectorisation of `CS_1` carrying no new information. Obstruction 4's underdetermination is between the joint law of 1024 real neurons and its one-dimensional marginals: a larger *commutative* system containing cross-products `a_i a_j`, on the state side. Same English word "underdetermines", different mathematics. This is the cluster's most seductive false friend and should be recorded as null rather than as a conceptual rhyme.

Compute veto, independently: `dist_q` is an infimum over all operator systems and all pairs of embeddings; Wu's `D_{L_n}` is a supremum over all matrix levels, needing elements of `M_1024(V)` with `dim V ~ 1025`, ~1.07e9 entries before any norm is evaluated; Rieffel's finite approximation (Prop 13.18) needs an eps-net of `S(A)` with covering number `((D/eps)+5)^(Fin_L(eps)-1)`. Against a ceiling where `n^3` per layer is affordable (covariance sandwich 2.34% over 16 layers) and `n^4` is not.

### Negative results that explain a measured obstruction

- **Rieffel math/0011063 §8, read contrapositively.** Its hypothesis set is diagnostic: a truncation admits a universal error schedule when the grading it truncates is the grading that defines the seminorm. That names precisely the property obstruction 1 measures as absent, and it is the cleanest available statement of what a *working* compression would need — a grading in which the answer is a rapidly decaying series. No method, but a citable framing.
- **Latrémolière math/0310214.** Its only two concrete compression maps are the smearing `alpha^phi = ∫ phi(g) alpha^g dg` (an isotypic multiplier — our failed compression (a) and its rank-graded relatives (c), (d)) and Prop 5.1's `P_n`, literally the Cartan conditional expectation onto the diagonal of `M_n` (our failed compression (b)). Two of our four falsified compressions are the cluster's entire constructive toolkit. That is confirmation that the toolkit is drawn from the family we killed, not an independent prediction.
- **Wu math/0411475, Def 3.1 axiom (2).** `L_{m+n}(v ⊕ w) = max{L_m(v), L_n(w)}` is the formal statement that Lipschitz structure aggregates summands by max and never lets them interfere. It is a hypothesis, not a theorem, and it is exactly the property our path sum lacks. It explains nothing we did not measure, but it states the mismatch in one line.
- **Anshu–Jekel–Landry Lemma 4.15.** `W_tensor(phi_0,phi_0) = 1 - (1/n) Σ_ij ∫ tau(u_ij)^2 dmu_0` says the irreducible cost of a factorised relaxation is the variance of the extremal decomposition — the right *shape* for obstruction 4 plus obstruction 5 (marginal defect is a second-moment object). It is Jensen's inequality in a C*-algebra, with an RHS the paper itself lists as open to compute (Question 5.8), and it cannot produce 7.07e-07 or 3.1x.
- **Not predictions, despite appearances:** Kerr–Li Thm 6.2 and Kerr Thm 4.11 (non-determination is order-unit vs matrix level, orthogonal to our score); Rieffel 1999 Example 7.1 (non-determination is of which seminorm generates a metric, not of a joint from marginals).

### Worth reading in full

Almost nothing. Two exceptions, both short and both for the writeup rather than the estimator:

- **Rieffel, math/0011063, Section 8 only (~5 pages): Thm 8.2 and the construction of `phi_n`.** It is the cluster's only explicit, instance-universal truncation rate, and reading the proof is the cheapest way to state precisely why our four compressions fail — the theorem's hypothesis is the alignment we measured to be absent, and its universality-in-`A` is the same signature as our kernel's across-network coefficient spread (sd 0.00314) falling below MC seed spread (0.031–0.040). One paragraph of the final writeup should cite it; no code follows.
- **Aguilar–Kaad–Kyed, Section 3 only (~3 pages): Prop 3.9 and Thm 3.10.** The one non-amplifying compression theorem in the cluster, with its symmetry dependencies fully exposed. Useful as the negative template: it shows what would have to be true for a compression to be provably harmless, and makes it immediately visible that a fixed He-init weight matrix supplies none of it.

Everything else in this cluster — Rieffel 1998 and 1999, Kerr 2002, Kerr–Li 2004, Wu 2004, Latrémolière 2003 and 2026, Jacelon 2021, Anshu–Jekel–Landry 2025 — is adequately served by abstract plus the adversarial rulings already on record. One genuine shared object is worth noting for honesty and then setting aside: the Mehler/Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup *is* a group average whose isotypic components are the Wiener chaoses, so our Hermite ReLU closure is literally an instance of Rieffel's `alpha_f` setting. The papers add nothing over standard Gaussian analysis there — we already ship the exact coefficients `d_1 = Phi(t)`, `d_k = phi(t) He_{k-2}(-t)` — and obstruction 4 says a sharper bound on marginal chaos resolution cannot move the score, since the readout's representation defect (1.9e-09 at `S_4`) is ~500x below the binding error.
