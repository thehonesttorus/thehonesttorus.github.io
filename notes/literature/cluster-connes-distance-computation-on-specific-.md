# Cluster: Connes distance computation on specific noncommutative spaces

## Cluster verdict: Connes distance computation on specific noncommutative spaces — **nothing here transfers**

### What this body of work is actually for

These three papers compute a *metric* on the state space of a fixed noncommutative algebra. The setup is always the same: you are handed a spectral triple (algebra A, Hilbert space H, Dirac operator D) that is *given a priori and never changes*, and you evaluate Connes' distance d(ω₁,ω₂) = sup{ |ω₁(a) − ω₂(a)| : ‖[D, π(a)]‖_op ≤ 1 } between two states. The entire technical content is the difficulty of that supremum: it is an operator-norm-constrained optimisation over an infinite- (Moyal) or (2n+1)-dimensional (fuzzy sphere) algebra, and the papers' achievement is finding the handful of cases where it closes in a formula. Prajapat et al. get closed forms in su(2) irrep labels; Devi et al. get d = Ñ·‖Δρ‖_tr under a unitary-transitivity hypothesis; Licht does not compute a Connes distance at all — he Berezin-smears a Coulomb operator on a θ-deformed space. There is no dynamics anywhere in the cluster: no composition of maps, no layer index, no error propagated forward, no truncation parameter with a rate, no FLOP count, not one numerical error bar. Devi et al. report that their optimisation is "virtually intractable even with Mathematica" at **algebra dimension 4**. Our algebra dimension is 1024, sixteen times over.

That single sentence is the whole verdict for a reader who knows our problem: this literature measures distance on one frozen space; we must close a sixteen-step non-Gaussian propagation under a budget. The two activities share no object.

### The strongest idea in the cluster, and what it would actually cost

The best candidate is Devi et al.'s **collapse theorem** (arXiv:1608.05270, lines ~1955–1966): *if* the unitary group acts transitively on trace-norm level sets of Δρ, the entire transverse infimum — the intractable term-level object — reduces to a universal numerical constant times one scalar norm. That is formally our obstruction-2 shape: don't approximate the terms, approximate the aggregate, and the aggregate is a universal coefficient times one simple functional. It would, if it transferred, *derive* rather than fit the universal depth schedule in our shipped kernel a_l·t·σ³, and would suggest re-basing the kernel from {σ³, t·σ³} onto a nuclear norm of the omitted source contribution.

Computationally at n=1024, L=16 this would mean: per layer, form the accumulated omitted-source object, take its trace/nuclear norm (an n×n SVD, ~n³ ≈ 10⁹ FLOPs, affordable by obstruction 6 — comparable to the 2.34% covariance sandwich), and use ‖·‖_tr in place of σ³ as the kernel's scalar. Cheap enough to run. It still should not be run, for three reasons. **(a) The hypothesis is absent and the paper supplies no test for it.** The premise is a transitive symmetry action relating the terms. Our input law N(0,I) is O(n)-invariant but the *weights are fixed and given exactly* — that is the task — so the layer-l source terms are determined by specific W₁…W_l with no group relating them. Obstruction 1 measures the negation directly: per-source-unit sign agreement 0.506–0.510 against chance 0.500, coherence 0.089–0.111 (results.txt §1b). Terms on a transitive unitary orbit do not look like that. Devi et al. verify their condition case-by-case by explicit construction of the state manifold, and find it **fails on the fuzzy sphere at every finite n**. **(b) The headroom is ~13%.** 87% of the omitted tail already lies in {σ³, t·σ³}, with the σ³ coefficient statistically zero on the diagonal channel and the t·σ³ coefficient statistically zero on the two-point channel — orthogonal directions, both fitted, both shipped (results.txt §19). A nuclear-norm re-basing contests the 13% remainder, against a 45× gap to the leader. **(c) It is the wrong aggregate.** See below.

### The structural mismatch, precisely

Three mismatches, each independently fatal.

1. **A seminorm fixed a priori versus one induced by future observables.** The Lipschitz ball {a : ‖[D,π(a)]‖ ≤ 1} is defined *before* you know what you are measuring, by a Dirac operator that comes with the space. Our weighting problem is the opposite: the thing that determines whether a source term matters is what the *remaining* transports W_{l+1}…W_16 and the readout do to it. Every one of our four failed compressions (SVD spectrum, Cartan corner, rank-R symmetric memory, polarised source diagrams) was a fixed-basis, future-blind weight — and the cluster offers only more of the same kind. Devi et al.'s one *successful* construction is the sequence π_N(a_s) of finite-rank projections onto Dirac eigen-spinor subspaces: literally an instance of obstruction 1(a), spectral truncation of the transport-side element. Wrong-direction precedent.

2. **Norms don't cancel; our target does.** This is the decisive point and it kills the aggregate hook cleanly. A supremum over a Lipschitz ball selects the *magnitude-maximising* direction; the resulting functional is a norm, and every aggregate appearing anywhere in this cluster is manifestly positive — sums of P_l3², Casimir factors n(n+1) − n₃², partition functions, squared moduli, trace norms. Our target is a *signed* sum whose coherence is 0.09: the object a dual-ball extremal reconstructs is sum‖term‖, which our own diagnostics measure as ~11× inflated relative to the coherent residue we need. The cluster contains no cancelling sum at all, so no lesson about our regime survives even as analogy.

3. **Scalar tests on a frozen space versus matrix tests under a budget.** These papers evaluate one number between two states. We must carry a rank-structured n×n covariance plus off-diagonal third cumulants through sixteen compositions with n³/layer as the ceiling. Devi et al.'s 7-parameter operator-norm minimisation at algebra dimension 3, declared intractable at dimension 4, is not a cost model that survives contact with obstruction 6.

There is also a **provenance point**: this NCG line is not merely inapplicable, it is *already run to ground in our own notes*. `notes/groupoid-carrier-and-cartan-truncation.md` §5 makes Connes–van Suijlekom spectral truncation the basis of an explicit falsifiable prediction and measures it to fail (rank 128 is 2.4× worse than exact; the Cartan-compatible repair is slightly *worse*, results.txt lines ~380–391). `notes/finite-resolution-question-algebra.md` line 89 already identifies Connes' distance for our actual Dirac operator — the Gaussian Hodge–Dirac supplied by the input state — as the Kantorovich W₁ metric, and its spectral truncations as Wiener chaos resolutions. So the cluster's generic machinery is already instantiated in our setting, correctly, and already falsified. Three papers about the *finite spectral distance on two particular fixed spaces* add no mechanism to that.

Finally, Connes distance cannot serve as a scoring-aligned surrogate: we are scored on a fixed final-layer MSE against a baked ground truth, so there is no freedom to choose the metric.

### Negative results that predict or explain our obstructions

**One partial, none load-bearing.**

- **Devi et al., fuzzy-sphere non-saturation** — the supremum is *never* attained at any finite n, so finite-rank truncation is permanently biased below the true value. Signature matches obstruction 1 (immediate bias, then flat in the truncation parameter, rank 16 no worse than rank 64). But the **mechanism differs**, and the difference matters: theirs is an *attainment/compactness* failure — no optimal element exists in the algebra rather than its multiplier completion. Ours is a *conditioning* failure — dropping fraction f of terms injects ~√(f·n) typical terms against a signal of order 0.1·n, giving ~22% per source layer at f = 1/2, n = 1024. results.txt §1b already derives this quantitatively, and it is sharper and more predictive than anything in the paper. Same word, different phenomenon; no discriminant we can evaluate without a Dirac operator we don't have.
- **Licht §7** (E² operator integral diverges though the squared symbol is finite) and **Prajapat et al. eq. 132** (pure-state distance depends on spectator-sector labels though D acts only on the left) are both "the reduced/first-moment description does not determine the full object." They rhyme with obstruction 4 (marginals do not determine the joint) and with the caveat at results.txt §19 that the rank-one reduction of K21_far captures 97–99% of the mean direction but only 49.5–73.0% of induced next-layer variance. But we have already measured both, with error bars (perfect-marginals oracle 7.07e-7). **Confirming a measured obstruction has no value; only routing around one does.** Neither paper supplies a bound, a rate, or a repair.

### What is worth reading in full

**Nothing in this cluster.** If a single item had to be read, it would be Devi et al. §5 (arXiv:1608.05270, the collapse theorem and the fuzzy-sphere non-saturation proof, ~4 pages) — but only to confirm at first hand that the collapse hypothesis is a transitive-symmetry condition we provably lack, and that their one success is a spectral truncation of exactly the family we measured to fail. That is a confirmation, not a lead, and this summary already carries it.

**One residue worth carrying to the parent, as a note and not a lead:** a seminorm *induced by future observables* — weighting source terms by what the remaining transports and the readout actually do to them, rather than by any fixed basis — remains a live untested idea in our setting, and is not in the class of our four failures. It must come from the Connes/Rieffel template directly (Rieffel's best-approximation-from-subalgebras framing, already cited in our own notes), not from this cluster. And it inherits a hard constraint from mismatch 2: any such construction must select the *cancellation pattern*, not a norm-saturating extremal, because the extremal direction is precisely the 11×-inflated object.

**Recommendation: drop all three from the reading list.**
