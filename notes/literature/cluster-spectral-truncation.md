# Cluster: Spectral truncation, cut-offs, operator systems

## Cluster verdict: Spectral truncation, cut-offs, operator systems

**Bottom line: nothing here transfers as a method.** The cluster is the literature ancestry of two of the four compressions we have already implemented and measured to fail. Its one negative result that shape-matches obstruction 1 is driven by a mechanism with no finite-*n* content. Recommend citing it as prior art for the *phenomenon*, spending no further compute on it.

### What this body of work is for

Encode a space as a spectral triple `(A, H, D)`: an algebra of functions, a Hilbert space, and a Dirac operator whose spectrum carries the metric via Connes' formula `d(φ,φ') = sup{φ(a)−φ'(a) : ‖[D,a]‖ ≤ 1}`. Now suppose you can only see eigenmodes of `D` below a cut-off Λ. The single construction of the entire cluster is `a ↦ P_Λ a P_Λ`. The result is not an algebra — products leave the corner — so the object of study becomes an *operator system*, and the research program asks what geometric data (metric, topology, isometry group, dimension) survives, and whether the truncated state spaces converge to the original in Gromov–Hausdorff distance as Λ → ∞. It is an existence-and-convergence program about approximating one **fixed infinite-dimensional object** by finite-dimensional ones. There is no dynamics: the projection is applied **once**, never composed. There is no cost model. And with two exceptions (D'Andrea Prop. 5.11(ii), two-sided, circle-and-Fejér-specific; Lemma 6.9, `‖f − B_θ(f)‖_∞ ≤ √(πθ)·L_D(f)`, per-element) there are no finite-cut-off error rates on any target functional at all. A reader of our problem can decide relevance from one sentence: we are not approximating a limit — our algebra is already `M_1024` — we are closing a 16-fold composition of non-closed maps under a metered budget.

### The single strongest idea, and what it would cost at n=1024

The strongest is Connes–van Suijlekom §2.3/§4.3: Choi–Effros duality makes "truncate on the point side" and "truncate on the moment side" **exactly dual, not identical** operations (Prop. 4.6: the Toeplitz system `C(S¹)^(n)` is completely order isomorphic to the dual of the Fejér–Riesz system `C*(Z)^(n)`). That is the one place in the literature where the aggregate-vs-terms distinction — which separates every success from every failure we have measured — is a theorem rather than a slogan. Every failed compression of ours truncates on the point/unit/mode side; the shipped `a_l · t · σ³` kernel truncates on the moment side.

Computationally at n=1024, L=16, it prescribes **nothing we do not already ship**. The duality's concrete form is a support restriction on `Z`, licensed by translation invariance; `K21_far` has no such structure, and our `{σ³, t·σ³}` basis is a Hermite basis in the per-neuron scalar `t = μ/σ` from the Gaussian closure, not a dual pairing on an operator system.

The only *new* budget-sized experiment anywhere in the cluster is derived, not stated: D'Andrea Prop. 5.1 makes the resolution floor a function of `‖D_Λ‖`, not `rank(P_Λ)`, which would predict that a **norm-preserving** compression (rescale the retained rank-R modes to restore the discarded operator norm) beats a plain energy truncation. Cost: free — rank-256 transport is 0.39% of budget, the rescale is O(1). It is dead on arrival against our own data: obstruction 2's kernel already fits the omitted tail as a one-parameter aggregate with a universal depth schedule, which absorbs any global rescaling of the retained part by construction. Norm restoration is a strictly worse special case of a fit we already run.

And the only quantity the cluster would have us *evaluate* — the Connes distance — is at n=1024 an SDP over 1024×1024 matrices, far past the n³-per-layer affordability line (covariance sandwich 2.34% of budget over 16 layers; one exact source layer 6.25%). Our score does not depend on it.

### The structural mismatch, precisely

Five axes, each independently sufficient:

1. **Fixed space in a limit vs. closing a dynamics under budget.** No composition of maps appears anywhere in these papers — no semigroup, no error accumulation, no memory. Our binding fact is the negation: Lemma 1 of `/home/user/thehonesttorus.github.io/notes/finite-resolution-question-algebra.md` proves ReLU's chaos expansion has nonzero components at every even degree, so **no** finite resolution is closed under a layer, and the memory generated by that non-closure is the whole problem. Fiore–Pisacane make this explicit against themselves: their reduction is declared legitimate only under `PH = HP` (Sec. 2), i.e. the retained sector is dynamically invariant and the projected theory is exact. They occupy the memoryless corner of projection dynamics; we live entirely in its complement.
2. **A seminorm fixed a priori vs. one induced by future observables.** Their metric is a sup over the *entire* unit ball `{a : ‖[D,a]‖ ≤ 1}`. Obstruction 4 says our target is one specific benign functional reached through 16 transports, and that the representation defect at the readout is ~500× smaller than the total error (S₄ scores 1.9e-09 against a deployed 8.7e-07). Worse, Connes–vS Thm 4.20 runs the wrong way: truncation makes states *farther* apart than `W_1`, so it cannot even furnish the upper bound one would want.
3. **Term-preserving vs. aggregate-approximating.** `PaP` keeps a corner exactly and discards the complement exactly — the definition of what coherence 0.09 punishes. The smoothed variant does not escape: Lemma 6.9 bounds error **per element**, and per-element bounds sum incoherently. Perturbing 1024 source terms by ε moves the answer by ~√n·ε against a signal of ~0.1n typical terms. That is compressions (c) and (d), already measured.
4. **Scalar/element tests vs. matrix tests.** Complete positivity and the order-unit structure are the cluster's technical core, and are the right notion for their questions. Ours is measured non-binding: one negative eigenvalue at ~1e-4 of trace, cone projection buys ~2% (5.183e-07 → 5.092e-07), a larger floor hurts (8.538e-07 at 1e-04).
5. **Symmetry buys every quantitative success there, and we have none.** D'Andrea Prop. 6.10's exactness (`d(Ψ_z,Ψ_z') = |z−z'|` at every finite θ) needs a Heisenberg group acting transitively with coherent-state orbits and a ladder-structured Dirac operator. Fiore–Pisacane's counterterm `x_h = π_Λ[f₁(L²) L_{h(D+1)} f₂(L²)]` is fixed by exact so(D+1) representation theory, with **no error functional and no residual** — closure is exact because the symmetry is exact, and nothing in the construction survives losing it. Our transport is a product of fixed He-random matrices with state-dependent ReLU Jacobians: no group, no ladder, no coherent family. The universality we *do* exploit (kernel coefficient sd 0.00314 across networks vs. 0.031–0.040 across MC seeds at layer 16) is a property of the He-init **ensemble**, established empirically in §12 of the diagnostics; no argument flows from these papers to it.

### Negative results that predict or explain our obstructions

Two, both worth recording, neither predictive of a number:

- **Connes–van Suijlekom**: `prop(C(S¹)^(n)) = 2` **independently of n**, plus the introduction's own statement that the C\*-algebra generated by `PAP` is "a non-informative full matrix algebra." A published defect that is *constant in the truncation parameter* is the exact shape of obstruction 1 — "immediate bias, then flat," rank 16 no worse than rank 64. But `prop` counts algebraic **products** of retained observables; our pipeline never multiplies two truncated observables, it applies a linear transport to a tensor and sums. And by our Lemma 1 the ReLU pullback lies in `E^{on}` for no finite n, so `prop = ∞` is the only value the invariant returns — no quantitative content.
- **D'Andrea–Lizzi–Martinetti Prop. 5.1 / 5.4**: the minimal separable length under a cut-off is `‖D_Λ‖⁻¹`, a function of the norm and not the rank; and for any self-adjoint finite-rank `D_Λ`, distinct pure states are at *infinite* distance, at every rank. Theorem-shaped "raising the rank does not buy resolution." The mechanism is finite rank against a **continuum** of pure states — pigeonhole with no finite-cardinality version, and the paper supplies none. Our unit space has 1024 points and we keep 16–128 of them.

Our own measurement already explains the flatness *and* predicts the magnitude, which neither paper does: coherence 0.0887–0.1106 at every source age, sign agreement 0.506, so dropping a fraction f injects ~√(f·n) typical terms against a signal of ~0.1·n, i.e. ~22% per source layer at f = 1/2. A post-hoc analogy that predicts no number does not displace a measured one that does.

The cluster's confirmatory value is real but retrospective: it is the ancestry of spectral rank-128 (5.461e-07 vs. 2.316e-07 exact, non-monotone in rank) and the Cartan-compatible corner `1_Y A 1_Y` (6.318e-07 at top 50%, *worse* than the Cartan-breaking version, with the importance ranking barely mattering: 6.844/6.791/6.772e-07 at site_pow 0/1/3). It is prior art for why those failed, published in a neighbouring field, and nothing more.

### What to read in full

**Connes–van Suijlekom §2.3, §4.3, §5 only** — roughly ten pages. Read §2.3/§4.3 for the Choi–Effros dual pair as vocabulary for the aggregate-vs-terms distinction we are currently stating informally, and §5 (Toeplitz retains the full S¹ isometry group, circulant discretisation retains only Z_m, both GH-convergent) as a clean statement of why two compressions with the same convergence can differ in what symmetry they respect. The one place it might sharpen an open question is §19 of the diagnostics: `K21_far`'s mean-direction coefficient vector is 97–99% explained by σ³ alone, yet the rank-one reduction captures only 50–73% of its induced next-layer variance — an aggregate that is analytic but whose rank-one form is lossy. Go in expecting a framing, not a method; the only cone machinery it offers that could touch a moment problem (Carathéodory–Vandermonde flat extension, Thms 4.12–4.15) is Toeplitz-specific and lands on obstruction 3, where the whole prize is 2%. Note the paper is already in the bibliography of our finite-resolution note.

**D'Andrea–Lizzi–Martinetti and Fiore–Pisacane: abstracts only.** Full text was retrieved for both (7,442 and 4,828 lines) and read; there is nothing in either that a careful abstract reading would have missed for our purposes.
