# Cluster verdict — Fuzzy spaces, matrix geometry, quasi-coherent states

**Bottom line: nothing in this cluster transfers.** Ten papers, all retrieved (nine full text, one from arXiv LaTeX source after alphaXiv failed on the pre-2007 identifier), all adversarially challenged, all rulings survived as not-relevant. The cluster's residual value is four *negative* results that independently corroborate obstruction 1 — none of which changes a decision, because we measured the same thing more sharply ourselves.

## What this body of work is actually for

These papers solve an **inverse problem in a limit**: given a fixed classical geometry (a sphere, CP^k, a coadjoint orbit, a spacetime, a point cloud), construct a finite tuple of matrices X^1..X^d on one common Hilbert space whose commutator algebra reproduces that geometry's topology, dimension, curvature or Chern class, exactly or as N → ∞. The success criterion is *preservation of a discrete invariant* or *convergence of a symbol calculus*, and the deliverable is a construction, not an estimator. There is no state being propagated, no time or depth index, no accumulating truncation error, no cost model, and — verified by grep across the cluster — essentially no approximation theory at all: `cumulant` 0 hits in every paper checked, `error`/`bound`/`converg` present only in topological or trace-class senses, `truncat`/`closure` 0 hits in Schneiderbauer–Steinacker and Oriti et al., no numbered theorem anywhere in Abanov et al., and Barrett states outright at line 106 that "a notion of approximation is not investigated here." Our problem is the opposite shape in every axis that matters: fixed n = 1024, fixed weights, no limit available, error measured in a specific directional contraction against a metered FLOP budget. The single sentence a reader needs: **this literature approximates a fixed space by matrices; we must close a dynamical propagation under a budget, and it has no object that propagates.**

## The strongest single idea, and what it would cost

The best candidate is not a compression but a *projection class*: the group-averaging conditional expectation. Balachandran §6.3, eq. (6.26), C(A) = ⟨∪_i U^(i)⟩′, is genuinely not the failed corner restriction 1_Y A 1_Y — it is the range of an averaging projector E(a) = avg_u u a u*, and eq. (6.25) returns one scalar per arm times a projector summing over all units. That is aggregate-preserving, i.e. structurally on the winning side of obstruction 2. (This is a real correction to the first-pass reading of that paper and should be recorded.) The same idea recurs as Berezin–Toeplitz smearing in Karabali–Nair §5, where the authors explicitly *reject* hard mode projection P(F) = F₊ ("this product is not associative in general... the lack of associativity limits the utility of this approach") in favour of a soft penalty (h/2)Φ†[n(n+1) − T²]Φ that suppresses modes continuously.

Instantiated at n = 1024, L = 16 it dies twice over. (i) **It has no group.** The papers hand you the invariance free (arm structure of a Hasse diagram; SU(k+1) acting transitively on a coadjoint orbit). The obvious candidate here, S_n on the 1024 source units, is wrong on its face: κ₃ depends on per-unit (t_a, σ_a) and the target is per-neuron E[h_16,j]. The correct conditional expectation is one conditioned on (t_a, σ_a) — which *is* a regression onto {σ³, tσ³, t²σ³, ...}. We have already run exactly that, twice, and fitted it to saturation: §11, diagonal channel, R² = 0.869 at rank 2 with rank 3 adding 0.000 and the σ³ coefficient statistically zero; §19, two-point channel, R² = 0.974–0.989 on σ³ alone with tσ³ and higher fitted at ~0. The invariant-subalgebra picture, correctly instantiated, is the shipped kernel. (ii) **Soft weighting saves zero FLOPs.** Karabali–Nair's fix keeps a full-rank operator and *adds* an action term. Under our metering the only reason to compress is the budget, so a smooth full-rank spectral weight costs what the exact computation costs; their remedy is unavailable at precisely the point we need it. The measured ceiling confirms this is not where the money is: the deterministic ladder plateaus at 3.3e-06 against 1.19e-06 for the sampled channels, because "the reduction captures the ALIGNED part of the omitted sector only" (§13), and §19's middle column shows the rank-one mean-direction form captures only 50–73% of the two-point tail's induced variance. The binding remainder is the **non-invariant, per-neuron** part — exactly what any invariance-averaging formalism is blind to by construction.

For completeness, the other candidate mechanisms are measured-dead rather than merely unproven:
- **Anti-Wick / contravariant symbol** (Karabali–Nair eqs. 53–55), F = ∫dμ(g)|g⟩f(g)⟨g| — a signed density over symmetric rank-one projectors — is the continuum version of our compression (d): §17 polarized memory at R = 1024 scores 2.018e-06 against the deployed 1.167e-06. Its exactness in the fuzzy case is bought entirely by the transitive group action; with generic Gaussian W_l there is no orbit, no coherent-state family, no deconvolution.
- **Commutator-Laplacian mode selection** (Abanov et al. eq. 26; Steinacker's spin decomposition) is n⁴ before it is anything else. Δ = Σ_a[X_a,[X_a,·]] acts on Mat(1024), a 1.05e6-dimensional space; one apply is ~4n³ ≈ 4.3e9 FLOPs (0.2% of 2^41), so even a 256-vector Krylov basis is ~1.1e12 ≈ 50% of the entire per-network budget — and delivers a term-level truncation at the end of it.
- **Berezin smearing inserted in the transport** is a linear, term-independent operator, i.e. the same functional form as compression (a), whose damage is flat in the cut sharpness (rank 128: 5.544e-07; rank 64: 5.917e-07; rank 32: 6.229e-07) — and sharpness is the only axis smearing moves along.

## The structural reason it does not transfer

Three precise mismatches, in decreasing order of decisiveness.

**1. The seminorm is fixed a priori by geometry; ours is induced by future observables.** Every reduction in this cluster ranks modes by a geometric energy functional decided before any target is named: commutator-Laplacian eigenvalue, spin s ≤ n, the eigenvalue gap of a d×d Hessian (Schneiderbauer–Steinacker Def. 4), Frobenius-form bimodule structure (Barrett Lemma 3). Our measurement forecloses the entire family, not any particular metric: the importance ranking *barely matters* — site_pow = 0 gives 6.844e-07, site_pow = 3 gives 6.772e-07, against 2.316e-07 exact. The damage comes from dropping terms, not from ranking them badly. So "a better a-priori mode ordering" is a closed direction, and every method here is one. What works instead is a seminorm defined by what happens downstream: fit the aggregate against the readout residual in {σ³, tσ³}. No paper in the cluster has an error functional at all, so none can express that.

**2. Approximating a fixed space in a limit vs. closing a propagation under a budget.** These constructions are exact by design (Balachandran's C(A) loses *nothing*; Karabali–Nair's eq. (qhe7) is derived only after "terms which vanish as n → ∞ have been dropped"; Steinacker 2019's physically central background M^{3,1}_n is infinite-dimensional and finite-dimensionalising it is listed as an open problem). A construction that loses nothing carries no information about what a *lossy* projection costs — which is the only question we have. Correspondingly there is no depth recursion anywhere in the cluster, no memory kernel, and no Q-sector propagator: Balachandran's p in eq. (4.11) *is* the Hilbert–Schmidt Mori–Zwanzig P-projector, but the paper stops at "make the Laplacian well-defined," where §8 already carries corr(F, tail) = 0.879, |F|/|tail| = 2.47, and the double-counting explanation for the path sum's reversal beyond depth 8.

**3. Scalar tests vs. matrix tests.** The invariants preserved are scalars or integers: Tr (Steinacker 2010 eq. 21), Chern number, intrinsic dimension, a structure space. Steinacker's eq. (21) is the cluster's best aggregate-preservation statement and it preserves exactly *one* functional. Our aggregate is a 1024-vector — the source path sum contracted against the propagated mean — and Tr bounds no particular matrix–vector product. "Aggregate-preserving" in obstruction 2's sense and in eq. (21)'s sense are different objects sharing an English word.

**4. The primitive object does not exist for us**, and manufacturing it would be the fabricated bridge the provenance note warns about. These constructions need d Hermitian matrices on one common Hilbert space with commutators defining a Poisson structure. Our W_l are non-symmetric, map *between* distinct layer spaces, and form a chain of sixteen, not a d-tuple on one space. Symmetrising to W + Wᵀ invents the object rather than finding it.

## Negative results that predict our obstructions

Four, all corroborative of obstruction 1, none decision-changing. Ranked by how mechanistic they are:

1. **Abanov et al. (2507.21135), Appendix C — the sharpest.** It names our object and rules it out by mechanism: "Deep quantum configurations, characterized by ‖[X_a,X_b]‖₂ = O(‖X_a X_b‖₂)... This holds e.g. for random matrices... their spectral distribution near the cutoff is very distinct from geometric configurations (power law versus sqrt(x))." Sixteen i.i.d. He-initialised matrices are exactly this class, and the claim is that there is no IR/UV separation in the commutator-Laplacian spectrum, i.e. low modes carry no privileged share — a mechanistic prediction of the flat-in-rank signature. The same appendix adds that non-local observables |x⟩⟨y| + h.c. sit at high Laplacian energy and are discarded *first* by a smoothness-ranked truncation, which is the shape of obstruction 5 crossed with obstruction 1. Both passages are one-paragraph asides with no equations and an explicit "out of the scope of this work."
2. **Steinacker (1911.03162), l.163–167** — same content in prose: "there is no way to assign any geometric significance to generic matrix configurations {Y^a}... most of the configuration space is basically 'white noise'." One sentence, no criterion, no theorem.
3. **Schneiderbauer–Steinacker (1601.08007), §7.1** — an operational applicability test: "If there is no clear hierarchy of eigenvalues of H, the matrices simply do not contain enough information to extract a meaningful semi-classical manifold," hard-coded as λ_crit in Algorithm 2. Our transport spectra (§5: 23/85/175 directions for 50/90/99% of energy on h_8 → z_15) are a smooth power law with no gap anywhere, so **by its own criterion the method would decline to run on our operators.** But it would be right for the wrong reason and would mislead in the counterfactual: our failure is cancellation (coherence 0.0887–0.1106, sign agreement 0.506), not spectral resolution, so a gap would not have rescued the compressions. The test is blind to the mechanism that binds.
4. **Karabali–Nair, §5** — practitioners who tried hard mode projection in their own setting, found it structurally defective, and switched to soft weighting. Independent testimony from another field that hard cuts are the wrong *kind* of compression. Their diagnosed mechanism (loss of associativity, compounding with the number of products) does not match our signature (immediate bias, flat in the parameter, rank 16 no worse than rank 64), so it has no retrodictive power here.

Worth stating plainly: all four say *that* naive truncation fails on random matrices. We know *how much*, per source layer, with a quantitative mechanism (coherence 0.0887–0.1106 against a random-sign floor of 1/√1024 = 0.0312, implying a coherent-drift fraction of only 8–10%; dropping fraction f injects ~√(fn) typical terms against an answer of 0.1n). Reading these papers through our own numbers actually *sharpens the kill*: the 8–10% coherent drift is precisely the rank-one tσ³ direction the shipped kernel already extracts, so any branch-selection or coherent-mode argument imported from this literature recovers something we already have and says nothing about the incoherent 90% where the residual error lives.

## What is worth reading in full

**Nothing.** No paper in this cluster warrants full-text reading for this problem; all ten were read in full during the study and the return was four confirmatory asides.

If someone wants the primary sources for the negative results — for citation in a writeup, not for method — the only two passages worth having in hand are **Abanov et al. arXiv:2507.21135, Appendix C** (~1 page: the deep-quantum/random-matrix taxonomy and the non-local-observable caveat, plus §4.4's admission that its own compression discards |x⟩⟨y| + |y⟩⟨x| operators encoding "correlations between distant or disconnected regions") and **Balachandran et al. hep-th/9403067, §6.3** (~2 pages: the group-averaging conditional expectation, which is the cluster's one aggregate-side construction and the one thing the first pass misfiled). Both are quotable in a paragraph. Neither changes a line of code.

Finally, on provenance: the claim that any of these papers "critiques and advances beyond" arXiv:2605.05179 is fabricated in every case and was verified false by direct text search. Seven of the ten predate that work by six to thirty-two years; the three 2025–2026 papers contain zero occurrences of `cumulant`, `propagat`, `closure`, `ReLU`, `estimator` or `neural network` in any load-bearing sense. The framing was correctly not inherited.
