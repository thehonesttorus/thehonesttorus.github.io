1b. Why: the path sum cancels.  Per-source-unit terms of kappa_3(z_16), network 0.
   'share' = cumulative share of sum|term| held by the top q% of units, ranked by |term| mass.
   'sign agreement' = fraction of (unit, output) terms whose sign matches the total's (chance = 0.500).
   'coherence' = ||sum of terms|| / sum||term||, averaged over outputs.

   source  age |  top 1%   10%    25%    50%  | sign agreement | coherence
     14     1  |  0.048  0.338  0.690  0.961  |     0.506      |  0.0887
     13     2  |  0.049  0.344  0.701  0.960  |     0.506      |  0.1000
     12     3  |  0.043  0.312  0.648  0.944  |     0.508      |  0.0961
     11     4  |  0.041  0.302  0.617  0.924  |     0.508      |  0.0950
     10     5  |  0.038  0.271  0.569  0.890  |     0.508      |  0.0919
      9     6  |  0.041  0.278  0.572  0.888  |     0.509      |  0.0920
      8     7  |  0.039  0.276  0.565  0.876  |     0.510      |  0.1106
      7     8  |  0.038  0.257  0.533  0.847  |     0.508      |  0.0902

   The sum is unconcentrated (top 10% of units hold ~1/3 of the magnitude) and incoherent (signs at
   chance; the total is ~10% of the sum of magnitudes).  Dropping a fraction f of terms therefore injects
   an error of order sqrt(f*n) typical terms against an answer of order 0.1*n typical terms: ~22% per
   source layer at f = 1/2, n = 1024.  This is a conditioning obstruction, not a resolution one, and it
   explains the signature both truncations share -- an immediate bias, then flat in the truncation
   parameter.

7. Operator-system programme, level 1: is the propagated state positive?
   Network 0, K=3.  lam_min / trace of the propagated post-activation covariance.

   layer   full state    Hermite part + exact diag   Hermite part alone   max|rho| offdiag   #neg eigs
     4     -1.852e-05    +1.072e-08                  +1.269e-08           0.271              1
     8     -1.108e-04    +1.508e-10                  +7.124e-12           0.373              1
    12     -1.011e-04    +2.581e-14                  -4.090e-19           0.513              1
    16     -1.001e-04    -4.892e-06                  -8.623e-06           0.597              2

   The Hermite covariance closure is Schur-positive by construction (a sum of Hadamard powers of a PSD
   correlation matrix against rank-one PSD factors), so the positivity violation is entirely due to the
   two-point cumulant correction: it produces exactly one negative eigenvalue at ~1e-4 of the trace.

   Projecting the state back into the positive cone (eigenvalue floor as a fraction of lam_max):

   floor      net 0       net 1       net 2       mean
   0 (none)   4.579e-07   5.224e-07   5.746e-07   5.183e-07
   1e-12      4.211e-07   5.200e-07   5.864e-07   5.092e-07
   1e-06      4.207e-07   5.201e-07   5.877e-07   5.095e-07
   1e-04      5.664e-07   7.329e-07   1.262e-06   8.538e-07

   Positivity is genuinely violated but is a weak constraint here: enforcing it buys about 2%.

8. Feshbach / leave-and-return: does a fitted low-parameter correction renormalize the omitted sector?
   Network 0, oracle inputs (true layer means, sampled covariances).
   tail = (exact sources to depth 8) - (exact sources to depth 3), i.e. the sector being integrated out.
   F    = the 2-parameter aligned correction (a*t + b)*sigma^3 fitted to the Monte-Carlo residual of the
          depth-3 model, which is what the estimator actually computes.

   layer  corr(F, tail)  |F|/|tail|  R2 of an oracle aligned model on tail  |tail|/|k3_depth8|
     5      +0.610          2.54       0.375                                 0.221
     8      +0.851          2.17       0.728                                 0.427
    12      +0.865          2.14       0.748                                 0.522
    16      +0.879          2.47       0.773                                 0.644

   The omitted sector is 64% of the full third cumulant by layer 16, and a two-parameter renormalization
   recovers ~80% of it.  This is the Feshbach picture: integrating out the omitted sources renormalizes
   the retained propagation, and the renormalization is low-dimensional even though the omitted sector
   is not.  It also explains why the exact path sum reverses beyond depth 8 (section 3): adding exact
   terms without re-fitting the renormalization double-counts.

   Relative sizes of the two levers, network 0, final-layer MSE:
     pure source model, depth 3, no fit          1.59e-06
     + Monte-Carlo fitted renormalization        3.78e-07   (4.2x)
     + source depth 3 -> 8                       1.61e-07   (2.3x further)

10. Is a renormalization coefficient on the second Price term just the dropped Hermite tail?
    Exact ReLU covariance is C_ij = s_i s_j sum_k rho^k d_k(t_i) d_k(t_j) / k!.  A two-term closure
    drops k >= 3.  The alpha it would need on the k=2 term to match exact (network 0):

    layer   rms|rho|   max|rho|   implied alpha (weighted)
      4     0.0563     0.271      1.002
      8     0.0813     0.373      1.004
     12     0.1035     0.513      1.006
     16     0.1288     0.600      1.007

    At most 1.007.  So an empirical alpha of 1.75-2.5 is NOT the omitted Hermite tail; it is absorbing
    the non-Gaussian omitted sector.  Consistent with section 8, where the fitted correction's magnitude
    ratio to the omitted third-cumulant sector is |F|/|tail| = 2.2-2.5.

11. Model reduction of the omitted sector: what rank is the memory kernel?
    Per-source contributions to kappa_3(z_16), network 0.

    Pairwise correlations between per-source contribution vectors run 0.22-0.59, and the singular
    spectrum of the 12-source stack needs 8 components for 95% of its energy.  The source channels are
    genuinely distinct; they do NOT collapse.

    But their SUM does.  R^2 of the tail (sources of age >= 4, which is 72% of the total by norm):

      basis                              rank   R^2
      {sigma^3}                          1      0.000
      {sigma^3, t sigma^3}               2      0.869
      {sigma^3, t sigma^3, t^2 sigma^3}  3      0.869
      age-1 contribution direction       1      0.151
      age-1,2,3 directions               3      0.477

    So the reduced kernel B(I-D)^{-1}C has rank ~2 in the analytically known basis {sigma^3, t sigma^3},
    while the omitted sector O itself has rank >= 8.  Fitting the coefficients per network shows the
    sigma^3 coefficient is consistent with zero, so the deployable kernel is rank ONE: a_l * t * sigma^3.

17. OQCP explicit memory channel: carry the long-lived cubic sector as R symmetric rank-one modes,
    T ~ sum_r lambda_r v_r^(x)3, readout k3_mem,j = sum_r lambda_r (W^T v_r)_j^3, transport shared
    with the readout (U = W^T V for the readout, V <- Phi * U for the transport).
    Held-out networks 8-15; the memory replaces the shipped rank-1 kernel.

    configuration                                  mean final MSE
    deployed: age-1 exact + shipped rank-1 kernel   1.167e-06
    memory R=64,   B=256    (diagonal modes)        2.101e-06
    memory R=128,  B=256                            2.096e-06
    memory R=256,  B=512                            2.080e-06
    memory R=512,  B=1024                           2.063e-06
    polarized memory R=128,  B=1024                 2.117e-06
    polarized memory R=256,  B=2048                 2.106e-06
    polarized memory R=512,  B=4096                 2.073e-06
    polarized memory R=1024, B=4096                 2.018e-06

    The first block carries only the diagonal diagram, which is the only one natively of the form
    lambda*v^(x)3.  That diagram is a small share of the source:

    source age    ||tree||    ||two-point||   ||diag||   diag share
       1          2.968e-02   2.058e-02       4.383e-03    0.114
       4          2.356e-02   1.971e-02       4.384e-03    0.124
       8          1.368e-02   1.217e-02       4.412e-03    0.195

    The second block polarizes the tree and two-point diagrams into symmetric rank-one modes
    (3 sym(e,b,b) = [(e+b)^3 + (e-b)^3]/2 - e^3, and similarly for sym(e,e,b)), so all three diagrams
    are represented.  It is still worse than the free rank-one kernel, and still nearly flat in R:
    an eightfold rank increase buys 5%.

    This is the fourth compression of this object to fail with the same signature (after spectral
    truncation of the transport, unit-space corner restriction, and the diagonal-mode memory).  The
    distinction that survives all four: approximating the SUM works, approximating the TERMS does not.
    The rank-one kernel is fitted against the aggregate and wins at zero cost; mode selection keeps R
    terms exactly and discards the rest, which the cancellation (coherence ~0.09) punishes.

20. ARC's power-cumulant closed form for the two-point channel, verified and priced.
    The literature study's completeness critic identified arXiv:2605.05179 section 4.1 footnote 17 as
    giving our dominant channel in closed form, and argued it would dissolve the "deliver the deep
    two-point channel under 37% of budget" problem at 0.006% of budget.  Both halves checked.

    The identity is elementary and correct.  With A = phi(Z_a), B = phi(Z_b), a = E[A]:
      kappa(A, A, B) = E[(A-a)^2 (B-b)] = Cov(A^2, B) - 2 a Cov(A, B)
    and for Gaussian Z both covariances are Hadamard power series in the correlation,
      Cov(f(Z_a), g(Z_b)) = sum_{k>=1} rho^k bf_k(t_a) bg_k(t_b) / k!
    with bf the Hermite coefficients of f.  Verified against 4e6-sample Monte Carlo on five bivariate
    Gaussians, all three quantities agreeing to about 0.3%, which is the sampling error:

      t_a    t_b   rho  | Cov(A,B) series/MC   | Cov(A^2,B) series/MC | kappa(A,A,B) series/MC
      +0.4  -0.3  0.30  | +0.08141 / +0.08134  | +0.16745 / +0.16703  | +0.064802 / +0.064518
      +1.0  +0.5  0.50  | +0.30248 / +0.30305  | +0.82170 / +0.82348  | +0.166346 / +0.167022
      +1.5  -1.0  0.45  | +0.06908 / +0.06883  | +0.26502 / +0.26374  | +0.053735 / +0.053268

    But it buys nothing.  Our source construction is the leading-order diagram truncation, one power of
    rho per edge; the closed form is the full series.  Compared on real layers of network 0 against a
    6e5-sample truth, with both given the true mean and covariance of the pre-activations:

    layer   ||K21_true||   leading-order diagrams   power-cumulant closed form   corr with truth
      4        7.6121        rel err 0.2641           rel err 0.2648             0.9705 / 0.9701
      8        3.4423        rel err 0.4639           rel err 0.4643             0.8981 / 0.8976
     12        2.2422        rel err 0.6869           rel err 0.6873             0.7476 / 0.7472

    The two agree to three decimal places, because the correlations are small (rms |rho| 0.04 to 0.13,
    section 10) so the k=1 term already dominates the series.  Our leading-order truncation was never
    the lossy step.

    What the 26 to 69% residual actually is: both constructions assume the pre-activation is Gaussian,
    and by layer 12 that assumption alone costs 69%.  The missing quantity is the INHERITED
    non-Gaussianity of z, which is exactly what the transported source channel and the Monte Carlo
    channel supply, and exactly why source depth pays 3.1x (section 2).  So the deep two-point channel
    is still needed and the closed form does not deliver it cheaply.

    Still open from the same critique, and untested: a fully factorized K=3 in ARC's sense costs
    L^2 n^3 = 2.75e11 = 12.5% of 2^41, which sits at the leader's 14.7% operating point.  The reference
    implementation is real, at github.com/alignment-research-center/mlp_cumulant_propagation (PyTorch;
    the critic's URL, .../mlp_kprop, is wrong -- that is the package path inside the repo).

22. The Gaussian mixed-jet identity and its homogeneity corollary: confirmed, then priced.

    Claim under test.  For f positively homogeneous of degree p (every coordinate of a bias-free ReLU
    suffix has p = 1), scaling M(cm, c^2 Sigma) = c^p M(m,Sigma) and differentiating twice gives
        D_m[v] D_Sigma[Sigma] M  =  ((p-1)/2) D_m[v] M  -  (1/2) D^2_m[v,m] M
    so at p = 1 the covariance-matched trace-cubic response equals a rank-<=2 covariance tangent,
        delta_{R(v,Sigma)} M  =  D_Sigma[ -(1/2)(v m^T + m v^T) ] M,
    and vanishes identically at a centered reference m = 0.

    (a) The identity holds through a real eight-layer ReLU suffix.  Central finite differences on
    2e5 Monte-Carlo samples with common random numbers, float64, suffix h_8 -> h_16 of network 0,
    ridged reference covariance, s = t = 0.05:

      D_m[v]D_Sig[Sig]M  vs  -(1/2) D^2_m[v,m]M          rel diff 0.0248   corr 0.99970
      D_m[v]D_Sig[Sig]M  vs  D_Sig[-(v m^T + m v^T)/2]M  rel diff 0.1116   corr 0.99388

    Residuals are finite-difference truncation and Monte-Carlo noise; the second comparison is looser
    because a rank-two covariance step at t = 0.05 is a coarser difference.  The nonsmoothness of ReLU
    does not obstruct it, as expected: the object differentiated is the Gaussian-smoothed expectation,
    not a pointwise path.

    The centered corollary is confirmed sharply.  At m = 0 the mixed derivative is 9.62e-05 against a
    scale ||M(0,Sigma)|| = 13.03, a ratio of 7.4e-06, versus 6.3e-03 at m != 0.  A suppression of about
    850x, at the finite-difference floor.

    (b) But the sector it reduces is nearly empty here.  In whitened coordinates w = Sigma^{-1/2}(h-mu),
    the metric trace is b_i = E[w_i ||w||^2] and ||kappa_3||^2 = E[(w.w')^3] for independent pairs, so
    the trace sector's share of the cumulant energy is 3||b||^2 / ((r+2) ||kappa_3||^2).  Network 0,
    6e4 samples, r = effective rank of Sigma:

      layer:      2      4      6      8     10     12     14     15
      eff rank: 1024   1023   1007    984    975    920    879    881
      share:  0.0124 0.0020 0.0028 0.0044 0.0063 0.0149 0.0057 0.0083

    So 0.2% to 1.5% of the third cumulant's energy, i.e. essentially all of kappa_3 is trace-free.
    For scale: an isotropic random symmetric 3-tensor would put 6/((n+1)(n+2)) = 5.7e-06 in that
    sector, so ours is enriched by roughly 350x to 2600x over isotropy -- there IS real trace
    structure -- and it is still about one percent of the object.

    Verdict.  The mathematics is correct and now verified in our setting, including through the
    nonsmooth activation.  It reduces a genuine sector to a rank-two covariance tangent for free.  It
    is not the unlock, because that sector carries about one percent of the cumulant energy.  The
    remaining 98.5% to 99.8% is the trace-free harmonic sector, which is the same off-diagonal, joint
    object that dominates the error (section 2) and that all four compressions failed on (section 1).
    The identity is most useful as a place NOT to spend effort.

23. Closure-induced return from a provably null sector.  A confirmed defect in our estimator.

    The prediction.  Write the state in radial coordinates X = R*Theta.  For degree-p homogeneous
    observables only the radius-weighted angular measure eta_p(A) = E[R^p 1{Theta in A}] matters, and it
    composes exactly under homogeneous maps: eta_p^+ = (tau_F)_*(a_F^p eta_p).  At a CENTERED Gaussian
    reference the covariance-matched trace-cubic perturbation has Hermite score
        (1/6) R(w,I):H_3(y) = (1/2)(w.y)(||y||^2 - (n+2)),
    whose contribution to eta_1 carries the radial factor E[R^2(R^2-(n+2))] = 0 for R ~ chi_n.  So that
    sector is EXACTLY invisible to every degree-one homogeneous observable, hence to every bias-free
    ReLU suffix mean.  But it is visible to intermediate SECOND moments (degree two), where the
    equivalent mean tangent is v/2 rather than 0.  A closure that keeps the second-moment change and
    drops the compensating structure can therefore manufacture a mean response out of nothing.

    The test.  Network 0, synthetic centered reference at layer 8 with the real ridged Sigma_8, suffix
    to layer 16, one fixed random unit direction v.  kappa_3(z) = R(W^T v, W^T Sigma W) transports
    covariantly, giving diagonal 3*vt_a*sz2_a and two-point 2*vt_a*Sz_ab + vt_b*sz2_a.

      truth,   mixed finite differences, common random numbers, 2e5 samples:
        s=t=0.05 : ||delta_R M|| = 9.2374e-05    relative to ||M|| = 7.09e-06
        s=t=0.10 : ||delta_R M|| = 1.3397e-04                        1.03e-05
        s=t=0.20 : ||delta_R M|| = 1.9204e-04                        1.47e-05
      (growing with the step, so this is finite-difference truncation, not signal)

      our closure, same injection:
        eps=1e-3 : ||response||/eps = 8.5086e-02  relative to ||M|| = 6.53e-03
        eps=3e-3 :                    8.5085e-02
        eps=1e-2 :                    8.5083e-02
      (identical to four significant figures across a decade, so cleanly first order)

    The closure's response is 921x the true one, in a sector where the truth is provably zero.

    Sizing.  Closure response per unit injection norm, trace sector against a norm-matched generic
    (non-trace) k3/K21 pair, three draws:
        trace sector R(v,Sigma)        8.5086e-02
        norm-matched generic           1.0359e-01, 9.8754e-02, 1.0148e-01   mean 1.0127e-01
        ratio                          0.840
    So the closure treats the null sector as 84% as consequential as a legitimate one.  Combined with
    section 22's measurement that the trace sector carries 0.2% to 1.5% of the cumulant energy, i.e.
    4.5% to 12% of its amplitude, roughly 4% to 10% of our cubic channel's response is spurious.

    The fix, not yet implemented.  Extract the covariance-metric trace of the generated cubic source,
        b_i = sum_jk (Sigma^-1)_jk kappa_3(z)_ijk,   w = b/(n+2),
    and replace its cubic treatment by the exact tangent it is equivalent to: zero at a centered
    reference, and D_Sigma[-(w m^T + m w^T)/2] at our actual non-centered operating point.  Reserve the
    cubic machinery for the trace-free remainder.  Note for a structured source K = R(v,G) the trace is
    b = tr(Sigma^-1 G) v + 2 G Sigma^-1 v, so it is NOT simply a rescaled v: the second term is nonzero
    even when G is Sigma-traceless.  Cost is one Sigma^-1 contraction per layer, n^3/3 = 0.016% of
    budget, but Sigma_z inherits the rank deficiency of Sigma_h (effective rank 879 to 1024, section 22)
    so it needs a ridge and the numerics want care.

    Also worth keeping: the observable-degree label must travel with the response.  The equivalent mean
    tangent of R(v,Sigma) is (p-1)v/2, so 0 for the final activation mean, v/2 for a raw second moment,
    v for a raw third.  No single mean-covariance perturbation reproduces all pairings at once, which is
    why this reduction cannot be installed as a physical covariance update.

24. The three-way identity audit, and why the mathematically correct fix is not implementable here.

    Defect A, homogeneity.  E_p = D_m[m]Mhat + 2 D_Sigma[Sigma]Mhat - p Mhat for p = 1, equivalently
    whether the closure is exactly scale-equivariant under (m, Sigma) -> (c m, c^2 Sigma).

      c        relative deviation of ||Mhat(c m, c^2 Sigma)|| from c ||Mhat(m, Sigma)||
      0.50     +0.000e+00      (max elementwise 0.000e+00)
      0.80     +2.220e-16      (max elementwise 1.126e-13)
      1.25     -2.220e-16      (max elementwise 1.052e-13)
      2.00     +0.000e+00
      4.00     +0.000e+00

    Exactly zero to machine precision.  Every ingredient is degree-correct by construction: t = mu/sz is
    scale-invariant, the Hermite coefficients depend only on t, the source coefficients carry
    p ~ c^-1, q ~ c, k3h ~ c^3, and the shipped kernels a_l*t*sigma^3 and c_l*sigma^3 both scale as c^3.
    So the closure satisfies the homogeneity relation identically, and section 23's nonzero centered
    response localizes cleanly to defect C: the separately implemented cubic channel and the derivative
    of the Gaussian-reference calculation implement incompatible versions of the same relation.

    The fix, implemented and measured.  Extract b_a = sum_jk (Sz^+)_jk kappa_3(z)_ajk from the
    factorised source (b = U^T c1 + Nt^T c2 with c1 = p*alpha + 2q*beta + k3h*gamma,
    c2 = 2p*beta + q*gamma, alpha = rowsum(Nt P . Nt), beta = rowsum(U P . Nt), gamma = rowsum(U P . U)),
    set w = b/(r+2), subtract R(w,Sz) from the cubic channels and add dSz = -(w mu^T + mu w^T)/2 to Sz.

    It fails, and two measurements say why.  Network 8, trace computed but NOT applied, so these are the
    unperturbed quantities:

      layer   cond(Sz)    ||w||     ||dSz|| / ||Sz||     (pseudo-inverse, threshold 1e-8)
        2     6.69e+07   6.33e-02      2.34e-02
        8     1.02e+12   3.37e-01      4.00e-01
       12     9.57e+16   7.38e-01      1.20e+00
       16     5.55e+16   9.17e-01      1.75e+00

    First, the prescribed tangent is not a tangent.  By layer 12 the perturbation dSz that the identity
    says is equivalent to the trace-cubic source is LARGER IN NORM THAN Sz ITSELF, reaching 1.75x by
    layer 16.  The identity is a first-order statement and our trace sector is not a small perturbation,
    so the linearisation is invalid at the amplitude we actually have.

    Second, Sigma^-1 does not exist numerically.  cond(Sz) reaches 5.6e16 by layer 16, at float64's
    limit.  This is not a closure artifact: the true post-activation covariance is genuinely
    rank-deficient (dead neurons), measured effective rank 879 to 1024 in section 22.  Any ridge large
    enough to stabilise the inverse changes the metric, and then the extracted vector is not the
    Sigma-metric trace, so the rank-two identity no longer applies to it.

    Held-out networks 8-15, final-layer MSE.  Every variant is worse than deployed:

      deployed, no trace fix                 8.827e-07
      trace fix, ridge 0.1 x mean diag       1.202e-06     (36% worse)
      trace fix, ridge 0.01 x mean diag      5.596e-03     (catastrophic)
      trace fix at half strength, ridge 0.1  8.870e-07     (0.5% worse)
      trace fix, pseudo-inverse              diverges to NaN

    Verdict.  The defect of section 23 is real and confirmed; the normal-form fix for it is correct
    mathematics that our pipeline cannot carry, because the object it needs (Sigma^-1 against a
    numerically singular Sigma) does not exist here and the perturbation it prescribes is not small.
    No change to the estimator; it stays at 8.3524e-07 raw on the full bank.

    What this selects.  The failure is specifically a failure of the INFINITESIMAL branch: a first-order
    tangent identity applied at an amplitude where first order does not hold.  The finite positive
    extension is the other branch, and it preserves the observable quotient EXACTLY at finite amplitude
    rather than to first order -- the radial construction with E[R h(R)] = 0 leaves eta_1 unchanged for
    the whole admissible interval of epsilon, not just its derivative at zero.  So this measurement is
    evidence for choosing the exact-quotient-preserving completion over the jet, which was posed as an
    open design choice rather than settled.

25. Correction to the literature study, from reading two of the papers directly.

    The study returned 49 of 49 "not relevant" at the reading stage, with zero changed by the
    adversarial stage.  Forty-nine independent agents across eight subfields, unanimous, with no
    movement under a stage built to argue the opposite, is a prior rather than a measurement, and the
    prompt installed it: readers were told "not relevant is the expected answer", handed six measured
    obstructions framed as hurdles a method must clear, warned about a fabricated citation, and given
    an actionability bar of "implementable this week".  Nothing pushed the other way.

    "0 actionable" stands.  "0 conceptually useful" does not.  Read directly, two papers carry the same
    distinction, and it is one we collapse.

    (a) Connes and van Suijlekom, arXiv:2004.14115, sections 4.1 and 4.2.  Two truncations of the same
    circle, dual to each other:
        Toeplitz  C(S^1)^(n) = P_n C(S^1) P_n     truncation on the STATE side       prop = 2
        Fejer-Riesz  C*(Z)^(n), finite Fourier support   truncation on the OBSERVABLE side  prop = infinity
    prop(E) is the least number of products of retained elements needed to generate the C*-envelope.
    On the state side two products already give all of M_n(C), the "non-informative full matrix
    algebra", INDEPENDENTLY OF n.  On the observable side no finite number ever does.  Both converge to
    the circle; one keeps the S^1 symmetry and a rich pure state space, the other (circulant
    discretisation) reduces it to a discrete group.  Their section 6: convergence in Gromov-Hausdorff
    distance does not imply retention of the structure the remaining dynamics acts through.

    Every truncation we have implemented is on the state side: the covariance, the cumulant tensor, the
    transport, the unit space, the memory modes.  And we do multiply retained objects, repeatedly --
    the covariance closure is a Hermite power series in the retained correlation to order 8, plus
    m2 - Psi^2, plus source terms quadratic in the transported quantities.  Degeneration after two
    products, flat in the truncation parameter, is obstruction 1's measured signature exactly.

    (b) Giannakis and Montgomery, arXiv:2603.20102, sections 4.2 and 4.3.  The same defect from the
    dynamics side, with a name and a repair.  Finite-dimensional approximations of a generator are
    dynamically consistent yet "generally fail to satisfy a Leibniz rule": products of approximate
    eigenfunctions are not approximate eigenfunctions.  The repair is Fock amplification -- lift the
    generator to the tensor algebra by the derivation rule
        W~(f_1 (x) ... (x) f_n) = (W f_1) (x) ... + ... + f_1 (x) ... (x) (W f_n)
    so that Leibniz holds by construction and exp(t W~) acts multiplicatively on tensor products.

    This names our confirmed bug.  Section 23's closure-induced return is a Leibniz failure: degree two
    is the product of two degree-one observables, and our closure treats them with separately fitted
    approximations that nothing forces to satisfy the product rule relating them.  The graded law
    T^(p) h . T^(q) g = T^(p+q)(hg) is precisely the multiplicativity a truncation must respect.

    The design consequence, untested.  Our estimator fits three objects at three degrees independently:
    a_l t sigma^3 for the third cumulant, a scalar g4 for the fourth, c_l sigma^3 for the two-point.
    Nothing makes them the derivation-extension of a single degree-one map.  A Leibniz-coherent closure
    would induce the higher-degree corrections from the degree-one one rather than fitting them
    separately.  That is a different architecture from anything tried here, and it is the first
    candidate this literature has produced that addresses a defect we actually measured.
