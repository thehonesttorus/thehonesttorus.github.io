1b. Why: the path sum cancels.  Per-source-unit terms of kappa_3(z_16), network 0.
   'share' = cumulative share of sum|term| held by the top q% of units, ranked by |term| mass.
   'sign agreement' = fraction of (unit, output) terms whose sign matches the total's (chance = 0.500).
   'coherence' = ||sum of terms|| / sum||term||, averaged over outputs.

   source  age |  top 1%   10%    25%    50%  | sign agreement | coherence
     14     1  |  0.048  0.338  0.690  0.961  |     0.506      |  0.0887
     13     2  |  0.049  0.344  0.701  0.960  |     0.506      |  0.1000
     12     3  |  0.043  0.312  0.648  0.944  |     0.508      |  0.0961
     11     4  |  0.041  0.302  0.617  0.924  |     0.508      |  0.0950
     10     5  |  0.038  0.271  0.569  0.890  |     0.508      |  0.0919
      9     6  |  0.041  0.278  0.572  0.888  |     0.509      |  0.0920
      8     7  |  0.039  0.276  0.565  0.876  |     0.510      |  0.1106
      7     8  |  0.038  0.257  0.533  0.847  |     0.508      |  0.0902

   The sum is unconcentrated (top 10% of units hold ~1/3 of the magnitude) and incoherent (signs at
   chance; the total is ~10% of the sum of magnitudes).  Dropping a fraction f of terms therefore injects
   an error of order sqrt(f*n) typical terms against an answer of order 0.1*n typical terms: ~22% per
   source layer at f = 1/2, n = 1024.  This is a conditioning obstruction, not a resolution one, and it
   explains the signature both truncations share -- an immediate bias, then flat in the truncation
   parameter.

7. Operator-system programme, level 1: is the propagated state positive?
   Network 0, K=3.  lam_min / trace of the propagated post-activation covariance.

   layer   full state    Hermite part + exact diag   Hermite part alone   max|rho| offdiag   #neg eigs
     4     -1.852e-05    +1.072e-08                  +1.269e-08           0.271              1
     8     -1.108e-04    +1.508e-10                  +7.124e-12           0.373              1
    12     -1.011e-04    +2.581e-14                  -4.090e-19           0.513              1
    16     -1.001e-04    -4.892e-06                  -8.623e-06           0.597              2

   The Hermite covariance closure is Schur-positive by construction (a sum of Hadamard powers of a PSD
   correlation matrix against rank-one PSD factors), so the positivity violation is entirely due to the
   two-point cumulant correction: it produces exactly one negative eigenvalue at ~1e-4 of the trace.

   Projecting the state back into the positive cone (eigenvalue floor as a fraction of lam_max):

   floor      net 0       net 1       net 2       mean
   0 (none)   4.579e-07   5.224e-07   5.746e-07   5.183e-07
   1e-12      4.211e-07   5.200e-07   5.864e-07   5.092e-07
   1e-06      4.207e-07   5.201e-07   5.877e-07   5.095e-07
   1e-04      5.664e-07   7.329e-07   1.262e-06   8.538e-07

   Positivity is genuinely violated but is a weak constraint here: enforcing it buys about 2%.

8. Feshbach / leave-and-return: does a fitted low-parameter correction renormalize the omitted sector?
   Network 0, oracle inputs (true layer means, sampled covariances).
   tail = (exact sources to depth 8) - (exact sources to depth 3), i.e. the sector being integrated out.
   F    = the 2-parameter aligned correction (a*t + b)*sigma^3 fitted to the Monte-Carlo residual of the
          depth-3 model, which is what the estimator actually computes.

   layer  corr(F, tail)  |F|/|tail|  R2 of an oracle aligned model on tail  |tail|/|k3_depth8|
     5      +0.610          2.54       0.375                                 0.221
     8      +0.851          2.17       0.728                                 0.427
    12      +0.865          2.14       0.748                                 0.522
    16      +0.879          2.47       0.773                                 0.644

   The omitted sector is 64% of the full third cumulant by layer 16, and a two-parameter renormalization
   recovers ~80% of it.  This is the Feshbach picture: integrating out the omitted sources renormalizes
   the retained propagation, and the renormalization is low-dimensional even though the omitted sector
   is not.  It also explains why the exact path sum reverses beyond depth 8 (section 3): adding exact
   terms without re-fitting the renormalization double-counts.

   Relative sizes of the two levers, network 0, final-layer MSE:
     pure source model, depth 3, no fit          1.59e-06
     + Monte-Carlo fitted renormalization        3.78e-07   (4.2x)
     + source depth 3 -> 8                       1.61e-07   (2.3x further)

   The marginal operator system S_4 determines the target to 1.9e-09, below the leaderboard frontier

   perfect marginals at every layer is 7.07e-07, so the ambiguity that matters lives in the joint.

10. Is a renormalization coefficient on the second Price term just the dropped Hermite tail?
    Exact ReLU covariance is C_ij = s_i s_j sum_k rho^k d_k(t_i) d_k(t_j) / k!.  A two-term closure
    drops k >= 3.  The alpha it would need on the k=2 term to match exact (network 0):

    layer   rms|rho|   max|rho|   implied alpha (weighted)
      4     0.0563     0.271      1.002
      8     0.0813     0.373      1.004
     12     0.1035     0.513      1.006
     16     0.1288     0.600      1.007

    At most 1.007.  So an empirical alpha of 1.75-2.5 is NOT the omitted Hermite tail; it is absorbing
    the non-Gaussian omitted sector.  Consistent with section 8, where the fitted correction's magnitude
    ratio to the omitted third-cumulant sector is |F|/|tail| = 2.2-2.5.

11. Model reduction of the omitted sector: what rank is the memory kernel?
    Per-source contributions to kappa_3(z_16), network 0.

    Pairwise correlations between per-source contribution vectors run 0.22-0.59, and the singular
    spectrum of the 12-source stack needs 8 components for 95% of its energy.  The source channels are
    genuinely distinct; they do NOT collapse.

    But their SUM does.  R^2 of the tail (sources of age >= 4, which is 72% of the total by norm):

      basis                              rank   R^2
      {sigma^3}                          1      0.000
      {sigma^3, t sigma^3}               2      0.869
      {sigma^3, t sigma^3, t^2 sigma^3}  3      0.869
      age-1 contribution direction       1      0.151
      age-1,2,3 directions               3      0.477

    So the reduced kernel B(I-D)^{-1}C has rank ~2 in the analytically known basis {sigma^3, t sigma^3},
    while the omitted sector O itself has rank >= 8.  Fitting the coefficients per network shows the
    sigma^3 coefficient is consistent with zero, so the deployable kernel is rank ONE: a_l * t * sigma^3.

12. Is the kernel coefficient universal?  Fitted per network on a 2400-row sample, source depth 1.

    layer     a = coef of t*sigma^3            g4 = mean excess kurtosis
              mean       sd     sd/|mean|      mean      sd     sd/|mean|
      6      +0.01752  0.00105    0.060       +0.02607  0.00182   0.070
     11      +0.02675  0.00243    0.091       +0.04884  0.00708   0.145
     16      +0.03266  0.00314    0.096       +0.06464  0.01008   0.156

    Noise floor: the same network with three Monte-Carlo seeds gives a = 0.0397, 0.0307, 0.0371 at
    layer 16, a spread larger than the across-network sd of 0.00314.  The across-network variation is
    therefore entirely sampling noise: the coefficient is a universal function of depth.

17. OQCP explicit memory channel: carry the long-lived cubic sector as R symmetric rank-one modes,
    T ~ sum_r lambda_r v_r^(x)3, readout k3_mem,j = sum_r lambda_r (W^T v_r)_j^3, transport shared
    with the readout (U = W^T V for the readout, V <- Phi * U for the transport).
    Held-out networks 8-15; the memory replaces the shipped rank-1 kernel.

    configuration                                  mean final MSE
    deployed: age-1 exact + shipped rank-1 kernel   1.167e-06
    memory R=64,   B=256    (diagonal modes)        2.101e-06
    memory R=128,  B=256                            2.096e-06
    memory R=256,  B=512                            2.080e-06
    memory R=512,  B=1024                           2.063e-06
    polarized memory R=128,  B=1024                 2.117e-06
    polarized memory R=256,  B=2048                 2.106e-06
    polarized memory R=512,  B=4096                 2.073e-06
    polarized memory R=1024, B=4096                 2.018e-06

    The first block carries only the diagonal diagram, which is the only one natively of the form
    lambda*v^(x)3.  That diagram is a small share of the source:

    source age    ||tree||    ||two-point||   ||diag||   diag share
       1          2.968e-02   2.058e-02       4.383e-03    0.114
       4          2.356e-02   1.971e-02       4.384e-03    0.124
       8          1.368e-02   1.217e-02       4.412e-03    0.195

    The second block polarizes the tree and two-point diagrams into symmetric rank-one modes
    (3 sym(e,b,b) = [(e+b)^3 + (e-b)^3]/2 - e^3, and similarly for sym(e,e,b)), so all three diagrams
    are represented.  It is still worse than the free rank-one kernel, and still nearly flat in R:
    an eightfold rank increase buys 5%.

    This is the fourth compression of this object to fail with the same signature (after spectral
    truncation of the transport, unit-space corner restriction, and the diagonal-mode memory).  The
    distinction that survives all four: approximating the SUM works, approximating the TERMS does not.
    The rank-one kernel is fitted against the aggregate and wins at zero cost; mode selection keeps R
    terms exactly and discards the rest, which the cancellation (coherence ~0.09) punishes.
